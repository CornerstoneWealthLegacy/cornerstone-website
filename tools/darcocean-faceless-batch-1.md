## PART 1 — "DID YOU KNOW — FLORIDA EDITION" (F-4) ×10
~15–25s each. Hook → fact → statute on screen → soft CTA.
================================================================

### DYK-1 — Dying without a will
**On-screen:** "No will? Florida writes one for you."
**VO:** "Did you know — in Florida, if you pass away without a will, state law decides who inherits your
property, not you. It's called intestate succession, and the order is set by statute — spouse, children,
and on down the line. The only way your wishes control is if you put them in writing."
**Statute card:** Fla. Stat. §732.101–§732.103
**B-roll:** quiet FL home exterior → empty desk with a single document. **CTA:** "Don't let the state
decide. Start your plan." → /quiz

### DYK-2 — A will still goes through probate
**On-screen:** "A will does NOT skip probate."
**VO:** "Here's one that surprises people: in Florida, having a will does not avoid probate. A will is
your instructions *to* the probate court — it still has to go through it. What actually keeps your family
out of probate is a properly funded revocable living trust."
**Statute card:** Fla. Stat. Ch. 733 (probate); Ch. 736 (trusts)
**B-roll:** courthouse steps → calm home interior. **CTA:** "Learn the difference — free quiz." → /quiz

### DYK-3 — Two witnesses required
**On-screen:** "A Florida will needs 2 witnesses."
**VO:** "Did you know a Florida will isn't valid unless you sign it in front of two witnesses, who also
sign in your presence? A handwritten note or a will from another state may not hold up. The formalities
matter — get them wrong and the whole document can fail."
**Statute card:** Fla. Stat. §732.502
**B-roll:** pen signing → two hands witnessing. **CTA:** "Built Florida-correct. See how." → builder

### DYK-4 — No state estate tax (but...)
**On-screen:** "Florida has no estate tax. There's still a catch."
**VO:** "Good news: Florida has no state estate tax and no inheritance tax. But a large estate can still
owe *federal* estate tax, and your family can still get stuck in probate. No state tax doesn't mean no
planning."
**Statute card:** Fla. Const. Art. VII; IRC §2001 (federal)
**B-roll:** Florida coastline → home → documents. **CTA:** "See where your plan stands." → /quiz

### DYK-5 — Homestead can't always be left in a will
**On-screen:** "You can't always leave your home to whoever you want."
**VO:** "Did you know Florida's homestead law can override your will? If you're survived by a spouse or
minor child, the state limits how you can leave your homestead — even if your will says otherwise. It's
one of the most misunderstood rules in Florida estate planning."
**Statute card:** Fla. Const. Art. X §4; Fla. Stat. §732.4015
**B-roll:** family home, porch, palm shadows. **CTA:** "Get the homestead right." → /book

### DYK-6 — Surviving spouse elective share
**On-screen:** "A Florida spouse can't be fully disinherited."
**VO:** "Did you know you generally can't cut your spouse out of your estate in Florida? A surviving
spouse can claim an 'elective share' — thirty percent of the elective estate — no matter what your will
says. Planning around it takes intention."
**Statute card:** Fla. Stat. §732.201, §732.2065
**B-roll:** two wedding rings on a table → document. **CTA:** "Plan with intention." → /book

### DYK-7 — Power of attorney dies with you
**On-screen:** "Your POA stops the moment you pass."
**VO:** "A common misunderstanding: a power of attorney only works while you're alive. The moment you
pass away, it's void — your agent has no authority. After that, it's your will or trust that controls.
You need both kinds of documents, for two different jobs."
**Statute card:** Fla. Stat. Ch. 709
**B-roll:** clock → documents in a folder. **CTA:** "Cover both. Free quiz." → /quiz

### DYK-8 — Florida durable POA needs notary + 2 witnesses
**On-screen:** "A FL power of attorney has strict rules."
**VO:** "Did you know a Florida durable power of attorney must be signed with two witnesses *and* a
notary? And Florida no longer allows 'springing' powers that kick in later — it's effective when signed.
Miss a formality and banks can reject it."
**Statute card:** Fla. Stat. §709.2105
**B-roll:** notary stamp → signature. **CTA:** "Done the Florida way." → builder

### DYK-9 — Florida has no TOD deed (Lady Bird instead)
**On-screen:** "Florida doesn't have transfer-on-death deeds."
**VO:** "Did you know most states let you put a 'transfer on death' on your house deed — but Florida
doesn't? Floridians use a different tool, an enhanced life estate deed, often called a Lady Bird deed,
to pass a home outside probate while keeping full control during life."
**Statute card:** Florida common law (enhanced life estate / 'Lady Bird' deed)
**B-roll:** house key → deed document. **CTA:** "Ask about a Lady Bird deed." → /book

### DYK-10 — Small estates can skip full probate
**On-screen:** "Not every Florida estate needs full probate."
**VO:** "Did you know Florida has a shortcut? If an estate is under seventy-five thousand dollars, or the
person passed more than two years ago, it may qualify for 'summary administration' — a faster, simpler
process than full probate. Whether you qualify depends on the details."
**Statute card:** Fla. Stat. §735.201
**B-roll:** documents → calendar. **CTA:** "Not sure which applies? Let's talk." → /book

## PART 2 — MYTH vs FACT (F-5) ×5
~15–25s. Two-beat reveal: MYTH (red/muted) → FACT (gold). Keep it general info.
================================================================

### MVF-1
**MYTH (on-screen):** "A will keeps my family out of probate."
**FACT (on-screen):** "In Florida, a will goes *through* probate."
**VO:** "Myth: a will avoids probate. Fact: in Florida, a will is your instructions to the probate court —
it doesn't skip it. A funded revocable living trust is the tool that can keep your family out of probate."
**Statute card:** Ch. 733 / Ch. 736. **CTA:** "Know the difference — free quiz." → /quiz

### MVF-2
**MYTH:** "I'm young and healthy — I don't need a plan."
**FACT:** "A power of attorney protects you *while you're alive.*"
**VO:** "Myth: estate planning is only for the old. Fact: the documents that matter most if you're in an
accident — a power of attorney and a health care surrogate — protect you while you're living. Planning
isn't about age. It's about being ready."
**Statute card:** Fla. Stat. Ch. 709; §765.202. **CTA:** "Start in 3 minutes." → /quiz

### MVF-3
**MYTH:** "My spouse automatically gets everything."
**FACT:** "Florida law may split it with your children."
**VO:** "Myth: if I die, my spouse automatically inherits everything. Fact: in Florida, if you have
children from a prior relationship, state law can divide your estate between your spouse and your kids —
not the way most people expect. A plan is how you control it."
**Statute card:** Fla. Stat. §732.102. **CTA:** "Control where it goes." → /book

### MVF-4
**MYTH:** "My will controls my life insurance and 401(k)."
**FACT:** "Beneficiary designations override your will."
**VO:** "Myth: my will decides who gets everything. Fact: your life insurance, IRA, and 401(k) pass by
beneficiary designation — and that beats your will every time. An outdated beneficiary form can send
money to the wrong person, no matter what your will says."
**Statute card:** Beneficiary designations / nonprobate transfers. **CTA:** "Check your forms. Free quiz." → /quiz

### MVF-5
**MYTH:** "I'll just give my house to my kids now to avoid probate."
**FACT:** "That can trigger taxes and risk — there are better tools."
**VO:** "Myth: just add my kids to the deed and avoid probate. Fact: gifting your home during life can
create capital-gains and Medicaid problems for your family. Florida tools like a Lady Bird deed or a
trust often protect them far better. Talk to someone before you sign a deed."
**Statute card:** General information — consult an attorney. **CTA:** "Before you sign — let's talk." → /book
