Wills, Trusts & Powers of Attorney for Lakeland Families

A complete Florida estate plan usually rests on a few core documents working together:

  • A Florida will that names your personal representative and beneficiaries
  • A revocable living trust to avoid probate and plan for incapacity
  • A durable power of attorney and health care directives
  • Homestead planning to protect your Lakeland residence

Keeping Your Lakeland Estate Out of Probate

For most Lakeland homeowners, the goal is to pass the home and accounts to family without a Polk County court filing. A funded revocable living trust is usually the most direct route, supported by beneficiary designations, payable-on-death accounts, and the right deeds. Florida's constitutional homestead protection (Art. X, §4, Fla. Const.) adds another layer for your primary residence.

Lakeland Estate Planning FAQs

What estate planning documents do I need in Lakeland?

Most Lakeland adults need a will, a durable power of attorney, a health care surrogate designation, and a living will. Homeowners, parents, and anyone who wants to avoid probate should also consider a revocable living trust. The right combination depends on your assets and family under Florida law.

Do I need a living trust if I live in Lakeland?

A revocable living trust is often worthwhile for Lakeland homeowners because it keeps your home and accounts out of Polk County probate, stays private, and works smoothly if you become incapacitated. A trust only works if it is funded — assets must be retitled into it. For smaller or simpler estates, a will plus beneficiary designations may be enough.

How are wills signed in Florida?

A Florida will must be signed by the testator and attested by at least two witnesses, who sign in the testator's presence and in the presence of each other (Fla. Stat. §732.502). We make sure your Lakeland documents are executed correctly — improper signing is one of the most common reasons a will fails.

Can I do my Lakeland estate plan online or remotely?

Yes. You can complete a Florida-valid plan from home: answer a few questions in our secure intake, we prepare your documents under current Florida law (with an Attorney-Guided option reviewed by Arthur Simpson, Esq.), and we guide you through signing under Florida's witness and notary rules.

Does Florida have an estate or inheritance tax?

No. Florida has no state estate tax and no state inheritance tax. Only very large estates may owe federal estate tax above the federal exemption. Florida's homestead protection and the absence of a state death tax make careful titling and beneficiary planning especially valuable for Lakeland families.
