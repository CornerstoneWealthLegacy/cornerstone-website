The Standard We Were Raised On

There's a story every Florida lawyer should know. A judge in Jacksonville — a real one, from the 1960s — spent years receiving death threats in the mail. His offense? Ruling that Florida's schools, pools, and streets belonged to every Floridian who lived here. The Governor of Florida pushed back; the judge threatened him with contempt, and the governor stood down. They'd called that judge "Cowboy" since his ranch-hand days — and cowboys, as the story goes, don't spook. Today, a federal courthouse in Jacksonville carries his name: Simpson.

We're a fifth-generation Florida family. We grew up on stories like that one — stories where the measure of a lawyer isn't the marble in the lobby. It's a simpler question: when the pressure comes, do they hold?

"When it gets ugly — and it does get ugly — you want the lawyer who doesn't spook."
The Truestead standard
