_Companion to `BUSINESS-BROKERAGE.md`. How we evaluate every business that comes through the door, how we put a number on it, how we verify it for a buyer, and everything we need to build, buy, or sign up for to make that possible. Working draft, 2026-09-12._

## 0. Four evaluations, not one

A brokerage runs four distinct evaluations, and mixing them up is how brokers get sued. Each has a different question, a different depth, and a different deliverable.

| # | Evaluation | Question it answers | When | Deliverable | Who pays |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| E1 | **Listing screen** | Is this business sellable, and is it worth our time? | First call, before any engagement | Internal scorecard (green / yellow / red) + a 20-minute call | Nobody (free) |
| E2 | **Broker Opinion of Value (BOV)** | What will this business most likely sell for, to whom, and on what terms? | Before listing; for buy-sell agreements, estates, divorce, E-2 plans | 12–18 page written BOV with a price range and a recommended asking price | Seller (S3 flat fee, credited on listing) |
| E3 | **Due diligence** | Is the business what the seller says it is? | After LOI, before closing | Diligence report + issues list + closing conditions | Buyer (S2 or S5); sell-side version is the Exit-Readiness Audit (S4) |
| E4 | **Buyer qualification** | Can this buyer actually close, and are they allowed to? | Before any confidential information goes out | Buyer file: NDA, financial statement, proof of funds, SBA/E-2 eligibility | Nobody (gate) |

The rest of this file goes through each, then §5 covers what we have to put in place.

## 1. E1 — The listing screen

Purpose: say no early. Industry data is consistent that only a minority of listed Main Street businesses ever close; the ones that don't were usually unsellable on day one. The screen is a 25-minute call plus a document request.

### 1.1 Minimum documents to even screen
- Last 3 years of **business tax returns** (not just P&Ls)
- **YTD profit & loss and balance sheet**, and the same period last year
- Current **lease** (or deed if the property is owned)
- List of **licenses and permits** the business operates under
- One sentence on **why they're selling** and **when**

If a seller won't produce tax returns, stop. The BOV, the buyer, and the SBA lender all start from the returns.

### 1.2 The scorecard  **[Claude → template]**

Score 0–2 on each line. 0 = red, 1 = yellow, 2 = green.

| # | Factor | Green (2) | Yellow (1) | Red (0) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Seller's Discretionary Earnings (SDE) | ≥ $150K | $75K–$150K | < $75K (hard to finance, hard to sell) |
| 2 | Books | Tax returns reconcile to P&Ls; bank deposits tie out | Small gaps, explainable | Cash business with unreported income the seller wants credit for |
| 3 | Trend | Revenue and SDE flat or rising over 3 years | One down year, explained | Declining 2+ years |
| 4 | Owner dependence | Business runs with a manager; owner < 40 hrs/week | Owner is the key operator but processes are documented | The owner *is* the business (sole practitioner, personal relationships) |
| 5 | Customer concentration | No customer > 10% of revenue | Largest customer 10–25% | One customer > 25%, or a non-assignable contract |
| 6 | Lease / premises | ≥ 5 years remaining or options, assignable, market rent | 2–5 years, landlord cooperative | < 2 years, no options, or landlord hostile; or below-market rent that resets |
| 7 | Transferability of licenses | Licenses run with the business or transfer routinely | Transfer requires a qualifier or DBPR approval (contractor F.S. 489, alcohol) | License is personal to the owner and buyer can't get one |
| 8 | Legal standing | Entity active on Sunbiz, no litigation, no liens, taxes current | Minor issues curable before listing | Active litigation, unpaid DOR sales tax, unfiled returns, undisclosed liens |
| 9 | Staff | Key employees will stay; no misclassification | One key person at risk | Key person leaving; 1099 workers who are really employees |
| 10 | Seller expectation vs. BOV range | Within 10% | Within 25% | Seller wants 2× what it's worth and won't move |
| 11 | Motivation & timeline | Clear reason, 6–12 month horizon, will stay for transition | Vague | "Testing the market" or must close in 30 days |
| 12 | Industry outlook | Stable or growing in the local market | Cyclical | Structurally declining or regulation-threatened |

**Decision rule:** list at ≥ 17/24 with **no reds on lines 2, 8, or 10**. 12–16: send to the Exit-Readiness Audit (S4) with a plan to fix. Below 12: decline, offer S4 or a re-look in 12 months.

### 1.3 Things that automatically change the path
- Real property owned by the seller or a related entity → S6 combined evaluation; separate real-estate valuation (§2.7).
- Business in an estate, trust, or guardianship → S7; personal representative authority and any court approval requirement come first.
- Seller or buyer is a foreign national → S8; E-2 or EB-5 lens applied in E4.
- Seller is an existing Truestead Law client → Rule 4-1.8 consent before any brokerage engagement (main plan §3.3).

## 2. E2 — The Broker Opinion of Value

### 2.1 The legal line first
- **What we can call it.** "Broker Opinion of Value" or "Most Probable Selling Price." Never "appraisal," never "certified," never "USPAP." For any **real property** component, Florida licensees may prepare a BPO/CMA but may not call it an appraisal (F.S. 475.612 / 475.628); the same discipline applies to the business.
- **When a BOV is not enough.** The following require a credentialed business appraiser, not us:
  - **SBA-financed purchases** above the SOP threshold: under SBA SOP 50 10, when the amount financed less the appraised real estate/equipment exceeds $250,000, or when buyer and seller have a close relationship, the lender must obtain an independent valuation from a "qualified source" (credentials such as ASA, CBA, ABV, CVA). **[Arthur]** confirm the current SOP text each year.
  - **Estate tax, gift tax, charitable transfers** — qualified appraisal rules, Rev. Rul. 59-60 factors.
  - **Litigation, divorce, shareholder disputes, buy-sell agreement triggers** where the agreement calls for an "appraisal."
  - Our own estate and probate practice will generate these regularly → a standing referral relationship with a CVA/ABV (see §5.3).
- **Disclaimer block** on every BOV: not an appraisal; relies on seller-provided information not audited by us; prepared for pricing and marketing; not for lending, tax, or litigation; no guarantee of sale price.

### 2.2 Step 1 — Recast the earnings (the SDE schedule)
Everything starts with **Seller's Discretionary Earnings** for Main Street, or **Adjusted EBITDA** above roughly $2M in value. Build the schedule from tax returns, not from the seller's P&L.

**SDE = pre-tax net income**
+ one owner's salary and payroll taxes
+ owner's benefits and perks run through the business (auto, health, phone, travel, meals, family on payroll who don't work)
+ interest
+ depreciation and amortization
+ non-recurring expenses (lawsuit, one-time repair, moving cost) — documented
+ non-cash charges
+ personal expenses run through the business — *only if documented*
− any income that won't transfer (rental income from property not included, one-time gains, PPP/ERC)
− market-rate replacement cost for a second working owner or unpaid family
− normalized rent if the seller owns the building and pays themselves below market

Rules: every add-back has a document behind it; "cash I didn't report" gets **zero** credit; three years plus trailing twelve months, weighted toward the most recent.

For Adjusted EBITDA (lower middle market): same, but leave a **market salary for a manager** in the expenses, normalize working capital, and expect a CPA **quality-of-earnings** review.

### 2.3 Step 2 — Pick the approaches
Use at least two, reconcile to a range.

| Approach | Method | Use it for | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Market — multiple of SDE** | SDE × multiple | Every Main Street deal; primary method | Multiples from comparable closed transactions (§2.4); closed Main Street deals have recently clustered around **2–3× SDE** on BizBuySell Insight data, with the median a little below 2.5×; adjust with the §2.5 grid |
| **Market — multiple of revenue / industry rule of thumb** | Revenue × % | Sanity check; practices and agencies where revenue multiples are the trade convention | Never the primary method; rules of thumb are where bad prices come from |
| **Market — comparable transactions** | Direct comps from DealStats / BIZCOMPS / Peercomps by NAICS, size, region | Lower middle market and any unusual business | Requires data subscriptions (§5.1) |
| **Income — multiple of Adjusted EBITDA** | EBITDA × 3–6 | Deals above ~$2M | Buyers here are strategic, PE, or search funds; they price on EBITDA |
| **Income — capitalization of earnings / DCF** | Earnings ÷ cap rate; or discounted projected cash flows | Growth businesses, contracts with visible cash flow, E-2 business plans | Rarely for Main Street; document assumptions heavily |
| **Asset — adjusted net asset value** | FMV of assets − liabilities | Asset-heavy or under-earning businesses; the **floor** value | Equipment appraiser for anything material; inventory at cost |
| **Buyer's test — debt-service / justification of price** | Can SDE cover the buyer's salary + debt service + a cushion? | Every deal, always | If an SBA buyer can't hit ~1.15–1.25× debt-service coverage after a market salary, the price is wrong regardless of the multiple |

### 2.4 Where the multiples come from  **[Arthur to subscribe]**
- **BizBuySell Insight Reports** (free, quarterly) — Florida and national medians for sale price / SDE and sale price / revenue.
- **IBBA & M&A Source Market Pulse** (member) — quarterly, by deal size and sector.
- **DealStats (Business Valuation Resources)**, **BIZCOMPS**, **Peercomps** — transaction databases by NAICS with financial detail; the tools a BOV should cite.
- **Business Reference Guide** (annual, Tom West) — the rules-of-thumb book every BBF office keeps on the desk.
- **BBF's own closed-sale data** once we are members.

### 2.5 Step 3 — Adjust the multiple (the risk grid)
Start from the industry median multiple and move it by the factors below. Document each move.

| Factor | Moves the multiple down | Moves it up |
|---|---|---|
| Owner dependence | Owner-operated with personal relationships | Management in place, documented systems |
| Size of SDE | < $100K | > $300K (buyer pool and lender appetite widen) |
| Trend | Declining | 3-year growth |
| Customer concentration | > 20% in one customer | Diversified, recurring revenue |
| Lease | Short, non-assignable, above-market rent | Long, assignable, below-market rent (or real estate available) |
| Books | Cash-heavy, unreconciled | Clean returns, reviewed statements |
| Staff | Key-person risk, turnover | Tenured staff staying on |
| Licenses | Hard to transfer (qualifier needed) | Transferable or none needed |
| Assets | Deferred maintenance, old fleet | Recent CapEx, clean equipment |
| Financeability | Not SBA-eligible (cash business, seller won't carry paper) | SBA-eligible with seller note |
| Transition | Seller leaving immediately | 3–12 months of seller training included |

### 2.6 Step 4 — Deal structure affects price
The BOV states a **range** and a **structure**, because the same business is worth more with a seller note and less all-cash.
- Asset sale vs. stock sale (buyers pay more for asset sales because of the step-up and no inherited liabilities).
- Seller financing (typically 10–30% of price on Main Street; the SBA often requires a seller note on standby).
- Earn-outs for growth claims we can't underwrite.
- Inventory: at cost, in addition to the price, or included — say which.
- Working capital peg on larger deals.
- Non-compete term and geography (F.S. 542.335: ≤ 3 years presumed reasonable in a sale).
- Transition period and consulting agreement.

### 2.7 Florida-specific items that change value
- **Quota liquor licenses (4COP)** trade separately and can be worth six figures in some counties; value the license as its own line, and confirm transferability with DBPR's Division of Alcoholic Beverages & Tobacco.
- **Contractor businesses** need a licensed **qualifying agent** (F.S. 489); if the seller is the qualifier and won't stay, the buyer must bring one — price it in.
- **Sales tax exposure (F.S. 213.758)** — a DOR clearance is a closing condition; a business behind on sales tax is worth less than its SDE says.
- **Tangible personal property tax** filings and county business tax receipts must be current.
- **Real property**: value separately with a commercial BPO/CMA (cap-rate or comparable-sales approach) or an appraisal; then decide combined sale vs. sale-leaseback. A below-market lease from a related landlord must be re-priced to market in the recast.
- **Hurricane exposure**: insurance loss runs and current premium quotes affect coastal businesses and any owned property.

### 2.8 The BOV document  **[Claude → template]**
1. Summary: most probable selling price range, recommended asking price, expected structure, expected time to sell.
2. Business description and transferability.
3. Financial summary: 3 years + TTM, recast schedule with every add-back documented.
4. Approaches applied and reconciliation.
5. Comparable transactions cited (source, date, size).
6. Risk grid and adjustments.
7. Real estate / license components valued separately.
8. Buyer's test: illustrative SBA structure and debt-service coverage.
9. What would raise the value in 12 months (feeds S4).
10. Limiting conditions and disclaimer.

**Turnaround:** 10 business days from complete documents. **Fee:** S3 flat, credited against the success fee if listed within 6 months.

### 2.9 Sanity checks before the BOV goes out
- Does the price survive the buyer's test with a 10% down SBA loan, a market salary for the buyer, and 1.25× coverage?
- Would we personally pay that for the business? If not, why would a buyer?
- Does the seller's expectation gap (§1.2 line 10) mean we should decline the listing even if the BOV is paid for?

## 3. E3 — Due diligence

### 3.1 Buy-side: verify, don't trust
The recast built the price on the seller's documents. Diligence proves them from third-party sources.

| Area | What we verify | Against |
|---|---|---|
| **Revenue** | Reported sales are real | Bank deposits, merchant-processor statements, POS reports, **DOR sales tax returns** (F.S. 212), 1099-Ks |
| **Expenses & add-backs** | Every add-back is real and won't recur | Invoices, payroll reports, vendor statements |
| **Payroll** | Headcount, wages, classification | Form 941s, W-2s/1099s, workers' comp audit, E-Verify status |
| **Taxes** | Nothing owed that follows the assets | IRS transcripts, DOR clearance / certificate of compliance, county tangible tax, business tax receipt |
| **Liens** | Assets come free and clear | UCC search (Florida Secured Transaction Registry), judgment and lien searches, equipment financing payoff letters |
| **Entity** | Seller can sell what they're selling | Sunbiz, operating agreement/bylaws, member consents, any spousal consent |
| **Contracts** | Customer, vendor, and franchise contracts transfer | Assignment clauses, change-of-control clauses, franchisor consent |
| **Lease** | Terms are as represented, assignment allowed | Lease + amendments, landlord estoppel, assignment consent, security deposit |
| **Licenses** | Every permit transfers or can be re-issued | DBPR, DOH, ABT, county/city, EPA/DEP where relevant |
| **Litigation & compliance** | No hidden claims | Court dockets, demand letters, OSHA/EEOC/DOL history, insurance loss runs |
| **Employees** | Key people stay | Interviews after LOI, retention agreements, existing non-competes |
| **Assets & inventory** | Physical count matches the list | Walk-through, equipment list with serials, inventory count at closing |
| **IP & digital** | Name, domain, reviews, social, software transfer | Trademark search, domain registrar, account ownership |
| **Real property** | Title, survey, environmental, condition | Title commitment, survey, Phase I ESA where any risk, inspection |
| **Insurance** | Coverable and at what cost | Loss runs, quotes for the buyer |

Deliverable: a diligence memo with **deal-breakers, price adjustments, and closing conditions**. Timeline: 30–45 days from LOI on Main Street, 60–90 on larger deals.

### 3.2 Sell-side: the Exit-Readiness Audit (S4, law firm)
Same checklist, run 6–24 months *before* listing, so problems get fixed instead of discovered. Output is a punch list with a value impact next to each item ("extend the lease: +0.3× on the multiple"). Sold as legal work through Truestead Law; feeds E2 and the listing.

## 4. E4 — Qualifying the buyer

No confidential information (name, address, financials) leaves the office until:
1. **NDA** signed (e-sign via `/signing`).
2. **Buyer profile**: background, industry experience, why this business, timeline.
3. **Personal financial statement** and **proof of funds** for the down payment (10–20% on SBA deals; more on non-SBA).
4. **Financing path**: SBA pre-qualification letter from a lender we know, or cash, or seller-note expectation stated up front.
5. **Eligibility checks:**
   - SBA 7(a) requires the buyer to be a U.S. citizen or lawful permanent resident (E-2 holders are generally not eligible) — steer foreign buyers to cash or non-SBA structures.
   - **E-2 buyers**: the investment must be "substantial" and the business non-marginal; the BOV, business plan, and source-of-funds trail are what the consulate reads. Co-counsel on immigration; we do the acquisition side.
   - Licenses the buyer must personally hold (contractor qualifier, professional licenses, alcohol-license background check).
6. **Fit call** with the broker before any management meeting.

Buyers who fail the gate get a polite no and stay in the database for a better-fit listing.

### 5.2 Templates and internal tools  **[Claude]**
- **Listing screen scorecard** (§1.2) as a form → score → path, wired into the intake.
- **Seller document request list** (§1.1 + full diligence list) with a secure upload folder per prospect (portal).
- **Recast / SDE workbook**: 3 years + TTM, add-back schedule with a "document attached?" column, weighted SDE, multiple grid, three-approach reconciliation, buyer's-test tab with an SBA amortization calculator. Build once as a spreadsheet; later as an internal web tool.
- **BOV report template** (§2.8) with the disclaimer block and a comps table that pulls from the workbook.
- **Diligence checklist** (§3.1) as a tracked list per deal with owner, status, and document link.
- **Buyer qualification file** (§4): NDA e-sign, profile form, proof-of-funds upload, eligibility flags.
- **Public tools** (main plan §4.4): Business Value Estimator (simplified SDE × industry range, always framed as a starting point) and the Exit-Readiness Score, both feeding the S3/S4 pitch.

### 5.3 People and credentials
- **Arthur**: Certified Business Intermediary (CBI, IBBA) — the brokerage credential that includes valuation and deal-structure training; requires coursework, exam, and closed-deal experience. Later, M&AMI for larger deals. **[Arthur]**
- **Credentialed appraiser partner** (CVA / ABV / ASA) for SBA-threshold deals, estate and gift tax, litigation, and buy-sell disputes. This is also a referral source *to* us. **[Arthur]**
- **CPA partner** for quality-of-earnings on deals above ~$2M and for purchase-price allocation (Form 8594).
- **Equipment appraiser** and **commercial real estate appraiser** on call.
- **SBA lender relationships** (2–3 preferred lenders) so buyer pre-qualification is fast and our BOVs are built the way their underwriters read them.
- **Immigration co-counsel** for E-2 / EB-5 buyers.

### 5.4 Process standards
- No BOV without tax returns. No listing without a BOV. No confidential package without a qualified buyer file.
- Every number in a BOV traces to a document; every add-back has an exhibit.
- BOVs are reviewed by a second person (associate or Arthur) before release.
- Multiples data refreshed quarterly; the grid in §2.5 revisited annually against our own closed deals.
- Files retained per Chapter 475 record-keeping rules and Bar rules where the law firm was engaged.

### 5.5 Compliance reminders specific to evaluations
- Never call a BOV an appraisal; never let a buyer or lender rely on it as one; put the SBA/estate-tax referral in writing when it applies.
- The valuation is brokerage work (transaction-broker relationship, F.S. 475.278). The Exit-Readiness Audit is legal work. Different engagement letters, different entities, per the main plan's one-hat rule.
- Seller-provided numbers are represented by the seller; the listing agreement and the CIM carry the seller's representation and our non-verification disclaimer, and diligence is the buyer's protection.

## 6. Worked example (illustrative only)

Ormond Beach HVAC contractor, owner-operated, 8 employees, leased shop with 6 years left, seller is the qualifying agent and will stay 12 months.

| Line | Amount |
|---|---|
| Revenue (TTM) | $2,100,000 |
| Net income per return | $180,000 |
| + Owner salary & payroll tax | $110,000 |
| + Owner auto, health, phone (documented) | $22,000 |
| + Interest | $9,000 |
| + Depreciation | $48,000 |
| + One-time lawsuit settlement | $15,000 |
| − Market rent adjustment (owner's LLC charges below market) | −$18,000 |
| **SDE** | **$366,000** |
| Industry median multiple (HVAC, this size) | ~2.5× |
| Grid: management in place (+), qualifier risk (−), 6-yr lease (+), clean books (+) | net ≈ +0.2× → 2.7× |
| **Indicated value (SDE method)** | **≈ $990,000** |
| Asset approach floor (fleet, tools, receivables − payables) | ≈ $410,000 |
| Rule-of-thumb check (2–3× SDE for HVAC) | $730K–$1.1M |
| **BOV range** | **$900K–$1.05M**, asking $995K + inventory at cost |
| Buyer's test: 10% down, SBA 10-yr at ~10.5%, seller note 10% | Debt service ≈ $145K/yr; buyer salary $100K; cushion ≈ $120K → coverage ≈ 1.5× ✔ |
| Structure notes | Asset sale; buyer must bring or hire a qualifier or contract the seller as qualifier for 12 months; 3-year, 50-mile non-compete; DOR clearance and lease assignment as conditions |

Everything above is invented to show the mechanics. Real multiples come from §2.4 sources at the time of the BOV.
