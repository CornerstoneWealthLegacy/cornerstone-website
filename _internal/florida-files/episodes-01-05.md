## EP 01 — "Florida Sold the Same Swamp Eleven Times" (DEED · real estate)
**HOOK (on screen + spoken):** "In the 1920s, Florida sold the same piece of swamp to eleven different people."
**SCRIPT:**
> In the 1920s, Florida sold the same piece of swamp to eleven different people. Not a scam artist — the *market* did. The land boom was so insane that lots were flipped by telegram, sight unseen, sometimes three times in a day. People bought "waterfront" that was water*front* because it was underwater. When the music stopped in 1926, thousands of families held deeds to land that didn't exist, wasn't theirs — or was somebody's alligator pond.
> And that disaster is why closings work the way they do today. Title searches. Title insurance. Surveys. Every "annoying" step in your closing exists because Florida already ran the experiment of skipping them — and it cost people everything.
> So when someone tells you title insurance is a rip-off? Florida history disagrees.
> Buying or selling? DM the word **DEED** and I'll take it from there.
**B-ROLL:** 1920s land-sales office, crowds waving paper deeds; telegram close-up; aerial swampland; 1926 bust — empty sales office, blowing papers.
**CAPTIONS EMPHASIZE:** ELEVEN DIFFERENT PEOPLE · UNDERWATER · TITLE INSURANCE · DEED
**Compliance:** educational; "Attorney advertising" microline.

## EP 02 — "Why the Beach Belongs to You" (CRASH-adjacent trust builder · PI brand halo)
**HOOK:** "Someone once tried to own the beach. Florida said no."
**SCRIPT:**
> Someone once tried to own the beach. Rich landowners fenced the sand, hired guards, and told regular Floridians the ocean was private property now. Florida courts said no — and built a rule with a beautiful name: the *public trust doctrine*. The wet sand, the shore, the water — held in trust for everyone, forever. Your beach day is a legal right people had to fight for.
> Here's why I tell that story: it's the whole idea of Florida law at its best. The powerful push; the law is supposed to push back — for everybody, not just the people with fences and guards.
> That's the kind of law we practice. When an insurance company treats you like you're fenced out of your own claim — that's exactly the fight the law was built for.
> If you've been hurt and you're getting stonewalled, DM the word **CRASH**.
**B-ROLL:** old-Florida beach, wooden fence on sand, guard silhouette; gavel; modern family on open beach.
**CAPTIONS:** TRIED TO OWN THE BEACH · HELD IN TRUST FOR EVERYONE · PUSH BACK · CRASH
**Compliance:** no outcome promises ("the fight the law was built for" = principle, not result). Attorney advertising.

## EP 03 — "The Snowbird Trap" (LEGACY · estate planning)
**HOOK:** "Your northern will can break the moment you become a Florida resident."
**SCRIPT:**
> Every year, thousands of people retire to Florida with a will from New York, or Ohio, or Michigan — and no idea it may not work the way they think here. Florida has its own rules: who can be your personal representative, how homestead property passes, what makes a will valid, what your spouse is entitled to no matter what the document says. A plan that was airtight in Buffalo can spring leaks in Boca.
> This isn't new. Florida's been America's retirement dream for a century — and for a century, the saddest files in any Florida courthouse have been families discovering *after* the funeral that the plan didn't survive the move.
> The fix is usually simple. A Florida review. Before it matters.
> If you've moved here — or your parents have — DM the word **LEGACY**.
**B-ROLL:** vintage postcards "Greetings from Florida"; 1950s retirees at a mid-century motel pool; courthouse steps; document close-up.
**CAPTIONS:** CAN BREAK · HOMESTEAD · AFTER THE FUNERAL · LEGACY
**Compliance:** "may not work / usually simple" hedged; educational. Attorney advertising.

## EP 04 — "The Citrus Fortune War" (LEGACY · elder law)
**HOOK:** "One of Florida's great citrus fortunes was nearly taken from the man who built it — while he was still alive."
**SCRIPT:**
> Here's a Florida story nobody tells at the orange-juice commercial. A grove baron builds a fortune over fifty years. He gets old. And the circling starts — a late-life "advisor" here, a new signature there, family pushed out of the room. Fights like that are why Florida built one of the country's most detailed guardianship and elder-protection systems: courts that can step in, undo the signatures, and put family back at the table.
> But here's what those old files teach: the law usually arrived *after* the damage. The families who kept their fortunes — and their dignity — were the ones with paperwork done early: powers of attorney they chose, trusts they controlled, while they were sharp and nobody could argue it.
> Florida invented the retirement dream. It also perfected protecting it.
> If someone you love is aging in Florida, DM the word **LEGACY**.
**B-ROLL:** golden-hour orange groves, 1940s citrus crates and packing house; an old ledger with signatures; courthouse; family at a kitchen table.
**CAPTIONS:** WHILE HE WAS STILL ALIVE · THE CIRCLING STARTS · DONE EARLY · LEGACY
**Compliance:** composite/archetype framing ("a grove baron," no named real family) — defamation-safe; educational. Attorney advertising.

## EP 05 — "Cowboys Don't Spook" (TRUTH · flagship — the judge, told as PUBLIC history; kinship line added post-Phase-0)
**HOOK:** "A Florida judge got death threats in the mail for years. He kept every ruling anyway."
**SCRIPT:**
> They called him Cowboy — an actual cowboy, who paid for law school working a cattle ranch near Ocala. By the 1960s he was a federal judge in Jacksonville, and his mail was full of death threats. Why? Because he ruled that Florida's schools belonged to every child in them. Because in the summer of 1964, when marchers were being beaten in St. Augustine, he signed the first federal orders in the South protecting them. The Governor of Florida himself pushed back — and the Cowboy threatened him with contempt. The governor blinked.
> Asked about the threats, he basically shrugged: real assassins don't write first. He whittled wood during recesses and kept ruling.
> Today the federal courthouse in Jacksonville has his name on it: Bryan Simpson.
> That's the standard for lawyering in this state. Courage is supposed to be part of the job.
> DM the word **TRUTH** if you ever need someone who won't spook.
**B-ROLL:** cattle ranch dawn; law books; 1960s courthouse exterior; marchers (respectful, archival-style); letter on a desk; whittling hands; the modern courthouse sign.
**CAPTIONS:** DEATH THREATS · FIRST FEDERAL ORDERS IN THE SOUTH · THE GOVERNOR BLINKED · BRYAN SIMPSON
**Compliance + versioning:** v1 = pure public history (all sourced — see simpson-heritage dossier), NO kinship claim. **v2 (post-Phase-0):** re-record the final 8s: "…the courthouse has his name on it. It's also my family's name. Justice runs in this family."
