# GCRID — Two-Entity Setup Brief
### What to hand your nonprofit + FCPA-aware attorney

> Not legal advice — a structuring brief to brief counsel efficiently. The goal: a nonprofit that can take UN status + grants + government MOUs, with a clean wall to the for-profit engine, so neither contaminates the other.

---

## The structure

```
                 DARCOCEAN (parent brand / holding concept)
                          │
        ┌─────────────────┴──────────────────┐
   GCRID INSTITUTE                     GCRID HOLDINGS / Truestead Law, LLC
   501(c)(3) nonprofit                 for-profit engine
   (+ optional (c)(6) trade arm)       (law firm + GCRID LLC)
   ─ UN status, grants, MOUs           ─ deal flow, builder fees,
   ─ the Charter + Standard              structuring, referral share,
   ─ certification, summit, registry     development equity
   ─ capacity-building                 ─ Truestead = U.S./FL legal execution
        │                                       │
        └──────────── arm's-length ─────────────┘
                  (FMV contracts only)
```

### Why two (or three) entities
- **501(c)(3)** — charitable/educational: research, capacity-building, transparency, property-rights education. Eligible for grants + tax-deductible donations + the credibility governments/UN require. *Trade-off: tighter limits, can't primarily benefit a private firm.*
- **Optional 501(c)(6)** — a trade/membership body (like FIABCI/NAR). Better fit for *member dues + industry standard-setting*, fewer charitable constraints, but no tax-deductible donations and limited grant eligibility. Many ecosystems run **both**: (c)(3) for grants/education, (c)(6) for the membership coalition.
- **For-profit LLC** — everything that makes money from deals.

**Recommendation:** start with the **(c)(3) Institute** (it's the UN/grant key), run the **coalition membership** either inside a (c)(6) or as a program, and keep **all transactional revenue in the LLC.**

---

## The wall (violate this and the nonprofit is dead)
- **No private inurement / private benefit:** the Institute cannot exist to enrich Truestead/Arthur. Its activities must primarily serve the public/charitable mission.
- **Independent board majority** — a majority not financially tied to the for-profit. This is what auditors and grantors check.
- **Arm's-length, FMV contracts** for any service the LLC provides the Institute (and vice versa), documented, board-approved, conflicts disclosed and recused.
- **No legal-fee splitting** with non-lawyer members (FL Bar 4-5.4) — referral/co-counsel/commission only.
- **Separate books, bank accounts, branding** on government-facing materials.

---

## Compliance policies to adopt at formation (the survival kit)
1. **Anti-Corruption / FCPA policy** — no bribes or facilitation payments; due diligence on every intermediary/agent; gifts/hospitality limits; red-flag training; audit rights.
2. **OFAC / Sanctions screening policy** — screen every member, counterparty, country, and donor; documented hard-no list; re-screen periodically.
3. **AML / KYC policy** — mirrors the GCRID Transaction Standard.
4. **Conflict-of-interest policy** — board + staff; annual disclosures (also an IRS Form 1023 expectation).
5. **Data/privacy policy** — for cross-border personal data in transactions.

---

## Filing checklist (for counsel)
- [ ] Form the nonprofit (state) — articles with the IRS-required charitable purpose + dissolution clauses.
- [ ] EIN.
- [ ] **IRS Form 1023** (501(c)(3) recognition) — or 1024 for (c)(6). Conflict-of-interest policy attached.
- [ ] State charitable-solicitation registration (each state you fundraise in).
- [ ] Bylaws + independent board seated.
- [ ] Adopt the 5 compliance policies above.
- [ ] LLC operating agreement updated to define the arm's-length relationship.
- [ ] Trademark: GCRID, the Standard mark, UN1FYD (separately).
- [ ] D&O insurance for the board.

## What to tell the attorney in one paragraph
"I'm forming a nonprofit institute to advance transparent cross-border real-estate markets — it will pursue UN ECOSOC consultative status, NGO/development grants, and MOUs with foreign real-estate authorities, and publish a transparency certification standard. I have a separate for-profit law firm and LLC that will handle the actual transactions. I need a clean two-entity structure with a private-inurement firewall, an FCPA/OFAC/AML compliance stack, and FL Bar 4-5.4-compliant referral arrangements with foreign counsel. Help me file the (c)(3) (and advise whether to add a (c)(6) for the membership side)."

---

## Sequencing
File the (c)(3) **now** — it's the prerequisite for UN status, grants, and credible MOUs. The LLC already exists (Truestead). The wall + compliance policies are cheap to adopt at birth and very expensive to retrofit.
