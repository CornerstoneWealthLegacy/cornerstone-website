## 0. The one-page version

**The thesis.** Most Florida business sales under ~$5M are handled by one of three people who each only see a third of the deal: a business broker who can't paper it, a lawyer who can't price or market it, or a commercial realtor who ignores the operating company. Arthur is already all three at once — Florida attorney (Bar #529265), licensed Florida real estate broker, CIPS, and founder of GCRID. A brokerage that sits on top of the existing firm turns that into a product: **one advisor takes the owner from "should I sell?" to a funded closing, and the buyer from "what should I buy?" to owning it.**

**Why it's cheap for us to start.**
- Florida's license for business brokerage *is* the real estate broker license. Chapter 475 defines "broker" to include anyone who, for compensation, sells or lists **"business enterprises or business opportunities"** (F.S. 475.01(1)(a)). Arthur already holds it. The only new registration is a brokerage entity with DBPR/FREC.
- The firm already sells the two documents that create future sales: the **buy-sell agreement** ($399/$999) and the **Florida LLC Kit** ($149–$1,200). The Estate Kit's "Business Interests" section is a live census of owners with something to sell.
- Probate, trust administration, elder law, and the international practice already produce **forced or motivated sales**: a business in an estate, an owner who can't continue, a foreign buyer who needs an operating business.
- The site machine (city/metro generators, quiz and calculator patterns, Stripe, e-signing, portal, daily article pipeline) can stand up the brokerage's web presence in weeks.

**What we're selling.** Sell-side brokerage (Main Street, $250K–$5M), buy-side search, broker opinions of value, exit-readiness legal audits, and a flat-fee transaction legal package for deals brokered by *other* brokers. Real estate is bundled in whenever the business owns or leases its premises.

**The hard part.** Not licensing — ethics. A lawyer who is also the commissioned broker on the same deal walks into Rules 4-1.7, 4-1.8, 4-5.4, and 4-5.7. The plan is built around **one hat per transaction** (§3). This is the first thing Arthur decides; everything else waits on it.

**Illustrative year-one target.** 6–8 closings at a ~$500K average and a 10% success fee is roughly $300K–$400K in commissions, plus the legal and valuation revenue around deals we don't broker. Detail in §7.

## 3. Structure & compliance — decide this first  **[Arthur]**

### 3.1 Licensing (the easy part)
- **F.S. 475.01(1)(a):** "broker" includes a person who sells, exchanges, lists, or negotiates the sale of **business enterprises or business opportunities** for compensation. Business brokerage in Florida requires a Chapter 475 license. Arthur holds a broker license → **no new individual license.**
- **F.S. 475.011(2)** exempts attorneys acting *within the scope of their duties as an attorney*. That covers S4/S5 legal work, not commissioned listing or buyer representation. Do not rely on the attorney exemption for anything with a commission attached.
- **Brokerage entity:** register a brokerage (LLC or corporation) with DBPR/FREC with Arthur as qualifying broker; DBA "Truestead Business Brokerage" (or the name Arthur picks — see §9). Sales associates, if hired, license under it.
- **Transaction-broker default (F.S. 475.278):** Florida presumes a transaction broker unless single agency is established in writing. Use transaction-broker relationship for S1 by default; single agency only where the client insists and we document it. Buy-side (S2) → single agent in writing.
- **Escrow (F.S. 475.25(1)(k)):** deposits held by the brokerage must be in a broker escrow account with monthly reconciliation, *or* held by a title company / attorney with the required written disclosures. **Recommendation:** route all deal deposits to a third-party escrow (title company) in year one; do not mix the law firm's trust account with brokerage deposits.

### 3.2 Securities: asset sales vs. stock sales
- An **asset sale** (most Main Street deals) is not a securities transaction. Prefer it.
- A **stock / membership-interest sale** can be. Two safe harbors:
  - **Federal:** Exchange Act §15(b)(13) (effective March 2023) exempts "M&A brokers" from broker-dealer registration where the target is privately held with EBITDA under $25M or revenue under $250M, the buyer will control and actively operate the business, and the broker does not handle funds or securities, among other conditions.
  - **Florida:** the Office of Financial Regulation's M&A-broker exemption rule under Chapter 517 (amended May 6, 2024; Ch. 517 itself reorganized by Ch. 2024-168, effective Oct. 1, 2024). **[Arthur]** confirm the current rule text and conditions before the first stock deal; build the engagement letter to stay inside both exemptions.
- Never advertise or solicit passive-investor deals (minority stakes, syndications) through the brokerage.

### 3.3 The ethics wall — "one hat per transaction"
This is the design constraint the whole business runs on.

| Rule | Issue | How we comply |
|---|---|---|
| **4-5.7** (nonlegal services) | If the brokerage is not *distinct* from the law practice, every Bar rule applies to the brokerage work too | Separate entity, separate engagement letter, separate bank accounts, separate letterhead/site section; written 4-5.7(d)-style notice that brokerage clients are **not** law-firm clients and get no privilege |
| **4-1.7** (conflicts) | Attorney can't represent buyer and seller; broker on one side can't be lawyer for the other | Per deal Arthur is **either** the broker **or** a party's lawyer, never both. Other parties are told in writing to retain their own counsel. Checklist gate in the intake |
| **4-1.8(a)** (business transactions with clients) | Earning a commission from an existing law client's sale is a business transaction with a client | Written informed consent, fair terms in writing, advice to seek independent counsel, documented before listing |
| **4-5.4** (fee sharing with non-lawyers) | Commission splits with sales associates / co-brokers are fine at the brokerage; **legal fees** may never be split with them | Legal fees only flow to Truestead Law; commissions only flow through the brokerage; no referral fees between the two |
| **4-7** (advertising) | New pages, "attorney & broker" claims, testimonials, comparisons | All brokerage pages that mention the law license go through the same Bar-compliance pass as the estate-kit ads (`_internal/ADS-PLAN.md` §4 approach) |

**Action:** before launch, call the Florida Bar Ethics Hotline and, ideally, request a written informal opinion on the structure above. Attach the answer to this file. The Bar's *Ancillary & Other Business Arrangements* packet and Rule 4-5.7 are the starting references.

### 3.4 Insurance
- The malpractice policy almost certainly excludes real estate / business brokerage activity. **[Arthur]** obtain a separate brokerage E&O policy, and confirm the law-firm policy's ancillary-business exclusion in writing.

### 3.5 Deal-specific Florida compliance we bake into the checklist  **[Claude → template]**
- **Non-competes (F.S. 542.335):** a covenant tied to the sale of a business is presumed reasonable at ≤3 years and unreasonable at >7; draft to the safe end.
- **Sales-tax transferee liability (F.S. 213.758):** buyer obtains a Florida Department of Revenue tax-clearance / certificate of compliance before closing or inherits the seller's tax exposure. Florida has no UCC Article 6 bulk-sale notice, so this is the substitute protection.
- **UCC / lien searches**, Sunbiz standing, fictitious-name transfer, EIN/new entity for buyer.
- **Licenses:** DBPR alcoholic-beverage license transfers; contractor **qualifying agent** (F.S. 489) for trades; health/medical (Ch. 456/458), DOH facility licenses — flag in intake so a deal isn't dead at closing.
- **Lease assignment consent** and landlord estoppel; or buy the building (S6).
- **Employees:** Florida is at-will; still handle PTO, WARN thresholds if large, and assumption of employment agreements.
- **Allocation of purchase price (IRS Form 8594)** agreed in the APA with the CPA.

### 3.6 What the website must say
- The brokerage and the law firm are separate; which one you're engaging is on the first screen of every intake.
- Brokerage pages carry: "Truestead Business Brokerage, LLC is a licensed Florida real estate brokerage. It is not a law firm. Legal services are provided separately by Truestead Law, LLC under a separate written engagement."
- No "guarantee," "best," or comparative superlatives; no results claims without the Bar's required qualifiers.

## 6. The sell-side process (what the client experiences)

1. **Confidential conversation** (free) → is it sellable, roughly what range, what needs fixing.
2. **Exit-Readiness Audit** (S4, law firm) if 6+ months out; otherwise straight to 3.
3. **Broker Opinion of Value** (S3) → price strategy, deal structure options with the CPA.
4. **Listing agreement** (brokerage, transaction-broker disclosure, 4-1.8 consent if an existing law client).
5. **Package:** blind teaser, NDA gate, confidential information memorandum, financial recast.
6. **Market:** BBF/BizBuySell, buyer database, partner outreach, GCRID for cross-border.
7. **Buyer screening:** NDA, proof of funds, SBA pre-qual; management meetings.
8. **Offer → LOI** (brokerage) → **buyer's counsel and seller's counsel** paper the APA. If Arthur is the broker, the seller uses another lawyer; Truestead Law may be recommended only with the 4-5.7 / 4-1.7 disclosures and only if it is not a two-hat situation.
9. **Due diligence & closing checklist** (§3.5), landlord consent, tax clearance, license transfers, funding.
10. **Close & transition;** seller gets the post-sale estate / asset-protection consult (that one *is* Truestead Law, after the brokerage engagement ends).

Buy-side (S2) mirrors it: criteria → search (on- and off-market letters to owners in our matrix) → screening → LOI → diligence → close → the buyer's new entity, operating agreement, and lease are Truestead Law work under a separate engagement.

## 8. Risks and how we handle them

| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| Two-hat conflict complaint | One-hat rule, written consents, ethics-hotline opinion on file, intake gate that literally blocks dual engagement |
| Malpractice policy exclusion | Separate brokerage E&O before the first listing |
| Arthur's time (brokerage is labor-heavy) | Hire a licensed, BBF-experienced sales associate on a split; Arthur does valuation, structure, negotiation, and the legal-adjacent parts |
| Stock deal drifts into securities territory | Asset-sale default; §15(b)(13) + Florida OFR exemption checklist in every stock-deal file |
| Confidentiality breach kills a listing | Blind teasers, NDA-before-name, watermarked CIM, buyer verification |
| Escrow handling errors | Third-party escrow in year one |
| Listing businesses that can't sell | Minimum-fee floor, financials-first rule, decline sub-$150K |
| Bar advertising issue on "attorney & broker" claims | Compliance pass on every page; factual license statements only |
| Brand confusion (law firm vs. brokerage) | Distinct name/lockup, separate domain or clearly separated section, disclosure block everywhere |
