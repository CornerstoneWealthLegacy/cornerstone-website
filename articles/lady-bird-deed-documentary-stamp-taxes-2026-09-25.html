Florida Lady Bird Deeds

Does a Lady Bird Deed Trigger Florida Documentary Stamp Tax?

Quick Answer

In most cases, no. A properly drafted Florida lady bird deed does not transfer any present ownership interest, so it is not treated as a taxable conveyance and only the minimal statutory documentary stamp applies, along with the county's standard recording fee.

By Arthur Simpson, Esq. · FL Bar #529265 Florida Estate Planning Attorney September 25, 2026

Marta's Question: What Her Cousin's Deed Cost Her

Marta is 71, lives in Hialeah, and has owned her home for over three decades. She wants her son to inherit the house without a probate case, but she isn't ready to hand over the deed today. She still wants to sell it, refinance it, or change her mind if life throws her a curveball. A lady bird deed does exactly that: it lets an owner keep full control during life, including the right to sell or revoke, while the property passes automatically to named beneficiaries at death without probate.

What Documentary Stamp Tax Actually Is

Florida's documentary stamp tax, found in F.S. § 201.02, is a tax on the transfer of real property, calculated based on the consideration paid for the property. Consideration usually means cash changing hands, but it can also include a mortgage balance the new owner takes on. When a house sells for a stated price, the doc stamp tax is calculated on that sale price, and it adds up fast on a typical home purchase.

The tax is collected by the county clerk when the deed is recorded, not by the Florida Department of Revenue directly, though the DOR sets and enforces the rules behind it.

When a Deed Has No Consideration

Not every deed involves a sale. When a parent adds a child to the title as a gift, or transfers property to a family member for love and affection with no money involved, Florida law treats that as a transfer with no consideration. In that situation, the deed still gets recorded, but instead of a tax calculated on a sale price, only the statutory minimum documentary stamp applies, a small fixed amount rather than a percentage of value.

That's the deed Marta's cousin thought she was signing. And if the property had been completely free of any mortgage, that minimum is likely all she would have owed.

The Mortgage-Balance Trap on Gift Deeds

⚠ Where Gift Deeds Go Wrong If the home carries an existing mortgage and the new co-owner is considered to be taking on responsibility for that debt, Florida law can treat the unpaid mortgage balance as "consideration." That transforms what felt like a simple gift into a taxable transfer, with documentary stamp tax calculated on the loan balance, not the home's market value.

This is almost certainly what happened to Marta's cousin. Once her daughter's name went on the title of a mortgaged property, the clerk's office (correctly, under Florida law) calculated doc stamps as though the daughter had paid consideration equal to the mortgage balance. On a home with real debt left on it, that bill can run into the hundreds or thousands of dollars, exactly what Marta's cousin experienced.

This is one of the most common surprises I see in my practice: families think a gift deed is free, and then the mortgage turns it into a taxable event.

Why a Lady Bird Deed Usually Sidesteps This Problem

A lady bird deed is structured differently from a standard gift deed, and that structural difference is exactly why it usually avoids the mortgage trap. Because the owner retains full control, including the unrestricted right to sell, mortgage, or revoke the deed at any time, the beneficiaries named in the deed receive nothing during the owner's lifetime. Florida's own tax authority has recognized this distinction directly: the Florida Department of Revenue issued a Technical Assistance Advisement addressing lady bird deeds and concluded that because the deed transfers no present beneficial interest in the property, it is not subject to documentary stamp tax regardless of any stated consideration.

That ruling involved a property with no mortgage or liens. Whether the same reasoning applies cleanly to mortgaged property has not been fully settled, since a remaining loan balance introduces the same kind of "consideration" question that trips up gift deeds. Most Florida practitioners, myself included, take the position that a lady bird deed should not trigger doc stamps on the mortgage balance because there is no present transfer of anything, including no present transfer of debt responsibility. But if your situation involves a mortgage and you want certainty, that's worth a direct conversation with your attorney before recording.

The Core Distinction A gift deed transfers ownership today. A lady bird deed transfers nothing until death. Florida's documentary stamp tax is a tax on present transfers, so a deed that reserves full lifetime control generally has nothing present to tax.

Frequently Asked Questions

Will I owe documentary stamp tax when I record a lady bird deed in Florida?
In most cases, no more than the small statutory minimum, because the deed does not transfer any present ownership interest. The Florida Department of Revenue has confirmed this position in at least one published ruling involving unencumbered property.
Does it matter if my home still has a mortgage?
It can add complexity. While most practitioners believe a lady bird deed should not trigger doc stamps on the mortgage balance since nothing transfers during your lifetime, this specific scenario has not been fully tested, so it's worth confirming with your attorney if your home is mortgaged.
How is this different from adding my child to the deed as a joint owner?
Adding a co-owner through a standard deed is a present transfer, and if there's a mortgage, the balance can be treated as consideration, triggering documentary stamp tax right away. A lady bird deed avoids a present transfer entirely, which is what usually keeps it out of that tax.
What does the county clerk actually charge to record a lady bird deed?
Recording fees are set by the county clerk and generally charged per page, separate from any documentary stamp tax. These fees are modest and are included in Truestead's attorney-prepared lady bird deed package.
Is a lady bird deed created by a specific Florida statute?
No. Florida has no statute that creates the lady bird deed by name. It rests on long-standing common law principles, Florida title standards, and decades of accepted practice among Florida real estate attorneys and title companies.
Can I use a lady bird deed if I'm married and the home is our homestead?
A homestead owned by a married person generally cannot be conveyed or devised without both spouses joining in the deed, so this is an important detail to review with an attorney before signing.
