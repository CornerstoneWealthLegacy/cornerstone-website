Car Accident Lawyer in Lake Placid, Florida: What You Need to Know First

Quick Answer

If you were hurt in a car accident in Lake Placid or anywhere in Highlands County, Florida law gives you two years from the date of the crash to file a lawsuit, and you generally have only 14 days to see a doctor if you want your own insurance to pay your initial medical bills. A Florida-licensed personal injury attorney typically works on contingency, meaning you pay nothing upfront, and reviewing your situation early helps protect both deadlines.

By Arthur Simpson, Esq. · FL Bar #529265 Florida Personal Injury Attorney November 17, 2026

What Does a Car Accident Lawyer in Lake Placid Actually Do for You?

In my practice, I find that most people searching for a car accident lawyer in Lake Placid aren't looking for a courtroom drama. They want someone to deal with the insurance company, make sense of their medical bills, and tell them honestly whether they have a claim worth pursuing. That's really the core of the job: investigating what happened on the road, dealing with Personal Injury Protection (PIP) and liability insurers, documenting your injuries and losses, and, if a fair settlement isn't offered, filing suit before your time runs out.

Lake Placid sits in Highlands County, and residents here deal with the same statewide rules as drivers in Sebring, Avon Park, or anywhere else in Florida. There's nothing unique about Highlands County law, but local knowledge still matters: familiarity with the roads (like US-27 and State Road 70), local hospitals and treating physicians, and the court where your case would be filed if it comes to that.

How Long Do I Have to File a Car Accident Claim in Florida?

This is the question I get asked first, and it's the most important one to get right. Florida's statute of limitations for most personal injury claims, including car accidents, is two years from the date of the crash. This shorter deadline, found in F.S. § 95.11, took effect under a 2023 reform (HB 837) and applies to accidents occurring on or after March 24, 2023. Before that reform, Florida law allowed four years, so if you or a family member were hurt in an older accident, the timeline that applies may depend on exactly when the crash happened.

⚠ Don't Wait to Act Missing Florida's two-year deadline generally means losing your right to sue entirely, no matter how clear the other driver's fault was. If you're unsure which deadline applies to your situation, that's a conversation to have with a Florida attorney as soon as possible, not something to guess at.

What Is the 14-Day Rule, and Why Does It Matter So Much?

Florida is a no-fault insurance state, which means your own auto policy, not the other driver's, pays your initial medical bills through Personal Injury Protection (PIP) coverage, regardless of who caused the crash. Every registered vehicle owner in Florida is required to carry at least $10,000 in PIP coverage under F.S. § 627.736.

Here's the part that catches people off guard: Florida law requires you to seek initial medical treatment within 14 days of the accident to remain eligible for PIP benefits. Wait longer than that, even if you're genuinely still in pain and simply didn't get around to it, and your insurer can deny the claim outright. Once your insurer has written notice of a covered loss and the amount owed, it generally must pay within 30 days or the payment is considered overdue.

  • See a doctor, urgent care, or emergency room within 14 days of the crash, even if your injuries seem minor at first.
  • Keep copies of all medical bills and records tied to the accident.
  • Report the claim to your own insurer promptly, in addition to any report involving the other driver's insurer.

Do I Need a Lawyer If the Insurance Company Already Offered Me Money?

Not every fender-bender needs an attorney. But I'd encourage caution before signing any release or accepting a quick settlement, especially if you have ongoing medical treatment, missed work, or injuries that might take weeks or months to fully reveal themselves. Once you sign a release, you typically can't go back and ask for more if it turns out your injuries were worse than first thought.

Because Florida uses a modified comparative negligence standard, how fault is allocated between drivers can also significantly affect what you're entitled to recover. An early insurance offer may not reflect the full value of a claim once all medical treatment, lost wages, and the other driver's share of fault are properly accounted for. This is exactly the kind of situation where having your specific facts reviewed by a Florida attorney, rather than relying on the insurance adjuster's number, tends to pay off.

How Do I Choose the Right Car Accident Lawyer in Lake Placid?

Credentials matter here. At minimum, confirm the lawyer is a member in good standing of The Florida Bar with no disciplinary history, which you can verify directly at FloridaBar.org. Some attorneys also hold Board Certification in Civil Trial Law, a voluntary credential that only a small percentage of Florida lawyers earn, reflecting significant trial experience and peer review.

What to Ask Before You Hire Ask how the attorney's fee works, who will actually handle your file day to day, whether they've handled car accident cases in Highlands County before, and how they communicate case updates. A good fit should answer these clearly and without pressure.

On fees: Florida personal injury attorneys typically work on a contingency fee basis, meaning you pay nothing out of pocket upfront and the fee comes out of any settlement or verdict. Under Florida Bar guidelines, a common structure is 33.33% of the recovery if the case settles before a lawsuit is filed, rising to as much as 40% if litigation becomes necessary. Always get the fee agreement in writing before signing anything.

Frequently Asked Questions

What's the deadline to file a car accident lawsuit in Lake Placid, Florida?
For most accidents occurring on or after March 24, 2023, Florida law gives you two years from the date of the crash to file a personal injury lawsuit, under F.S. § 95.11. Accidents before that date may fall under the older four-year rule, so the exact deadline should be confirmed with an attorney.
Does Florida still require PIP insurance in 2026?
Yes. As of this writing, Florida remains a no-fault insurance state and still requires drivers to carry at least $10,000 in Personal Injury Protection coverage under F.S. § 627.736. Proposed legislation to repeal the no-fault system has not become law.
What happens if I don't see a doctor within 14 days of my accident?
Florida's PIP rules generally require that you seek initial medical treatment within 14 days of the crash to remain eligible for those benefits. Waiting longer can allow your insurer to deny the PIP claim, even if your injuries are real and accident-related.
How much does it cost to hire a car accident lawyer in Lake Placid?
Most Florida personal injury attorneys handle car accident cases on a contingency fee basis, meaning there's no upfront cost. A standard arrangement is around 33.33% of the recovery if the case settles before a lawsuit is filed, and up to 40% if litigation becomes necessary.
Can I still recover damages if I was partly at fault for the accident?
Florida uses a modified comparative negligence standard, which can reduce or bar recovery depending on your percentage of fault. How fault is calculated is fact-specific, so it's worth having an attorney review the details of your accident rather than assuming you're barred from recovery.
How do I verify that a Lake Placid car accident lawyer is actually licensed in Florida?
You can confirm any Florida attorney's license status and disciplinary history for free through The Florida Bar's website at FloridaBar.org before hiring them.

The Truestead Takeaway

If you've been in a car accident in Lake Placid or anywhere in Highlands County, the two things that matter most in the first two weeks are getting medical treatment within 14 days to protect your PIP benefits and keeping Florida's two-year filing deadline firmly in mind. Insurance adjusters move fast, and an early lowball offer can look appealing when bills are piling up, but it's rarely the full picture. The sensible next step is to have the specific facts of your accident reviewed by a Florida-licensed attorney before you sign anything or let a deadline slip by.
