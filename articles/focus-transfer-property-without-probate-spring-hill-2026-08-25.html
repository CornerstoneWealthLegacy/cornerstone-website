How to Transfer Property Without Probate in Spring Hill, Florida

Quick Answer

In Florida, you can transfer property without probate mainly through a Lady Bird deed, a revocable living trust, or joint ownership with rights of survivorship—each lets real estate pass directly to your chosen beneficiary at death without going through Hernando County probate court.

By Arthur Simpson, Esq. · FL Bar #529265 Florida Estate Planning Attorney August 25, 2026

What Does It Mean to Transfer Property Without Probate in Spring Hill?

If you own a home in Spring Hill, you've probably heard a neighbor or friend say they "avoided probate" when a parent passed. What that usually means is the property was titled or deeded in a way that let it pass directly to the next owner—without a Hernando County probate case at all. That matters because probate in Florida, even the simplified versions, takes time, costs money, and creates a public court record.

In my practice, I tell Spring Hill clients that there is no single magic form. There are several proven legal tools, and the right one depends on your family situation, whether you're married, whether the home is your homestead, and who you want to inherit it. The most common and reliable options are the Lady Bird deed, the revocable living trust, and joint ownership with right of survivorship.

The Lady Bird Deed: Florida's Favorite Probate-Avoidance Tool

A Lady Bird deed (formally an enhanced life estate deed) is one of the most popular ways Florida homeowners transfer property without probate. You sign and record a deed today that names a beneficiary to receive the home automatically at your death—but you keep full control while you're alive. You can still sell the home, refinance it, rent it out, or change your mind and name someone else, all without needing anyone's permission.

  • The deed is recorded in the Hernando County public records now, but ownership doesn't shift until death.
  • Because the beneficiary receives no present interest, the home isn't exposed to their creditors while you're alive.
  • Heirs generally receive a stepped-up basis for capital gains purposes, similar to inheriting through a will.

It's worth knowing that the Lady Bird deed isn't spelled out in a specific Florida statute—it's recognized through long-standing Florida legal practice and case law, not a numbered code section. That's exactly why it needs to be drafted correctly, with attention to your homestead status and any mortgage on the property.

Revocable Living Trusts for Spring Hill Homeowners

A revocable living trust, governed by Florida's trust code in F.S. Chapter 736, is another well-established way to keep real estate out of probate. You transfer your home into the trust while you're alive, and you typically serve as your own trustee, keeping full control. You name a successor trustee who steps in when you pass away or become incapacitated, and that person can distribute or sell the property according to your instructions—without a Hernando County probate filing.

Trusts tend to make the most sense when you own property in multiple states, have a blended family, want to plan for potential incapacity, or want more detailed control over how and when beneficiaries receive assets. A Lady Bird deed is simpler and less expensive for a single Spring Hill home; a trust is often better when your estate plan needs to do more.

Joint Ownership and Tenancy by the Entireties

If you hold your Spring Hill home with a spouse as tenants by the entireties—the default for married couples who take title together in Florida—the surviving spouse automatically becomes full owner the moment the other spouse dies. No probate is required for that transfer. Property held in joint tenancy with right of survivorship with a non-spouse works similarly, though it comes with real tradeoffs: your co-owner has an immediate ownership interest now, which exposes the property to that person's creditors, divorce, or judgment liens while you're both alive.

Good to know: Homestead status, creditor protections under Article X, Section 4 of the Florida Constitution, and the homestead devise restrictions in F.S. § 732.4015 can all affect which of these tools is available or advisable for your primary residence—especially if you're married or have minor children.

2026 Changes: Bigger Thresholds for Simplified Probate

Even when a family doesn't use a deed or trust in advance, Florida has streamlined paths for smaller estates—and those paths just got more generous. Effective July 1, 2026, new legislation raised the threshold for Summary Administration so that estates with non-exempt assets valued at $150,000 or less can now qualify for this faster, less expensive probate process, rather than requiring full formal administration. The threshold for the separate, even simpler Disposition of Personal Property Without Administration procedure also increased as part of the same reforms.

These changes don't eliminate probate for real estate outright, but they mean more Spring Hill families whose loved one passed without a deed, trust, or joint title in place may still qualify for a quicker, cheaper court process than they expect.

⚠ A Word of Caution: Florida does not recognize a standalone "transfer-on-death deed" for real estate the way some other states do. If you've seen that term online, be careful—in Florida, the equivalent tool is the Lady Bird deed, and using the wrong form or an out-of-state template can create title problems for your heirs.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is a Lady Bird deed legal in Florida?
Yes. Florida courts and title practice have long recognized enhanced life estate deeds, commonly called Lady Bird deeds, as a valid way to transfer real property at death without probate, even though there is no single statute that names them.
Does Spring Hill have its own probate court?
No. Spring Hill is an unincorporated community in Hernando County, and probate cases for Spring Hill residents are filed with the Hernando County Clerk of Court in Brooksville, following the same statewide Florida probate rules.
Can I avoid probate on my homestead in Spring Hill?
Often yes, using a Lady Bird deed, a revocable trust, or survivorship ownership with a spouse, but Florida's homestead protections and restrictions on devising homestead property to certain heirs can limit your options, so your specific situation should be reviewed.
What happened to Florida's small estate threshold in 2026?
New legislation effective July 1, 2026 raised the threshold for Summary Administration to estates with non-exempt assets of $150,000 or less and increased the separate threshold for Disposition of Personal Property Without Administration, making simplified probate available to more families.
Is a trust or a Lady Bird deed better for my Spring Hill home?
A Lady Bird deed is often simpler and less costly for transferring a single home, while a revocable living trust may be better if you own property in more than one state, want incapacity planning, or need more detailed control over distributions.
Will my family still need to go to court if I do nothing?
Possibly, but not always full formal probate; Florida's simplified procedures, especially after the 2026 threshold increases, may allow smaller estates to bypass the more lengthy court process even without advance planning.

The Truestead Takeaway

Every one of these tools—Lady Bird deeds, revocable trusts, and survivorship ownership—can work well for a Spring Hill homeowner, but the right choice depends on your marital status, your homestead rights, your mortgage, and who you want to inherit the property. What I tell my clients is that the paperwork is only as good as the planning behind it, so before you record a deed or retitle your home, have a Florida attorney review your specific situation to make sure it actually accomplishes what you intend.
