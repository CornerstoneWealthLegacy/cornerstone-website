Indian River County Probate Attorney: What Vero Beach Families Need to Know

Quick Answer

If you need to open probate in Indian River County, the case is filed with the Clerk of the Circuit Court's Probate Division at the Vero Beach courthouse, and Florida law now allows more estates to qualify for the faster, cheaper summary administration process after a July 2026 increase in the value limit to $150,000. A personal representative is legally required to hire a Florida probate attorney to handle the filing, and an experienced Indian River County probate attorney can tell you quickly which path your family's estate qualifies for.

By Arthur Simpson, Esq. · FL Bar #529265 Florida Estate Planning Attorney September 3, 2026

Where Indian River County Probate Cases Are Filed

In my practice, the first question a family asks after a loved one passes is simple: where do we even start? If the person who died lived in Vero Beach, Sebastian, Fellsmere, or elsewhere in Indian River County, or owned Florida property here, the probate case is opened with the Clerk of the Circuit Court, Probate Division, at the Indian River County Courthouse in Vero Beach. Indian River County sits within Florida's Nineteenth Judicial Circuit, alongside Martin, Okeechobee, and St. Lucie counties, and while the circuit applies the same statewide Florida Probate Code, each county's probate division has its own local conventions, forms, and staff expectations. That's part of why hiring a local Indian River County probate attorney who regularly appears in the Vero Beach courthouse can make the process smoother than working with an out-of-area firm.

Under Florida law, whoever has custody of a deceased person's original will is required to deposit it with the clerk of court in the county where the decedent lived, generally within 10 days of learning of the death, even if no probate case is opened right away (F.S. § 732.901). This is a legal duty separate from actually starting probate, and it applies whether or not you plan to hire an attorney immediately.

The 2026 Changes: Bigger Estates Now Qualify for Simplified Probate

Florida made meaningful changes to its probate laws effective July 1, 2026, and they matter a great deal to Indian River County families. Florida generally offers two main paths through probate:

  • Summary administration: a faster, simpler process available when the value of the probate estate (after subtracting property that's exempt from creditors) falls under the statutory threshold, or when the decedent has been deceased more than two years.
  • Formal administration: the standard process for larger estates, requiring the court to appoint a personal representative to gather assets, pay valid debts, and distribute what remains to heirs or beneficiaries.

Before July 1, 2026, an estate generally had to be valued at $75,000 or less to use summary administration. That threshold has now increased to $150,000, meaning many mid-size estates in Indian River County that previously required full formal administration may now qualify for the shorter, less expensive summary process. Florida also raised the limit on nonexempt personal property that can pass through the simplified disposition without administration procedure, from $10,000 to $20,000, for certain small intestate estates. These changes came through legislation enacted in 2026 and represent the most significant probate reform Florida has seen in years.

What this means for you: If a loved one's estate was worth somewhere between $75,000 and $150,000, it's worth having a probate attorney re-evaluate whether summary administration is now available, potentially saving months of time and meaningful legal expense compared to formal administration.

Do You Really Need an Attorney for Indian River County Probate?

Here's what I tell Florida clients directly: if you are serving as personal representative of an estate going through formal administration, Florida law requires that you be represented by a licensed Florida attorney, with narrow exceptions for personal representatives who are also the sole interested party. This isn't a formality designed to generate legal fees. Florida probate involves strict deadlines for notifying creditors and beneficiaries, specific accounting and inventory requirements, and real personal liability exposure for a personal representative who distributes assets improperly.

Even for summary administration or disposition without administration, where the legal requirements are lighter, most families in Vero Beach and Sebastian still choose to work with a probate attorney because mistakes in these filings can delay the release of funds, complicate a home's title, or create disputes among heirs that are far more expensive to fix later than to prevent up front.

Out-of-State Decedents and Ancillary Probate in Indian River County

Indian River County, with its concentration of seasonal residents, retirees, and vacation homeowners, sees a steady volume of what's called ancillary administration under Florida Statute § 734.102. This applies when someone who lived in another state died owning real estate or other property in Indian River County. Florida law requires that property to pass through a Florida probate proceeding, even if the primary probate case is already underway in the decedent's home state. If you're an out-of-state family member dealing with a parent's or relative's Vero Beach condo, beach house, or investment property after their death, an ancillary probate case here is almost always necessary before that property can be sold or transferred.

What the Digital Filing Process Looks Like in 2026

The Indian River County probate division, like courts throughout Florida, has continued modernizing its filing procedures. As of 2026, most probate documents in Indian River County are filed and signed electronically, with the court relying on secure digital signature standards for personal representatives, attorneys, and interested parties. This has generally sped up the process for families, particularly those who don't live locally and previously had to mail original signed documents back and forth. That said, the original will itself still must be physically deposited with the clerk, since Florida law requires the actual paper document, not a copy, to be lodged with the court.

⚠ A note on deadlines: Florida probate involves firm statutory deadlines, including the window for creditors to file claims against an estate. Missing these deadlines, whether you're the personal representative or a creditor, can permanently affect your rights. Because these timelines are specific to each case, they should be confirmed with a Florida probate attorney reviewing your actual filing.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does probate cost in Indian River County?
Probate costs depend heavily on which process applies (summary versus formal administration), the size and complexity of the estate, and whether disputes arise among heirs. An Indian River County probate attorney can typically give you a clearer estimate after reviewing the estate's assets and debts.
How long does probate take in Vero Beach or Sebastian?
Summary administration can sometimes be completed in a matter of weeks to a few months, while formal administration commonly takes longer, particularly if there are creditor claims, tax matters, or disputes to resolve. Timing varies case by case.
What if my family member died without a will?
Dying without a will means Florida's intestacy laws under the Florida Probate Code determine who inherits, based on the decedent's surviving family relationships. Probate is still generally required to transfer the property, though small estates may qualify for a simplified process.
Does every estate have to go through probate in Florida?
Not necessarily. Certain assets, like jointly owned property with rights of survivorship, accounts with named beneficiaries, and property held in a properly funded trust, generally pass outside of probate. Whether a specific estate needs probate depends on how the decedent's assets were titled.
Can I handle probate myself without a lawyer in Indian River County?
If the estate qualifies for summary administration or disposition without administration and there is only one interested party, self-representation may be possible in limited circumstances, but formal administration generally requires the personal representative to be represented by a Florida attorney.
What happened to Florida's summary administration limit in 2026?
Effective July 1, 2026, Florida increased the value threshold for summary administration eligibility from $75,000 to $150,000, allowing more mid-size estates to use the simplified process instead of full formal administration.

The Truestead Takeaway

Probate in Indian River County follows Florida's statewide Probate Code, but local courthouse procedures, digital filing standards, and the newly expanded 2026 summary administration threshold all affect how smoothly a family's case moves through the Vero Beach probate division. If you're facing probate for a loved one's estate, whether it's a modest Sebastian bank account or a larger Vero Beach property, the sensible next step is to have a Florida probate attorney review the estate's actual assets and value to confirm which process applies and what deadlines govern your case.
