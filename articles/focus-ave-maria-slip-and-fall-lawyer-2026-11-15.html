Ave Maria Slip and Fall Lawyer: What Florida Law Says About Your Claim

Quick Answer

If you were hurt in a slip and fall in Ave Maria, Florida's statewide premises liability law applies, not a special local rule. You generally have two years from the date of injury to file suit, and you must show the property owner knew or should have known about the hazard.

By Arthur Simpson, Esq. · FL Bar #529265 Florida Personal Injury Attorney November 15, 2026

Does Ave Maria Have Its Own Slip and Fall Law?

In my practice, one of the first things I clear up for clients searching for an Ave Maria slip and fall lawyer is this: Ave Maria does not have its own premises liability law. Ave Maria is a planned community in Collier County, Southwest Florida, and every slip and fall there, whether it happens at a shop near the Town Center, a church-related facility, a grocery store, a restaurant, or a private walkway, is governed by the same statewide framework that applies anywhere else in Florida.

That means the legal questions are consistent no matter where in Ave Maria the fall happened. What changes from case to case is the evidence: how long the hazard was there, whether the business had a cleaning or inspection schedule, what the surveillance footage shows, and who saw what. The law stays the same. The facts are what your case will turn on.

The Core Statute: Florida's Transitory Substance Law

Most slip and fall claims against a business in Florida, including in Ave Maria, are governed by Florida Statute § 768.0755, which applies specifically to falls caused by a transitory foreign substance, think spilled liquid, dropped produce, tracked-in water, or debris on a walking surface in a business establishment.

Under this statute, it is not enough to show that a hazard existed and you fell. You must prove the business had actual or constructive knowledge of the dangerous condition and failed to remedy it. Constructive knowledge, meaning the business should have known even without being directly told, can be shown through circumstantial evidence that:

  • The condition existed long enough that ordinary care should have caught it, or
  • The condition happened with enough regularity that it was foreseeable (for example, a produce section that regularly has dropped items on the floor).

This is a meaningfully higher burden than simply proving a hazard existed. Surveillance video, maintenance logs, employee schedules, and incident reports often become the center of these cases.

What You Have to Prove in a Florida Premises Liability Case

Beyond the transitory substance statute, every Florida slip and fall claim rests on the same four building blocks of a negligence case:

  • Duty: The property owner or business owed you a duty of care appropriate to your status as a visitor (customer, guest, invitee, etc.).
  • Breach: The owner failed to meet that duty, whether through inadequate inspection, failure to warn, or failure to fix a known hazard.
  • Causation: The hazardous condition actually caused your fall and your injuries, not some unrelated cause.
  • Damages: You suffered real, provable harm, medical bills, lost wages, pain and suffering, and so on.

Missing any one of these elements can sink an otherwise sympathetic case. That is why documentation close to the time of the fall, photos of the hazard, witness names, and an incident report, matters so much.

How Long Do You Have to File? The Two-Year Deadline

⚠ Deadline Alert Florida's statute of limitations for negligence claims, including slip and fall cases, was shortened by the 2023 tort reform law (HB 837). For causes of action accruing after March 24, 2023, the deadline under Florida Statute § 95.11 is generally two years from the date of injury, down from the previous four-year window. If your fall happened before that date, a different deadline may apply. Because this area of law changed recently and the exact deadline depends on when your injury occurred, you should confirm your specific filing deadline with a Florida attorney rather than assume.

Missing a filing deadline typically means losing the right to bring the claim at all, regardless of how strong the underlying facts are. This is one of the most important reasons not to wait before speaking with someone who can calculate your specific deadline.

What If You Were Partly at Fault?

Florida uses a modified comparative negligence standard under Florida Statute § 768.81. If you were partially responsible for your own fall, say you were looking at your phone or ignored a visible warning sign, your compensation can be reduced by your percentage of fault. Under the modified system, a plaintiff found more than 51% at fault is generally barred from recovery entirely.

This makes the facts surrounding the fall, lighting, signage, visibility of the hazard, whether warning cones were out, especially important. A property owner's insurance company will often look for any angle to shift blame onto the injured person, which is part of why a documented, well-supported claim matters.

A Note on Apartment and Condo Properties If your fall happened at a multifamily residential property, such as an apartment complex, Florida law includes provisions (F.S. § 768.0706) that can create a presumption against liability for owners who implement certain security measures, and more recent legislative changes have added exceptions tied to prior crime reports at the property. These rules are detailed and fact-specific, and a Florida attorney can help determine how they apply to your situation.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need a lawyer who is based in Ave Maria specifically?
No. Because Florida premises liability law is statewide, you do not need a lawyer physically located in Ave Maria. What matters more is experience with Florida slip and fall claims, familiarity with Collier County courts, and the ability to investigate and document your specific fall.
What should I do immediately after a slip and fall in Ave Maria?
Where possible, photograph the hazard, get names and contact information for witnesses, report the incident to the property or business, and seek medical attention promptly. Early documentation often becomes critical evidence later, since hazards like spills get cleaned up quickly.
How long do I have to file a slip and fall lawsuit in Florida?
For injuries occurring after March 24, 2023, Florida's statute of limitations is generally two years from the date of the fall under F.S. § 95.11. Because deadlines depend on exact dates and circumstances, you should confirm your specific deadline with a Florida attorney.
What if the store says they didn't know about the spill?
Under F.S. § 768.0755, you can still potentially prove the business should have known through circumstantial evidence, such as showing the hazard existed long enough that reasonable inspection would have caught it, or that similar hazards happened regularly at that location.
Can I still recover damages if I was partly at fault for the fall?
Yes, Florida's modified comparative negligence rule under F.S. § 768.81 allows recovery even if you share some fault, but your compensation is reduced by your percentage of responsibility, and recovery is barred if you are found more than 51% at fault.
Does it matter if the fall happened on private property versus a business?
Yes, the specific duties owed can differ depending on whether the property is a business open to the public, private residential property, or government-owned property (which involves separate sovereign immunity rules under F.S. § 768.28). An attorney can clarify which framework applies to your situation.

The Truestead Takeaway

A slip and fall in Ave Maria is governed by the same Florida premises liability law that applies throughout the state, centered on Florida Statute § 768.0755 and a shortened two-year filing deadline following the 2023 tort reform. The strength of these claims almost always comes down to evidence: how long the hazard existed, what the business knew, and how clearly the fall and injury can be documented. If you have been hurt in a fall in Ave Maria or anywhere in Collier County, the sensible next step is to gather what documentation you can and have your specific situation reviewed by a Florida attorney before any filing deadline approaches.
