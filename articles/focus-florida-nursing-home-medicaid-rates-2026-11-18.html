Florida Nursing Home Medicaid Rates: What Families Need to Know in 2026

Quick Answer

As of October 1, 2026, Florida's average Medicaid nursing home reimbursement rate is about $302.92 per resident day, a slight decrease from $307.37 in 2025, with South Florida facilities generally paid more per day than facilities in North Florida.

By Arthur Simpson, Esq. · FL Bar #529265 Florida Elder Law Attorney November 18, 2026

What Are Florida's Current Nursing Home Medicaid Rates?

I get some version of this question from almost every family I sit down with who is navigating a parent's or spouse's move into a nursing facility: what does Medicaid actually pay the nursing home, and does that affect my loved one's care? The honest, current answer is this: effective October 1, 2026, Florida's Agency for Health Care Administration (AHCA) set the statewide average Medicaid nursing facility rate at approximately $302.92 per resident day. That is down from an average of $307.37 per day in 2025, a decrease of roughly $4.45 per day, or about 1.4 percent.

These are not flat, one-size-fits-all numbers. Florida's Medicaid nursing home reimbursement system sets an individualized rate for each facility based on its costs, location, and quality performance, and then AHCA reports statewide and regional averages so the public, providers, and legislators can track trends. For 2026, the picture is a modest decline after several years of rate increases, which matters for how facilities budget for staffing and care.

Why Do Rates Vary by Region and Facility?

One of the most common misunderstandings I clear up for families is the assumption that every Florida nursing home gets paid the same Medicaid rate per resident. It does not work that way. Florida groups facilities into geographic regions, and the 2026 data shows a real divide:

  • South Florida facilities are averaging about $313.45 per resident day.
  • North Florida facilities are averaging about $300.26 per resident day.

South Florida also saw the steeper year-over-year decline, dropping about 2.2 percent compared to a 1.3 percent decline in the North region. These regional differences generally reflect variations in local labor costs, overhead, and the cost reports facilities submit to the state, since Florida's system is built around each facility's own audited costs rather than a single statewide number.

How Does Florida Actually Set These Rates?

Florida nursing homes are reimbursed under a Prospective Payment System (PPS), a methodology the state adopted effective October 1, 2018, replacing the older cost-based reimbursement model. Under Florida Statute § 409.908, reimbursement to nursing homes licensed under part II of chapter 400, and to certain state-operated intermediate care facilities, must be made prospectively. In plain terms, this means the state sets a facility's rate in advance for a rate period, rather than simply reimbursing whatever costs the facility reports after the fact.

The rate for each facility is built from several components, including:

  • Direct care costs (nursing staff and resident care)
  • Indirect care and operating costs
  • A fair rental value component for the physical facility
  • Medicaid assessment costs
  • A quality incentive component tied to performance

On that last point, Florida ties part of every facility's reimbursement to measurable quality performance. For 2026, the state set the quality incentive value at approximately $1.454 per quality point earned, a lower dollar value per point than the prior year. That means a facility can earn the same quality score as last year and still see its quality-related reimbursement shrink slightly, which is part of what's driving the overall 2026 rate dip. Separately, Florida law requires AHCA to rebase nursing home prospective payment rates every fourth year to reflect updated costs from each facility's most recently audited cost report, which periodically resets the baseline used in these calculations.

What Does This Mean for My Family Member's Care?

This is usually the real question underneath 'what are the rates,' and I want to answer it honestly rather than alarm anyone. A statewide average rate decrease of roughly 1.4 percent is a reimbursement and budgeting matter between the state and the facility. It does not change a Medicaid-eligible resident's legal right to receive medically necessary care, and it does not change the resident's own financial obligations under Florida's Medicaid rules.

What families pay out of pocket: Florida Medicaid nursing home residents are generally required to contribute most of their own income toward the cost of care, but Florida allows residents to keep a personal needs allowance, currently $160 per month, which is one of the highest such allowances in the country. This money belongs to the resident for personal items, not facility costs.

What rate changes can affect, over time, is a facility's financial margin, staffing decisions, and willingness to accept new Medicaid residents or expand Medicaid-certified beds. Families comparing facilities should ask directly about current staffing levels and quality scores rather than relying solely on statewide reimbursement figures, since an individual facility's rate and performance can differ meaningfully from the state average.

How Do Medicaid Rates Relate to Nursing Home Eligibility?

It's worth separating two different things that often get conflated: the rate Medicaid pays the facility, and the eligibility rules that determine whether your family member qualifies for Medicaid to cover nursing home care in the first place. For 2026, a single applicant generally may have no more than $2,000 in countable assets and no more than $2,982 in gross monthly income to qualify for nursing home or assisted living Medicaid in Florida, though income above that threshold can sometimes still work through a Qualified Income Trust (often called a Miller Trust). These income and asset figures are adjusted periodically and should always be verified against the current year's published limits before relying on them for planning.

⚠ A Note on Verifying Numbers: Medicaid income limits, asset limits, and reimbursement rates are updated on their own schedules and can shift from year to year. Before making any decision based on a specific dollar figure, confirm the current number with AHCA, the Department of Children and Families, or a Florida elder law attorney.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the average Florida nursing home Medicaid rate right now?
As of October 1, 2026, the statewide average is approximately $302.92 per resident day, down from $307.37 per day in 2025.
Do all Florida nursing homes get paid the same Medicaid rate?
No. Each facility has its own rate based on its reported costs, location, and quality performance, which is why South Florida facilities currently average around $313.45 per day while North Florida facilities average around $300.26 per day.
Why did Florida's nursing home Medicaid rates go down in 2026?
The decrease appears driven primarily by a lower dollar value assigned to each quality incentive point this year and budget neutrality adjustments within the prospective payment system, rather than any change to the underlying statute.
Does a lower Medicaid reimbursement rate mean my family member will get worse care?
Not automatically. The rate is a reimbursement figure paid to the facility; it does not change a resident's legal right to medically necessary care. Families should still evaluate individual facilities on staffing levels and quality scores.
How much income can a nursing home resident keep for personal use in Florida?
Florida allows a personal needs allowance of $160 per month for Medicaid nursing home residents, among the highest such allowances in the country, though this figure should be confirmed as current before relying on it.
Where does Florida's authority to set these rates come from?
Florida Statute § 409.908 requires that nursing home Medicaid reimbursement be made prospectively, and the state has operated under a Prospective Payment System since October 1, 2018, with rebasing required at least every four years.

The Truestead Takeaway

The headline number, Florida's average Medicaid nursing home rate of about $302.92 per resident day as of October 2026, is genuinely useful context for understanding how the state's long-term care system is funded, but it is not the number that determines whether your loved one qualifies for coverage or what they'll pay out of pocket. Eligibility rules, income limits, and the specific facility's own rate and quality record matter far more to your family's actual situation. If you're weighing nursing home Medicaid for yourself or a family member, the sensible next step is a review of the current income and asset limits alongside an honest look at the facility's quality data, ideally with a Florida elder law attorney who can walk through your specific numbers and timeline.
