Nursing Home Medicaid in Hillsborough County: Where to Apply and What to Expect

Quick Answer

In Hillsborough County, the financial side of a nursing home Medicaid application goes through the Department of Children and Families and its online ACCESS system, while the medical side goes through CARES, a Department of Elder Affairs unit that assesses level of care. The Aging and Disability Resource Center and the Elder Helpline are the best local starting point if you are not yet sure which path applies.

By Arthur Simpson, Esq. · FL Bar #529265 Florida Elder Law Attorney October 6, 2026

The Two Decisions Every Hillsborough County Family Faces

Ramon is 80 and lives in Tampa, where his family has run a small restaurant for years. When a fall sent him to the hospital and then into a rehab stay, his daughter realized nobody in the family had ever dealt with the Department of Children and Families, the CARES program, or a nursing home business office. Ramon is a composite example, not a Truestead client, but his situation mirrors what we see constantly in Hillsborough, Brandon, Plant City, and Riverview.

Every Florida nursing home Medicaid case, no matter the county, actually involves two separate decisions made by two separate agencies:

  • The financial decision is made by the Department of Children and Families (DCF) through its statewide ACCESS system. DCF looks at income, assets, and whether the spend-down and look-back rules have been satisfied. There is no county DCF office to visit in person for this; the application is filed online through the ACCESS portal, and DCF schedules the required interview by phone.
  • The medical decision is made by CARES, short for Comprehensive Assessment and Review for Long-Term Care Services, a program run by the Department of Elder Affairs. A CARES nurse or assessor determines whether the applicant actually needs a nursing facility level of care, which is a separate question from whether they qualify financially.

For a Hillsborough County family, both pieces have to come together before Medicaid pays the nursing home bill. A strong financial application does nothing without a CARES approval, and a CARES approval does nothing without DCF signing off on the numbers.

Local tip: The CARES office that serves Hillsborough County assessments is part of the Department of Elder Affairs network and works closely with hospitals and rehab facilities in the Tampa Bay area when a patient is already in a skilled nursing stay. If your parent is currently in rehab, ask the hospital discharge planner or the facility's social worker whether a CARES referral has already been started; often it has.

The Aging and Disability Resource Center: The Front Door for Home Care

Not every family is ready for a nursing home. Many Hillsborough, Brandon, and Riverview families start by trying to keep a parent at home longer, which is where the Aging and Disability Resource Center (ADRC) comes in. The ADRC that serves Hillsborough County also covers several neighboring counties and operates as the single front door for home and community-based waiver services, caregiver support, and general information about long-term care options.

The statewide Elder Helpline connects callers to their local ADRC and is the easiest first call for any family unsure where to begin. Through the ADRC, families can:

  • Ask about the home and community-based services waiver, which helps pay for in-home care as an alternative to a nursing facility, understanding that this waiver has a waiting list in Florida and is not guaranteed on request
  • Get a general orientation to long-term care options before deciding whether nursing home placement is the right path
  • Be connected to local caregiver support programs, meal programs, and other aging services

For a family like Ramon's, where everyone is stretched thin running the restaurant, the ADRC and Elder Helpline are a good place to ask basic questions even before deciding that a nursing home is necessary. If it becomes clear that skilled nursing is the right level of care, the ADRC can help point the family toward the CARES and DCF processes described above.

What Nursing Home Costs Look Like in the Tampa Bay Area

Private-pay nursing home rates vary significantly from facility to facility, and a reliable, current figure specific to Hillsborough County was not available at the time this article was researched. What families should understand in plain terms is the structure, not a single number:

  • Private-pay rates are what a nursing facility charges a resident (or their family) who is paying out of pocket, and these rates are set by the facility itself.
  • The Medicaid rate is what the state actually pays the facility once a resident is approved, and it is set by the state, not negotiated by the family.
  • The gap between those two numbers is often substantial, which is exactly why families pursue Medicaid planning well before a crisis, rather than paying privately until the money runs out.

Because facility rates change and vary by location and room type, we encourage Tampa area families to ask any specific facility for its current private-pay rate directly rather than relying on a figure from an article. Truestead's general eligibility guide covers how income and asset limits work statewide; this piece is focused on the Hillsborough County process itself.

Finding a Medicaid-Certified Bed and the Medicaid Pending Conversation

Not every nursing facility accepts Medicaid, and not every bed within a facility that does accept Medicaid is a certified Medicaid bed. The Agency for Health Care Administration (AHCA) maintains the state's official facility locator, which families can use to identify which nursing facilities in Hillsborough County are Medicaid-certified, without needing to rely on any particular facility's marketing claims. We do not name specific facilities here because that list changes and should be checked directly through the state's tool at the time a family is actually searching.

A few terms worth understanding before that first facility tour or call:

  • A certified bed means the facility has state approval to accept Medicaid payment for that bed, which is different from simply accepting private-pay residents.
  • Skilled rehab days (often following a hospital stay) are typically covered differently, often through Medicare for a limited period, and are distinct from long-term custodial care, which is what Medicaid is generally designed to cover once rehab benefits run out.
  • Medicaid pending is a status many Hillsborough facilities' business offices are familiar with: it means the family has applied but DCF has not yet issued a decision. Many facilities will admit a resident on a Medicaid pending basis, but the family should ask the business office directly how that facility handles the gap period, since practices vary.

This is exactly the conversation Ramon's daughter had to learn to have. She had never spoken with a nursing home business office before, and she did not know that asking directly about Medicaid pending status, and getting the answer in writing where possible, would save her family confusion later.

Three Local Wrinkles Hillsborough Families Should Know

Every county has its own texture, and Hillsborough, Brandon, Plant City, and Riverview are no exception. Three patterns come up often:

  • Hospital-to-rehab-to-long-term-care pattern. Many Hillsborough families, like Ramon's, enter the nursing home Medicaid system through a hospital stay that turns into a rehab stay that turns into a conversation about whether a parent can safely go home. Recognizing early that rehab coverage and long-term Medicaid coverage are two different things helps families plan ahead rather than being surprised when rehab benefits end.
  • Small business owners and family-run operations. Families who own a business, like Ramon's restaurant, often worry that the business itself will be at risk during the Medicaid asset evaluation. How a business interest is treated depends heavily on the specific facts: how it is titled, whether it is income-producing, and how it fits within the countable asset rules. This is precisely the kind of situation where an elder law attorney's involvement, rather than a general guide, matters most.
  • Spanish-speaking families. Tampa's large Spanish-speaking community means many families navigating DCF's ACCESS system and CARES assessments are doing so while also managing a language barrier. Both DCF and the ADRC network generally have language assistance available, and families should ask directly about it rather than assuming it is unavailable.

A Realistic Timeline From First Call to Approval

Families often ask how long this takes from the moment they realize Medicaid may be necessary to the moment coverage actually begins. A general shape looks like this for a Hillsborough County applicant:

  • An initial call to the Elder Helpline or ADRC, or a referral from a hospital or rehab social worker, starts the orientation process.
  • A CARES assessment is scheduled to determine the medical level of care, which can happen while the patient is still in a hospital or rehab setting.
  • The financial application is filed through DCF's ACCESS system, followed by a phone interview scheduled by DCF.
  • Once DCF has everything it needs, federal rules require a decision within a set number of days, though that clock depends on complete documentation being submitted promptly, including the five years of financial records DCF typically requests.
⚠ Timing depends on paperwork. The single biggest factor in how long a Hillsborough County application takes is how quickly a family can produce complete, organized financial documentation. Incomplete records are the most common cause of delay, not agency slowness.

For Ramon's family, the realistic path involved the hospital's social worker starting the CARES referral during his rehab stay, the ADRC answering early questions about whether home care might work instead, and ultimately a decision that skilled nursing was the right level of care given his needs. The financial side, involving the restaurant and the family's assets, was exactly where they needed an elder law attorney's guidance rather than guesswork.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need to visit a DCF office in Hillsborough County to apply?
No. Florida nursing home Medicaid applications are filed online through DCF's statewide ACCESS system, and DCF conducts the required interview by phone rather than in person.
What does CARES actually decide?
CARES, a Department of Elder Affairs program, determines whether an applicant medically needs a nursing facility level of care. This is separate from and in addition to the financial eligibility decision made by DCF.
Is the home care waiver the same as nursing home Medicaid?
No. The home and community-based services waiver, accessed through the Aging and Disability Resource Center, helps pay for care at home and has its own waiting list; nursing home Medicaid is a different program with its own CARES and DCF process.
How do I know if a nursing facility near Tampa accepts Medicaid?
The Agency for Health Care Administration maintains an official facility locator that shows which Florida nursing facilities are Medicaid-certified; families should check this directly rather than relying only on a facility's own claims.
What does Medicaid pending mean at a nursing home business office?
It means the family has submitted the Medicaid application but DCF has not yet issued a decision. Many facilities will admit on this basis, but how each facility handles that waiting period varies, so it is worth asking the business office directly.
Should a Hillsborough family with a small business talk to an elder law attorney before applying?
Generally yes. Business interests can be treated differently depending on how they are structured and titled, and that is exactly the kind of fact-specific question a Florida elder law attorney should review before an application is filed.

The Truestead Takeaway

Ramon's family learned, as most Hillsborough County families do, that getting a parent onto nursing home Medicaid is really two parallel processes: a medical determination through CARES and a financial determination through DCF's ACCESS system, with the Aging and Disability Resource Center and Elder Helpline available as an early guide if home care might work instead. The facts that make every family's situation different, like Ramon's restaurant, are exactly why a general guide can only take you so far. If your family is approaching this decision in Tampa, Brandon, Plant City, or Riverview, the sensible next step is to have your specific financial picture and timeline reviewed by a Florida elder law attorney, since figures, facility counts, and program rules referenced here were accurate as of this writing but do change.
