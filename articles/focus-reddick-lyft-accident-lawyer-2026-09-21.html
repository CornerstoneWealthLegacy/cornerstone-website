Reddick Lyft Accident Lawyer: What Marion County Riders and Drivers Need to Know

Quick Answer

If you were hurt in a Lyft accident in or around Reddick, Florida, your claim will likely be governed by Florida Statute 627.748, which sets different insurance coverage amounts depending on what the driver was doing at the time of the crash, and you generally have two years from the date of the accident to file a lawsuit.

By Arthur Simpson, Esq. · FL Bar #529265 Florida Personal Injury Attorney September 21, 2026

Why 'Reddick Lyft Accident Lawyer' Doesn't Point to a Special Law

When I see someone search for a "Reddick Lyft accident lawyer," I understand exactly what they're after: help finding an attorney who knows how Lyft accident claims work in their part of Florida. Reddick is a small community in Marion County, and while there's no separate body of law just for accidents that happen there, the same statewide rules apply whether the crash happened on Highway 441 near Reddick or anywhere else in Florida.

What matters is not the town, it's the facts: what the Lyft driver was doing on the app at the moment of the crash, who was at fault, and what insurance coverage applies. Those are the questions a Florida rideshare accident lawyer needs to answer, and they're the same questions I walk clients through no matter which county they're in.

Florida's Three-Phase Rideshare Insurance Framework

Florida Statute 627.748 governs how insurance works for Transportation Network Companies like Lyft and Uber. The coverage available to you after a crash depends heavily on which of three "phases" the Lyft driver was in when the accident happened:

  • App off: The driver isn't logged into the Lyft app. Their personal auto insurance applies, just as it would for any other driver.
  • App on, waiting for a ride request: Once the driver is logged in and available, Florida law requires minimum liability coverage of $50,000 per person for bodily injury, $100,000 per accident for total bodily injury, and $25,000 for property damage, along with PIP and uninsured/underinsured motorist coverage.
  • Ride accepted through drop-off: From the moment the driver accepts a ride request until the passenger is dropped off, Lyft's own policy typically provides up to $1,000,000 in liability coverage, plus uninsured and underinsured motorist protection. If you were a passenger injured during this phase, this is usually the policy your claim will draw from.

Figuring out which phase applies, and documenting it, is often the single most important step in a rideshare injury claim. Ride history data, app timestamps, and driver statements all matter here.

Can You Sue Lyft Directly, or Just the Driver?

In Florida, Lyft drivers are generally treated as independent contractors rather than employees. That classification matters because it limits Lyft's direct liability for a driver's negligence in many situations. Recent Florida court decisions have continued to give rideshare companies fairly broad protection from direct liability claims, particularly in property damage cases, which means that in most cases your claim will be built around the driver's available insurance coverage rather than a lawsuit against Lyft itself as a company.

That doesn't mean you're without options. It means a knowledgeable Florida attorney needs to correctly identify which insurance policy (the driver's personal policy, or Lyft's contingent or primary policy) actually applies to your specific accident, and pursue that coverage properly.

What I tell clients: Don't assume a big company means a big, easy payout. Rideshare claims often involve more insurance layers, not more direct liability, and untangling those layers is exactly where an experienced Florida rideshare accident lawyer earns their keep.

Florida's PIP Requirement and the 14-Day Rule

Florida is a no-fault state, which means that regardless of who caused the accident, your own Personal Injury Protection (PIP) coverage is typically the first source of payment for initial medical bills, under Florida Statute 627.736. A critical detail many accident victims miss: you generally must seek medical treatment within 14 days of the crash to preserve your PIP eligibility. Waiting longer than that, even if your injuries are real, can jeopardize this coverage.

If your injuries are serious enough to exceed your PIP limits or meet Florida's threshold for a lawsuit against the at-fault party, that's when the rideshare driver's (or Lyft's) liability coverage may come into play.

Comparative Negligence and Your Filing Deadline

Florida law changed significantly in 2023, and these changes still apply to rideshare accident claims today. Two rules matter most:

  • Modified comparative negligence (F.S. 768.81): If you're found partially at fault for the accident, your compensation is reduced by your percentage of fault. If you're found to be 51% or more at fault, Florida law bars you from recovering any damages at all.
  • Statute of limitations: Florida's negligence statute of limitations was reduced from four years to two years for causes of action accruing after the 2023 tort reform took effect. That generally gives injury victims two years from the date of the accident to file a lawsuit.
⚠ Don't wait to confirm your deadline. Statute of limitations rules can be affected by factors specific to your case, including when the injury was discovered and who the defendant is. Because the timeline was shortened by the 2023 tort reform, you should not rely on older information you may have read online. Confirm your specific deadline with a Florida attorney as soon as possible after an accident.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much time do I have to file a Lyft accident claim in Florida?
Florida's general statute of limitations for negligence claims is now two years from the date of the accident, following the 2023 tort reform. Because deadlines can vary by circumstance, you should confirm your specific filing window with a Florida attorney promptly.
Will my claim be covered by Lyft's $1 million policy?
That depends on what the driver was doing at the time of the crash. Lyft's higher coverage generally applies once a driver has accepted a ride request and is en route to or transporting a passenger, not simply because the app was open.
Can I still recover money if I was partly at fault for the accident?
Yes, but your recovery will be reduced by your percentage of fault under Florida's modified comparative negligence rule, and if you're found 51% or more at fault, you cannot recover damages at all.
Do I have to use my own insurance first after a Lyft accident?
In most cases, yes. Florida's no-fault system requires your own Personal Injury Protection (PIP) coverage to pay initial medical expenses regardless of fault, and you generally must seek treatment within 14 days of the accident to preserve that coverage.
Is there a special court case or law specifically about Reddick and Lyft?
No. Reddick is simply a Marion County community, and no special statute or court decision applies uniquely to accidents there. Statewide Florida rideshare and personal injury law applies the same way it would anywhere else in Florida.
Can I sue Lyft as a company instead of just the driver?
Generally, no, because Lyft drivers are classified as independent contractors rather than employees, and recent Florida court decisions have reinforced fairly broad protections for rideshare companies from direct liability. Most claims are pursued against the driver's applicable insurance coverage instead.

The Truestead Takeaway

If you or a loved one was hurt in a Lyft accident near Reddick or anywhere else in Marion County, the outcome of your claim will hinge on a few specific facts: what phase the driver was in under Florida's three-tier insurance framework, how fault gets allocated, and whether you acted within Florida's current filing deadlines. These are fact-specific, statute-driven questions, not something to guess at from a search result. The sensible next step is to have a Florida attorney review your accident details, insurance information, and medical treatment timeline so nothing falls through the cracks.
