What a Florida Medicaid Attorney Actually Does

In my practice, I explain to clients that a Medicaid attorney's job isn't simply filling out an application. It's structuring a family's finances, in advance or in a crisis, so that a loved one qualifies for Florida's Statewide Medicaid Managed Care Long-Term Care program (SMMC-LTC) while protecting as much of the family's resources as the law allows. Depending on the family's situation, that can involve:

  • A Qualified Income Trust (sometimes called a Miller Trust) to legally redirect income that exceeds Florida's strict income cap
  • An irrevocable Medicaid asset protection trust to shelter assets for a healthy spouse or future heirs
  • A Lady Bird deed to keep the homestead out of probate and reduce exposure to Florida's estate recovery program
  • A durable power of attorney with specific Medicaid planning authority, so a trusted family member can act
  • A personal services contract to properly compensate a family member who has been providing caregiving

Because Florida uses an income cap system, an applicant who earns even a small amount over the monthly limit can be denied outright unless a Qualified Income Trust is properly established. This is one of the most common and most avoidable mistakes I see families make when they try to navigate Medicaid applications without legal guidance.

2026 Florida Medicaid Limits Osprey Families Should Know

Florida updates its Medicaid financial thresholds every January, and the 2026 figures matter directly to anyone in Osprey currently planning for a parent's or spouse's long-term care:

  • The monthly income limit for a single Medicaid long-term care applicant is $2,982 as of January 1, 2026
  • The asset limit for a single applicant remains $2,000 in countable assets
  • The home equity limit increased to $752,000 as of January 1, 2026
  • When one spouse applies and the other remains at home, the healthy community spouse can retain up to $162,660 in assets under the Community Spouse Resource Allowance (CSRA)

Certain assets, including the primary home (within equity limits), one vehicle, personal belongings, and prepaid burial arrangements, are generally exempt and don't count against the $2,000 asset limit. Understanding exactly what counts, and what doesn't, is often the difference between qualifying this month or waiting several more months while a family burns through savings paying privately for care.

Why timing matters: Florida applies a five-year lookback period to asset transfers made before a Medicaid application. Gifts or transfers made for less than fair value within that window can trigger a penalty period of ineligibility, which is why planning ahead of a crisis, whenever possible, gives families far more options than planning after a hospitalization.

Estate Recovery: Why Protecting the Osprey Family Home Matters

One of the most common concerns I hear from Osprey clients is simple: will Medicaid take my parents' house after they pass away? Florida's Medicaid program includes a mandatory estate recovery process that can reach a recipient's probate estate, including the home, after death, to recoup what Medicaid paid for long-term care.

This is exactly why tools like the Lady Bird deed and certain trust structures are so central to Florida Medicaid planning. Properly used, these tools can help keep the homestead out of the probate estate, which in turn can take it outside the reach of estate recovery. Florida's homestead protections under the Florida Constitution add another layer of complexity, and how homestead property is titled, devised, and transferred has real consequences for Medicaid eligibility and estate recovery alike. This is not a do-it-yourself area; the interaction between homestead law, trust law, and Medicaid rules is exactly the kind of issue a board certified elder law attorney is trained to navigate.

How to Choose the Right Medicaid Attorney Near Osprey

When Osprey families ask me how to evaluate a Medicaid attorney, I give the same answer every time: look for someone who concentrates specifically in Florida elder law and Medicaid planning, not an attorney who dabbles in it alongside unrelated practice areas. The Florida Bar recognizes Board Certified Elder Law Attorneys, who practice specifically in probate, estate planning, public benefits planning, and guardianship, and who must demonstrate substantial experience and pass a specialty exam to earn that certification.

Ask any prospective attorney how many Medicaid applications they've prepared, whether they regularly draft Qualified Income Trusts and Medicaid asset protection trusts, and how they approach the five-year lookback period. A firm serving Osprey through Sarasota County should be comfortable meeting with families locally, reviewing bank and brokerage statements for the lookback period, and coordinating directly with the Department of Children and Families on the application itself.

⚠ A word of caution: Online "Medicaid planning" templates and generic trust kits are not a substitute for an attorney's review. Florida Medicaid rules change annually, and a document that worked for a friend's situation can easily disqualify a different applicant or trigger an unnecessary penalty period.
