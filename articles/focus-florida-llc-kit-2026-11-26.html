Florida LLC Kit: What's Really Inside, and What the Law Requires

Quick Answer

A "Florida LLC kit" is just a bundle of the documents you need to form and run a Florida LLC: Articles of Organization, an operating agreement, EIN paperwork, and a Sunbiz filing guide. Florida law itself only requires the Articles of Organization filed under Chapter 605, Florida Statutes, but a complete kit also protects you after you die or sell the business, which is where most self-filed LLCs fall short.

By Arthur Simpson, Esq. · FL Bar #529265 Florida Estate Planning Attorney November 26, 2026

What Is a "Florida LLC Kit," Exactly?

In my practice, when a client says they want a "Florida LLC kit," they almost always mean one of two things: a do-it-yourself bundle of forms they can file themselves on Sunbiz, or a more complete, attorney-built package that includes the formation documents plus the internal paperwork a bank, a buyer, or a probate court will eventually ask to see. There is no statute called the "LLC kit" statute. It is a marketing term. But the documents inside a good one map directly onto real requirements and real risks under Florida law.

A genuinely useful Florida LLC kit typically includes:

  • Articles of Organization that satisfy Fla. Stat. § 605.0201
  • A signed registered agent acceptance
  • An Operating Agreement (not legally required, but you will wish you had one)
  • An EIN application for the IRS
  • A Sunbiz filing and annual report guide
  • Membership certificates or an assignment of membership interest, especially if the LLC will be owned by a trust

Commercial kits generally run in the range of a modest self-guided fee up to several hundred dollars for an attorney-prepared package, in addition to the state's own filing fees. What you are paying for, beyond convenience, is whether the documents actually hold up when it matters: at a bank, in a lawsuit, or in a probate court after an owner dies.

What Florida Law Actually Requires to Form an LLC

Florida LLCs are governed by the Florida Revised Limited Liability Company Act, Chapter 605, Florida Statutes. To legally form the entity, Florida only requires that you file Articles of Organization with the Florida Department of State under Fla. Stat. § 605.0201. The Articles must include:

  • The LLC's name, which must end in "Limited Liability Company," "LLC," or "L.L.C." and must be distinguishable from names already on file
  • The street and mailing address of the LLC's principal office
  • The name and Florida street address of the registered agent, who must sign a written acceptance of that role

The current state filing cost is $125, made up of a $100 fee to file the Articles of Organization and a $25 fee for the registered agent designation. That is the entire legal minimum. Everything else, including the operating agreement, is a matter of good practice rather than statutory command.

A registered agent with a physical Florida street address is non-negotiable. A P.O. box will not satisfy Chapter 605, and an LLC without a current, responsive registered agent risks administrative problems with the state.

Why the Operating Agreement Matters More Than People Think

Florida does not require an operating agreement for an LLC to exist. I tell clients that this is exactly why so many Florida LLCs run into trouble later. Without one, state default rules under Chapter 605 fill the gaps, and those defaults may not reflect what you actually want to happen if an owner dies, a member wants out, or the business is sold.

A well-drafted operating agreement should address:

  • What happens to a member's ownership interest if that member dies or becomes incapacitated
  • Buyout rights and valuation methods between remaining members
  • Who can act as manager, and what happens if that person is unavailable
  • How the LLC continues (or winds up) after a triggering event

This last point is not just good drafting, it is often a legal deadline. For a single-member LLC, Florida law generally points the entity toward dissolution unless the estate or successor takes action to continue the business within a limited window, commonly discussed as roughly 90 days after the sole member's death. An operating agreement that names a successor manager in advance can prevent the business from stalling out while a probate case gets underway.

Connecting Your LLC to Your Florida Estate Plan

What I tell Florida clients who own rental property, a small business, or a side investment through an LLC is this: the LLC solves your liability problem while you're alive, but it does not automatically solve your succession problem when you die. Membership interests in an LLC are personal property, and if you own that interest in your individual name, it generally has to pass through probate like any other asset, unless you have planned otherwise.

Common Solution: Many Florida estate plans transfer LLC membership interests into a revocable living trust (governed by Florida Statutes Chapter 736). The trust, not the individual, becomes the member of record. When the owner dies, the successor trustee can step in and keep the business or property running without a probate proceeding interrupting it.

This is the single most overlooked piece of a "kit" bought off the internet. A generic LLC formation package gives you the entity. It rarely tells you how that entity fits into your broader plan, your trust, your beneficiaries, or your family's ability to keep a rental property or small business operating smoothly after you're gone.

Annual Compliance and the New Series LLC Option

Forming the LLC is only the beginning. Florida LLCs must file an annual report with the Department of State by May 1 of each year, with a current fee of $138.75, to remain active and in good standing. Missing this deadline can eventually lead to administrative dissolution. LLC owners should also keep their registered agent information current and stay aware of federal beneficial ownership information reporting obligations tied to the entity, since these requirements and deadlines have been subject to change.

⚠ 2026 Update: Protected Series LLCs As of July 1, 2026, amendments to the Florida Revised LLC Act allow an existing Florida LLC to designate multiple "protected series" under one parent LLC, each with its own separate assets and liability shield, through a low-cost online filing on Sunbiz. This can be useful for investors holding several rental properties who want to isolate liability property by property without forming and maintaining a completely separate LLC for each one. Whether this structure makes sense for a given portfolio, and how it should be coordinated with a trust-based estate plan, is a conversation worth having with a Florida attorney rather than assuming a generic online kit has it covered.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I legally need a Florida LLC kit to form an LLC?
No. Florida law only requires that you file Articles of Organization under Fla. Stat. § 605.0201 and pay the state filing fee. A kit is a convenience, not a legal requirement, though a complete one can help you avoid gaps that cause problems later.
What does it cost to form an LLC in Florida?
The current state filing fee is $125 total, consisting of $100 for the Articles of Organization and $25 for the registered agent designation. Commercial formation kits charge additional fees on top of the state's cost.
Is an operating agreement required for a Florida LLC?
No, Florida does not require an operating agreement, but it is strongly recommended. Without one, Chapter 605's default rules apply, which may not reflect what you want to happen regarding succession, buyouts, or management after a member's death.
What happens to my LLC if I die?
If you are the sole member and have not planned otherwise, Florida law generally points the LLC toward dissolution unless your estate or successor acts to continue it within a limited window, often discussed as roughly 90 days. Holding the membership interest in a revocable living trust, with a successor manager named in the operating agreement, can avoid this disruption.
What is a Florida protected series LLC?
As of July 1, 2026, Florida law allows an existing LLC to create multiple protected series under one parent entity, each with its own separate assets and liability shield, through a low-cost online filing. This is a newer option that may suit owners of multiple properties, but it should be set up carefully and reviewed against your overall estate plan.
What is the annual filing requirement for a Florida LLC?
Florida LLCs must file an annual report with the Department of State by May 1 each year, currently costing $138.75, to remain in active status and avoid administrative dissolution.

The Truestead Takeaway

A Florida LLC kit can be a genuinely helpful shortcut, but the real value isn't in the paperwork you file with the state, it's in the operating agreement, successor planning, and trust coordination that most off-the-shelf kits leave out. If you already own an LLC, or you're about to form one to hold rental property or a small business, it's worth having a Florida attorney review how that entity fits into your broader estate plan, particularly who takes over if you die or become incapacitated. That one conversation is usually what separates an LLC that protects your family from one that quietly becomes a probate headache.
