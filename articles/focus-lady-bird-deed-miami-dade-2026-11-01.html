Lady Bird Deed Miami-Dade: What Florida Homeowners Need to Know

Quick Answer

A lady bird deed in Miami-Dade lets you keep full control of your home during your life (you can sell, mortgage, or change your mind anytime) and automatically passes it to your chosen beneficiary when you die, without probate. Miami-Dade recognizes and records these deeds like any other Florida county, but condo property, homestead status, and spousal rights all require careful drafting.

By Arthur Simpson, Esq. · FL Bar #529265 Florida Real Estate Attorney November 1, 2026

What Is a Lady Bird Deed in Miami-Dade?

In my practice, Miami-Dade homeowners ask about lady bird deeds for one simple reason: they want their house to go to their kids or another chosen beneficiary without a probate case, but they do not want to give up control while they are alive. A lady bird deed (lawyers call it an enhanced life estate deed) does exactly that.

You, as the owner, sign a deed that reserves a life estate for yourself, but with an enhanced set of powers: the right to sell, mortgage, lease, refinance, gift, or even revoke the deed entirely, all without needing permission from the person named to inherit the property (the remainderman). When you pass away, the property transfers to that beneficiary automatically by operation of law, outside of probate.

There is no Florida statute specifically named 'Lady Bird Deed.' The legal foundation comes from general Florida real property law, including Chapter 689, Florida Statutes, which governs deeds conveying present and future interests in land, and from longstanding Florida case law recognizing that a grantor can retain a life estate coupled with broad powers over the property. Florida title insurers and the Florida Bar's title standards (Uniform Title Standards 6.10 through 6.12) have long recognized that a life tenant with these retained powers can convey or encumber the property without the remainderman joining in. Miami-Dade follows the same statewide framework as every other Florida county.

Does Miami-Dade County Have Special Rules for Lady Bird Deeds?

Miami-Dade does not have a separate lady bird deed law, but a few local and practical realities matter here more than in other parts of the state:

  • Documentary stamp tax. Miami-Dade applies its own base documentary stamp rate under Florida law of 60 cents per $100 of consideration, rather than the 70-cent rate used in most other Florida counties. A properly structured lady bird deed, where there is no consideration paid (it is a gift with a retained life estate), typically does not trigger documentary stamp tax at the time of signing, but this should be confirmed for your specific transaction.
  • Witness requirements. Under section 689.01, Florida Statutes, the deed must be signed before two witnesses. Miami-Dade recording practice calls for witnesses who are not parties to the transaction, and title underwriters generally prefer witnesses who have no financial interest in the property, to avoid any later challenge to the deed's validity.
  • Condominiums. Miami-Dade has one of the highest concentrations of condo ownership in the state. If your property is a condo unit, the deed must be drafted to account for the condominium declaration, any association approval requirements for transfers, and the legal description used by the association, which can differ from the county's recorded legal description.
Good to know: A lady bird deed does not need to be filed with or approved by the Miami-Dade Property Appraiser before it is effective, but it should be properly recorded in the Official Records of Miami-Dade County to protect the beneficiary's future interest and to keep the chain of title clear.

What Happens to Homestead Status and a Surviving Spouse?

This is where I see the most confusion, and it deserves careful attention. Florida's homestead protections, found in Article X, Section 4 of the Florida Constitution, and the homestead devise restrictions in F.S. § 732.4015, do not disappear just because you use a lady bird deed instead of a will.

If you are married, Florida Statute § 689.111 generally requires your spouse to join in any deed affecting homestead property, including a lady bird deed, for it to validly convey or encumber the homestead during your life. Separately, if you are survived by a spouse or a minor child, Florida's constitutional homestead restrictions limit who can inherit the homestead and how. A lady bird deed naming someone other than a spouse as the ultimate beneficiary can run into these restrictions if there is a surviving spouse or minor child at the time of death.

On the plus side, signing and recording a lady bird deed generally does not count as a change of ownership for Save Our Homes assessment cap purposes under F.S. § 193.155, because you have retained a life estate and the full bundle of ownership powers during your lifetime. This is one of the features that makes lady bird deeds attractive for homestead property in Miami-Dade, where property values and the accumulated Save Our Homes benefit can be substantial.

What Are the Medicaid and Tax Advantages?

Many of my clients come to this topic through elder law planning rather than estate planning alone. Florida limits Medicaid estate recovery to assets that pass through the probate estate. Because a lady bird deed transfers the home outside of probate, the home is generally not subject to Florida's Medicaid estate recovery claim after the owner's death, which is a meaningful difference from an outright gift deed made during life (which can trigger Medicaid's five-year lookback penalty).

On taxes, Florida has no state inheritance tax and no state estate tax, so Miami-Dade homeowners do not face a state-level tax bill on the transfer. The home is included in your gross estate for federal estate tax purposes, but federal estate tax only applies to estates above the federal exemption amount, which is very high and affects only a small number of estates. Most Florida families using a lady bird deed owe no federal estate tax on the home at all.

⚠ A note on drafting: Because there is no Florida statute spelling out the required language for a lady bird deed, the document must explicitly state every retained power (to sell, convey, mortgage, lease, gift, encumber, and revoke without the remainder beneficiary's consent). Thin or copied-form language is one of the most common reasons a lady bird deed gets challenged or rejected by a title company later.

What's New for 2026 and How Does It Affect Lady Bird Deeds?

I want to be straightforward here: there has been no recent change to the Florida law governing lady bird deeds themselves. Two other 2026 developments are worth knowing about because they affect the broader estate planning picture in Florida:

  • Florida's summary administration threshold, the dollar limit under which an estate can use the simpler, faster probate process, increased from $75,000 to $150,000 effective July 1, 2026. This makes probate avoidance tools like the lady bird deed somewhat less urgent for smaller estates, though it does not replace the benefits of avoiding probate entirely.
  • Florida voters are deciding a property tax ballot measure (Amendment 3) in the November 2026 election. As of this writing, nothing in the available research indicates this measure changes lady bird deed law or homestead transfer rules, but property tax law is worth monitoring if it affects your Miami-Dade homestead planning.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is a lady bird deed legal in Miami-Dade County, Florida?
Yes. Lady bird deeds are recognized statewide in Florida under general property law and common law principles, and Miami-Dade County records them the same way it records other deeds, through the Miami-Dade County Clerk's Official Records.
Does a lady bird deed avoid probate in Florida?
Yes, that is the main purpose. Because the property passes to the named beneficiary by operation of law at death, it does not become part of the probate estate, which also generally keeps it outside Florida's Medicaid estate recovery reach.
Can I sell my house after signing a lady bird deed?
Yes. A properly drafted lady bird deed reserves your right to sell, mortgage, lease, or otherwise deal with the property during your lifetime without needing permission from the beneficiary you named.
Does a lady bird deed affect my Save Our Homes homestead exemption in Miami-Dade?
Generally no. Because you retain a life estate and full control during your lifetime, recording a lady bird deed typically does not count as a change of ownership that would reset your Save Our Homes assessment cap, but this depends on how the deed is drafted.
Do both spouses need to sign a lady bird deed on homestead property?
If the property is homestead and you are married, Florida law generally requires your spouse to join in the deed. Florida's constitutional homestead rules can also limit who can ultimately inherit the home if a spouse or minor child survives you, so this situation should be reviewed individually.
Can I use a lady bird deed for a condo in Miami-Dade?
Yes, but condo transfers require extra care because the condominium association's declaration and legal description must be accounted for in the deed. An attorney familiar with Miami-Dade condo conveyances should review the title and association documents before drafting.

The Truestead Takeaway

A lady bird deed can be a genuinely useful tool for Miami-Dade homeowners who want to keep full control of their home during life while avoiding probate at death, and it pairs well with Florida's homestead and Medicaid planning rules when it is drafted correctly. Because there is no Florida statute spelling out the required form, and because homestead, spousal, and condo issues each carry their own traps, I always recommend having a Florida attorney review your specific deed, title, and family situation before you sign anything.
