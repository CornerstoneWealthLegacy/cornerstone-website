What Is the Florida 3008 Form? A Plain-Language Guide

Quick Answer

The Florida 3008 form (AHCA Form 5000-3008) is a medical certification that a doctor, physician assistant, or ARNP completes to prove someone meets the medical eligibility requirements for Florida Medicaid long-term care services, such as nursing facility care or home and community-based waiver services. It is not a will, trust, or estate planning document.

By Arthur Simpson, Esq. · FL Bar #529265 Florida Estate Planning Attorney October 20, 2026

The 3008 Form Isn't What Most People Expect

If you searched "3008 form" hoping to find an estate planning document, I want to clear up the confusion right away, because in my practice I hear this question often from families who are juggling a parent's long-term care needs at the same time they're thinking about wills and trusts. The 3008 form, officially AHCA Form 5000-3008, is a Medicaid medical certification and patient transfer form. It has nothing to do with drafting a will, funding a trust, or planning your estate. It is a healthcare eligibility document used within Florida's Medicaid long-term care system.

Where it does intersect with estate planning is practical, not legal: many of the same families applying for Medicaid long-term care benefits for a parent are also, at the same time, reviewing that parent's power of attorney, healthcare surrogate designation, and overall estate plan. So it's common for the 3008 form to show up in a family's paperwork pile right alongside trust and will documents, even though it's a completely separate legal animal.

What the 3008 Form Actually Does

Form 3008 is used to establish medical eligibility for Florida's Statewide Medicaid Managed Care Long-Term Care (SMMC-LTC) Program. This program covers services like nursing facility care and home and community-based services (HCBS) for Floridians who need a nursing-facility level of care but may be able to receive it at home or in an assisted living setting instead.

The form documents a person's functional need, meaning their ability or inability to perform Activities of Daily Living, such as:

  • Transferring from a bed to a chair
  • Mobility and walking
  • Eating independently
  • Toileting and personal hygiene

It also includes sections covering medical conditions, decision-making capacity, infection control issues, allergies, advance care planning, and physician contact information. The Comprehensive Assessment and Review for Long-Term Care Services (CARES) Program, which operates under Florida's Agency for Health Care Administration, uses this information to determine whether an applicant meets the medical criteria for Medicaid-funded long-term care.

Who Fills Out the 3008 Form, and When

This is a point of real confusion for families, so let me walk through the process as I'd explain it to a client. The 3008 form is not something you fill out yourself. It must be completed and signed by a licensed medical professional, specifically:

  • A licensed physician (M.D. or D.O.)
  • A physician assistant (PA)
  • An advanced registered nurse practitioner (ARNP)

Section 464.003, Florida Statutes, authorizes ARNPs to complete and sign the 3008 form, a change that became standard practice some years ago and remains the current rule today. If the treating physician, PA, or ARNP is not licensed in Florida but is appropriately licensed through the U.S. military, the Department of Veterans Affairs, or another state, additional documentation is required to validate the certification.

Here's how the timing typically works: when a Medicaid long-term care waiver slot becomes available for an applicant on Florida's waiting list, the local Aging and Disability Resource Center (ADRC) contacts the applicant and sends out the 3008 form. The applicant then takes the form to their doctor's office to have it completed, and it must be returned to the ADRC as part of the eligibility process.

Good to know: The 3008 form is only one piece of the Medicaid long-term care eligibility puzzle. Financial eligibility (income and asset limits) is assessed separately from this medical certification, and both must be satisfied before benefits begin.

How Long Is the 3008 Form Valid?

The 3008 form is valid for one year from the date the healthcare provider signs it. If the Medicaid long-term care eligibility process extends beyond that window, for example because of delays in financial eligibility review or waiver slot availability, a new, updated 3008 form may be required to confirm the applicant's medical condition hasn't changed in a way that affects eligibility.

⚠ Don't let the form lapse: Families sometimes lose momentum in the application process waiting on other paperwork, only to find the 3008 certification has expired and needs to be redone. If your family is navigating a Medicaid long-term care application, keep track of the signature date on this form just as closely as you track financial documents.

Where This Fits With Your Broader Florida Estate Plan

Even though the 3008 form itself is a Medicaid medical document and not an estate planning tool, the situation that brings a family to this form (a parent or loved one needing long-term care) is exactly the moment when I encourage clients to also review their broader planning. That typically includes:

  • A durable power of attorney under F.S. Chapter 709, so a trusted agent can handle financial and legal matters if the person becomes incapacitated
  • A healthcare surrogate designation under F.S. § 765.202, so someone is authorized to make medical decisions
  • A living will under F.S. § 765.301, expressing wishes about end-of-life care
  • A review of the person's revocable trust (F.S. Chapter 736) or will (F.S. Chapter 732), especially if long-term care costs or Medicaid planning strategies affect how assets are titled

Medicaid planning and asset protection for long-term care involves its own set of rules around countable assets, homestead treatment, and look-back periods that are separate from the 3008 form itself. If your family is facing a long-term care Medicaid application, it's worth having both the healthcare side (handled through your physician and the ADRC) and the legal and financial side (handled through an elder law attorney) moving forward together.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is the 3008 form the same as a Florida will or trust document?
No. The 3008 form is a Medicaid medical certification used to establish eligibility for long-term care services. It has no connection to wills, trusts, or probate under Florida law.
Who can legally sign the Florida 3008 form?
A licensed physician, physician assistant, or advanced registered nurse practitioner (ARNP) can complete and sign the 3008 form. Out-of-state or military-licensed providers may sign with additional supporting documentation.
How long does a completed 3008 form remain valid?
The form is valid for one year from the date it is signed by the healthcare provider. If the Medicaid application process takes longer than that, a new form may be needed.
Where can I get a copy of the 3008 form?
The form is typically sent directly to an applicant by their local Aging and Disability Resource Center (ADRC) once a Medicaid long-term care waiver slot becomes available, rather than being something you download and submit on your own initiative.
Does completing the 3008 form guarantee Medicaid long-term care eligibility?
No. The 3008 form only addresses medical eligibility. Financial eligibility, including income and asset limits, is reviewed separately, and both must be satisfied for Medicaid long-term care benefits to be approved.
Should I talk to an attorney about the 3008 form?
The form itself is typically handled between the applicant, their doctor, and the ADRC, but an elder law attorney can be valuable for the surrounding legal and financial planning, such as asset protection strategies and powers of attorney, that often accompany a long-term care Medicaid application.

The Truestead Takeaway

In my practice, the families who come across the 3008 form are almost always in the middle of a stressful transition, helping a parent or spouse qualify for Medicaid long-term care while also trying to get their legal affairs in order. The form itself is a medical certification handled by a doctor, PA, or ARNP, not something your estate planning attorney fills out. But if you're at this stage, it's a smart time to also have your power of attorney, healthcare surrogate, living will, and trust or will reviewed to make sure they still reflect your family's needs. If you're navigating a Medicaid long-term care application in Florida, consider having both the medical side and the legal side reviewed together, and don't hesitate to reach out to a Florida elder law attorney to make sure nothing falls through the cracks.
