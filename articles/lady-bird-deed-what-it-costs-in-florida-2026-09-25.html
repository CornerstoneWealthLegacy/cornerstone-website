What a Lady Bird Deed Costs in Florida (and What You Get for It)

Quick Answer

Lady bird deed prices in Florida range from about $30 for a bare online form to $1,000 or more for full attorney service, and the difference is almost always the title check, homestead and spousal analysis, and correct beneficiary language, not the paper itself.

By Arthur Simpson, Esq. · FL Bar #529265 Florida Estate Planning Attorney September 25, 2026

Marcus's Three Quotes

Marcus is 58, lives in Orlando, and has been helping his mother get her affairs in order after his father passed. She wants her house to go to Marcus and his sister without probate, and someone told her a lady bird deed would do it. (Marcus is a composite we use to illustrate common questions, not an actual Truestead client.) A quick lady bird deed, or enhanced life estate deed, lets an owner keep full control of the property for life, including the right to sell it or change their mind, while it passes to named beneficiaries automatically at death.

Marcus found three options online: a $29 fill-in-the-blank generator, a paralegal service quoting $349 flat, and two local law firms quoting $450 and $900. His question was simple: what is he actually paying for, and does the price difference matter for his mother's situation?

The Four Price Tiers, and What Each One Usually Includes

Having reviewed and corrected other people's deeds for years, I can tell you the price tiers roughly track how much legal judgment went into the document, not how many pages it has. Here is how they generally break down in Florida.

  • Under $100 (online generators and bare templates): You get a fillable form with lady bird language dropped in. There is usually no review of how title is currently held, no homestead analysis, no check for a surviving spouse who must join the deed, and no guidance on naming contingent beneficiaries. You are responsible for execution requirements and recording yourself.
  • $100 to $400 (paralegal or document-preparation services): These typically prepare the deed correctly on its face and coordinate signing and recording, but Florida law does not allow non-lawyers to give legal advice about your title, your homestead status, or how the deed interacts with your will or other beneficiaries. If the service does not have an attorney reviewing the specific facts, it is filling in a form, not practicing law.
  • $400 to $900 (law firm flat-fee packages): This is where a Florida-licensed attorney actually looks at the current deed, confirms how the property is titled, checks for a surviving spouse's homestead rights, drafts contingent beneficiary language in case a primary beneficiary dies first, and prepares the deed for proper execution and recording.
  • $900 to $1,500+ (complex or bundled matters): This tier usually reflects added complexity, such as multiple owners, a trust as beneficiary, out-of-state property interests, or the deed being bundled into a broader estate plan with a will, power of attorney, and healthcare directive.

What 'Including Recording' Actually Means

Every Florida deed, lady bird deeds included, must be signed by the owner in front of two witnesses and a notary, and then it must be recorded in the official records of the county where the property sits. Recording is what makes the deed effective against the world and part of the public title chain.

Recording fees are set by the county clerk based on the number of pages and names on the document, and they are a separate cost from whatever you pay for legal work or document preparation. Most lady bird deeds also owe only the minimum documentary stamp tax, because no ownership interest actually transfers while the owner is alive and keeps full control; the tax due on this kind of transfer is far smaller than the tax on a typical sale.

When a price advertises 'including recording,' that should mean the flat fee covers the clerk's recording charge so you are not billed separately after the fact. Ask directly whether documentary stamps and the clerk's recording fee are built into the quoted price or added on top.

What to ask before you pay: Does this price include an attorney reviewing my mother's current deed and homestead status? Is a surviving spouse's joinder addressed if one exists? Are contingent beneficiaries included if my sister predeceases our mother? Is recording, and the documentary stamp tax, part of the quoted price or separate?

Why the Cheap Form Worried Marcus

Marcus almost went with the $29 generator, until he read that Florida homestead property owned by a married person cannot be conveyed or devised without the spouse joining the deed. His mother is widowed, so that particular issue did not apply to her, but it made him realize the form had no way of catching that kind of problem for anyone. It also had no place to name a backup beneficiary if something happened to Marcus or his sister first, and no explanation of how the deed would interact with his mother's existing will.

Florida has no statute that creates the lady bird deed by name. It works because of long-standing common law, Florida title standards, and decades of practice by real estate attorneys and title examiners. That also means there is no official state form to fill in the blanks of, and the quality of the drafting depends entirely on whoever prepares it. A non-lawyer preparer who starts advising on title issues, homestead rights, or how the deed fits into an estate plan is generally crossing into the unauthorized practice of law, which is exactly the gap that separates the $29 tier from the $349 tier in substance, not just price.

⚠ A note on non-attorney services: Florida law does not require an attorney to prepare a deed, but a document-preparation service that is not supervised by a Florida-licensed attorney cannot legally advise you on your specific title or homestead situation. If a service is quoting a flat fee well above a bare template price, ask directly whether a Florida attorney reviews the facts or only the paperwork.

When the Cheaper Tier Is Genuinely Fine

I do not want to overstate the risk here. A lady bird deed on a simple, unencumbered home, owned outright by a single person, with one clear beneficiary and no complicating factors, is not a complicated document. If Marcus's mother owned the home free and clear, had no spouse to join the deed, and wanted to leave it entirely to Marcus with no contingent plan needed, a well-prepared low-cost option could work fine, as long as it correctly identifies the property by legal description, uses proper execution formalities, and gets recorded.

Where the cheaper tiers fall short is when there is a spouse, multiple beneficiaries, a desire for backup beneficiaries, uncertainty about how title is currently held, or any interplay with a will or trust. Marcus ultimately chose the $399 attorney-prepared route for his mother, mainly because he wanted someone to confirm the current deed was accurate and that the contingent beneficiary language protected his sister's children if something happened to her first.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is a $29 online lady bird deed form legally valid in Florida?
It can be, if it meets Florida's execution requirements (two witnesses and a notary) and is properly recorded, but the form itself does nothing to confirm your title, homestead status, or beneficiary structure are correct for your situation.
Does the price include recording fees and documentary stamps?
Not always. Ask specifically, since these are separate costs set by the county clerk and the state, and a package that says 'including recording' should mean those charges are built into the quoted price.
Why do some services charge under $500 while attorneys charge $900 or more?
The difference is usually the depth of review: whether someone is checking your current title, homestead status, spousal rights, and beneficiary planning, versus simply filling in a template.
Can a paralegal or online service give me legal advice about my deed?
No. Florida law reserves legal advice for licensed attorneys, so a non-lawyer service can prepare paperwork but cannot advise you on your specific title or homestead facts without crossing into unauthorized practice of law.
What happens if my spouse isn't included on a homestead lady bird deed?
Florida homestead property owned by a married person generally cannot be conveyed or devised without the spouse joining the deed, so leaving a spouse off when one exists can create a real title problem.
