What Does Florida Law Require After a Car Accident?

Florida requires drivers involved in a crash that results in injury, death, or a certain level of property damage to stop and, when applicable, report the accident. Indian Harbour Beach sits within Brevard County, so a crash there may involve the Indian Harbour Beach Police Department, the Brevard County Sheriff's Office, or the Florida Highway Patrol, depending on where the accident occurred.

  • Florida is a no-fault insurance state, meaning drivers typically carry Personal Injury Protection (PIP) coverage that pays a portion of medical bills and lost wages regardless of who caused the crash.
  • To step outside the no-fault system and pursue a claim against the at-fault driver for pain and suffering, an injured person generally must meet Florida's serious injury threshold.
  • Florida law also addresses hit-and-run accidents (choque y fuga) separately, and leaving the scene of a crash involving injury can carry serious criminal consequences in addition to any civil claim.

How Long Do I Have to File a Car Accident Claim in Florida?

⚠ Deadlines Changed in Florida Florida's rules on car accident lawsuits were significantly reformed in 2023, including changes to how long an injured person has to sue and how fault is shared between parties. Because these rules affect real deadlines, I strongly encourage anyone with a potential claim to confirm the current statute of limitations and comparative negligence rules with a Florida attorney rather than relying on older information found online.

What I tell every client: do not assume the deadline from a few years ago still applies. Florida's negligence statute of limitations and the comparative negligence standard were both revised by the 2023 tort reform legislation, and the specific current deadline for your situation should be verified by a licensed Florida attorney reviewing your facts, since it can vary depending on when the accident occurred and the type of claim involved.

Will My Own Fault Affect My Compensation?

Florida moved to a modified comparative negligence system as part of its 2023 tort reform. Under a modified comparative negligence approach, an injured person's compensation is typically reduced by their own percentage of fault, and if that person is found more than a certain percentage at fault, they may be barred from recovering at all. Because the exact threshold and its application can be fact-specific, I recommend having an attorney evaluate the police report, witness statements, and any traffic camera or dashcam footage before assuming how fault will be allocated in your case.

This is one of the most important reasons to have representation early. Insurance adjusters are trained to shift blame toward the injured party because even a modest increase in your assigned fault percentage can significantly reduce what you ultimately recover.

What Happens If the Accident Involved a Serious Injury or Death?

When a car accident in Indian Harbour Beach results in a serious or catastrophic injury, or a wrongful death, the legal and financial stakes rise considerably. Florida law allows certain claims for wrongful death to be brought by a personal representative of the deceased person's estate on behalf of surviving family members. This is one of the few places where personal injury law and estate law intersect directly, since a wrongful death claim is technically pursued through the probate estate of the person who died. If your family is facing this kind of loss, it is worth discussing both the personal injury claim and any necessary probate or estate administration steps with an attorney who understands how the two interact.

Frequently Asked Questions

Necesito hablar español para contratar un abogado de accidentes en Indian Harbour Beach?
Muchos bufetes en el área de Brevard County, incluyendo Truestead Law, pueden coordinar servicio en español o conectarlo con personal bilingüe. Siempre pregunte directamente si el abogado o su equipo habla español antes de contratar.
What if the other driver didn't have insurance?
Florida drivers are not required to carry bodily injury liability insurance in the same way as some states, so uninsured and underinsured motorist coverage on your own policy can become critical. An attorney can review your policy to see what coverage may apply.
Does it matter which city or county handles the police report?
Yes, the responding agency (Indian Harbour Beach Police, Brevard County Sheriff's Office, or Florida Highway Patrol) will generate the official crash report, which is often key evidence. Your attorney can help obtain and interpret that report.
How soon after a car accident should I contact a lawyer?
As soon as reasonably possible. Evidence, witness memory, and insurance company tactics all change quickly, and Florida's current deadlines for filing a claim make early legal advice especially valuable.
Can I still recover money if I was partly at fault for the accident?
Under Florida's modified comparative negligence system, partial fault can reduce your recovery, and significant fault may bar recovery entirely. An attorney should review the specific facts of your accident to assess this.
Is a car accident claim the same as a hit-and-run criminal case?
No. A hit-and-run (choque y fuga) can trigger a separate criminal case against the fleeing driver, while your personal injury claim for medical bills and damages is a separate civil matter, though the two can be related.
