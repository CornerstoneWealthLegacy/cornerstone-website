Davie Slip and Fall Accident Lawyer: What Florida Law Says About Your Claim

Quick Answer

If you were hurt in a slip and fall in Davie, Florida law gives you two years from the date of injury to file suit, requires you to prove the property owner knew or should have known about the hazard, and bars recovery if you're found more than 50% at fault. A Florida premises liability attorney can evaluate whether your specific facts meet these standards.

What a Davie Slip and Fall Accident Lawyer Actually Does for You

In my practice, I find that people searching for a Davie slip and fall accident lawyer usually want one thing first: to know whether they even have a case, and how much time they have to act. Davie sits in Broward County, and like every city in Florida, slip and fall claims here are governed by the same statewide statutes, not a separate local law. What changes from case to case is the property, the evidence, and the specific facts of the fall, whether it happened at a Davie shopping plaza, a grocery store near State Road 84, an apartment complex, or a government-owned sidewalk.

A premises liability attorney's job is to investigate the scene quickly, preserve surveillance footage before it's deleted, identify who legally controlled the property, and determine whether your evidence can meet Florida's specific proof requirements. That last part matters more than most people realize, because Florida law puts a real burden on the injured person, not just the property owner.

The Florida Law That Controls Most Slip and Fall Cases

Most slip and fall claims involving a transitory foreign substance, think spilled liquid, a dropped item, or a wet floor, are governed by Florida Statute § 768.0755. This statute requires the injured person to prove that the business had actual or constructive knowledge of the dangerous condition and failed to correct it in a reasonable time.

  • Actual knowledge means an employee or owner saw the hazard or was told about it.
  • Constructive knowledge can be shown through circumstantial evidence, such as the substance being on the floor long enough that a reasonably careful business should have discovered it, or evidence that the same type of hazard happened regularly and was foreseeable.

This is a meaningfully higher bar than simply proving you fell and got hurt. Without evidence of how long the hazard existed, or that it was a recurring problem, a claim can be very difficult to prove, which is exactly why fast evidence preservation matters so much.

How Long Do You Have to File a Slip and Fall Claim in Florida?

Florida's 2023 tort reform law, House Bill 837, changed the deadline for negligence claims, including most slip and fall cases. For injuries occurring on or after March 24, 2023, Florida Statute § 95.11 sets a two-year statute of limitations from the date of the injury. Falls that happened before that date generally fall under the older four-year window.

⚠ Don't wait to get advice. Two years sounds like a long time, but evidence disappears fast: surveillance video is often overwritten within days or weeks, witnesses move, and store maintenance logs get discarded. Even if your deadline isn't close, the strength of your case often depends on how quickly it was investigated.

What If You Were Partly at Fault?

Florida used to allow injured people to recover damages even if they were mostly at fault, reduced by their share of blame. HB 837 changed that. Under Florida Statute § 768.81, Florida now follows a modified comparative negligence rule: if you are found to be more than 50% at fault for your own injury, you are barred from recovering any compensation at all.

In practice, this means property owners and insurance companies have a strong incentive to argue that you weren't paying attention, were wearing inappropriate footwear, or ignored a warning sign. How your case is documented and presented from the start can significantly affect whether a fault argument like this succeeds.

Special Rules for Apartments, Condos, and Government Property

Not every Davie slip and fall case follows the exact same path. Two situations deserve special attention:

  • Multifamily residential properties. Florida Statute § 768.0706, also enacted through HB 837, created a presumption against liability for apartment and condominium owners who implement certain specified security measures. If you fell at a Davie apartment complex, whether those measures were in place can directly affect your claim.
  • Government-owned property. If you fell on property owned by the Town of Davie, Broward County, or another government entity, your claim is governed by Florida's sovereign immunity waiver under Florida Statute § 768.28. These claims come with additional notice requirements and procedural steps beyond a typical private property claim, so timing and paperwork matter even more.

What You Have to Prove to Win

Regardless of where the fall happened, Florida premises liability law generally requires proof of three things:

  • The property owner or operator owed you a duty of care (generally owed to lawful visitors);
  • The owner breached that duty by failing to fix, or adequately warn about, a dangerous condition; and
  • That breach actually caused your injury.
A note on punitive damages: These are rare in slip and fall cases. Florida Statute § 768.72 requires clear and convincing evidence of intentional misconduct or gross negligence, a high bar that ordinary negligence doesn't meet.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long do I have to file a slip and fall lawsuit in Davie, Florida?
For falls occurring on or after March 24, 2023, Florida law generally gives you two years from the date of injury under Florida Statute § 95.11. Falls before that date may still fall under the prior four-year deadline, so your specific timeline should be confirmed with a Florida attorney.
What do I need to prove if I slipped on a wet floor in a Davie store?
Under Florida Statute § 768.0755, you generally must show the business had actual knowledge of the hazard or constructive knowledge, meaning the condition existed long enough that reasonable care should have caught it, or that similar hazards happened regularly enough to be foreseeable.
Can I still recover damages if I was partly responsible for my fall?
Possibly. Florida's modified comparative negligence rule under Florida Statute § 768.81 allows recovery as long as you are found 50% or less at fault, with your award reduced by your percentage of fault. If you're found more than 50% at fault, you cannot recover.
What if I fell on a sidewalk or in a park owned by the Town of Davie?
Claims against government-owned property are handled differently under Florida's sovereign immunity statute, Florida Statute § 768.28, which includes notice requirements and procedural steps that don't apply to private property claims.
Do I need a lawyer right after a slip and fall, or can it wait?
Because key evidence like surveillance footage and maintenance records can disappear quickly, and because Florida's proof requirements are strict, getting a case reviewed early generally gives you the best chance of preserving the evidence needed to meet the legal standard.
Are slip and fall settlements guaranteed in Florida?
No outcome can be promised in any case. Florida law requires specific proof of the property owner's knowledge and fault, and every case depends on its own facts and evidence, which is why a case-specific review matters.

The Truestead Takeaway

If you were hurt in a slip and fall anywhere in Davie or elsewhere in Broward County, the law is less about the city you fell in and more about the specific facts: what the property owner knew, how long the hazard existed, and how much of the fault, if any, belongs to you. Florida's two-year deadline and the 50% fault bar under HB 837 make early evidence preservation and an honest case evaluation especially important. The sensible next step is to have your situation reviewed by a Florida personal injury attorney who can look at your specific facts and timeline.
