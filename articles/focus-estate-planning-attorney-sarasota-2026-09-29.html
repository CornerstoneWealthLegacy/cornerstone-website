Finding the Right Estate Planning Attorney in Sarasota, Florida

Quick Answer

A qualified estate planning attorney in Sarasota should be an active, licensed Florida Bar member who prepares wills, trusts, powers of attorney, and healthcare directives that meet Florida's specific execution rules, and ideally has real experience (or board certification) in wills, trusts, and estates law.

By Arthur Simpson, Esq. · FL Bar #529265 Florida Estate Planning Attorney September 29, 2026

What Does an Estate Planning Attorney in Sarasota Actually Do?

When Sarasota residents search for an estate planning attorney Sarasota can trust, what they usually want is straightforward: someone local who understands Florida law and can put together a plan that actually works when the family needs it. In my practice, I tell clients that a complete Florida estate plan generally has four building blocks:

  • A last will and testament, which must be signed by the testator in the presence of two witnesses who also sign, under Florida Statutes Chapter 732. Florida does not recognize handwritten wills that lack proper witnessing, even if they'd be valid in another state.
  • A revocable living trust, governed by Florida Statutes Chapter 736, often used to avoid probate on certain assets and to manage property if you become incapacitated.
  • A durable power of attorney under Florida Statutes Chapter 709, which lets someone handle your finances if you can't. Florida law does not allow "springing" powers of attorney that only activate later; a Florida durable power of attorney is effective immediately upon signing (though you control when it's used) and must specifically list each power granted, rather than relying on a vague catch-all clause.
  • Advance directives, including a healthcare surrogate designation (F.S. § 765.202) and a living will (F.S. § 765.301), which let you name someone to make medical decisions and state your wishes about end-of-life care.

Because Florida has its own formalities for each of these documents, an out-of-state will, trust, or power of attorney should always be reviewed by a Florida attorney, even if it was properly done elsewhere. What worked in another state does not automatically work here.

How Do I Know an Attorney Is Actually Qualified?

Anyone can put "estate planning" on a website. What matters is whether the attorney is a licensed, active member of The Florida Bar in good standing, with no unresolved disciplinary history. That is the baseline, and it's worth checking directly through the Florida Bar's public attorney directory before you ever sign an engagement letter.

Beyond licensure, some attorneys go further and become Board Certified in Wills, Trusts and Estates Law, a credential The Florida Bar awards only to attorneys who meet rigorous standards, including:

  • At least five years of law practice (or four years with an LL.M. in taxation or estate planning and probate law)
  • Substantial involvement in wills, trusts, and estates work, generally 40% or more of their practice, during each of the two years before applying
  • At least 90 hours of approved continuing legal education in the specialty over the prior three years
  • Peer review and a written examination

Board certification is not required to do good estate planning work, but it is a meaningful signal, and The Florida Bar's directory lets you search for certified specialists in the Sarasota area.

What to ask before you hire: Ask how much of the attorney's practice is dedicated to wills, trusts, and estates; whether they draft Florida-specific documents (not generic templates); and whether they will personally review your existing documents for Florida compliance if you moved here from another state.

Why Florida's 2026 Probate Changes Matter for Your Plan

Florida made a significant change to probate procedure that took effect on July 1, 2026. Under a new law (Chapter 2026-57, from CS/HB 1337), the threshold for using Florida's simpler summary administration process, rather than full formal probate, doubled from $75,000 to $150,000 in non-exempt assets. This matters because summary administration is generally faster and less expensive than formal administration, and more Sarasota families will now qualify for it.

The same law raised several small-estate dollar limits that let families skip formal probate altogether for modest assets:

  • Bank funds released to family by affidavit: increased from $1,000 to $2,000
  • Income tax refunds claimed without administration: increased from $2,500 to $5,000
  • Certain intestate personal property disposable without administration: increased from $10,000 to $20,000

The law also created a new tool, Section 733.6125, allowing a personal representative to go to court to enforce their authority when a bank or financial institution wrongly refuses to honor it, with the court required to award attorney fees and costs to the personal representative if they prevail. This directly addresses a frustration many Florida families have faced when institutions dragged their feet on releasing a deceased person's assets.

None of this changes the value of having a will or trust in place; it changes what happens procedurally after death for estates that end up in probate. An experienced Sarasota estate planning attorney should be able to explain how these updated thresholds affect your particular family's situation.

What About Federal Estate Tax and Homestead Protections?

Florida has no state estate tax or state inheritance tax, and that has not changed. Most Sarasota families never need to worry about federal estate tax either, because the federal exemption amount is set at a high level that covers the vast majority of estates; federal exemption figures are adjusted periodically, and I'd encourage you to confirm the current number with your attorney rather than relying on an old figure, since this is a federal tax matter, not Florida law.

What does matter enormously for Sarasota homeowners is Florida's homestead protection, found in Article X, Section 4 of the Florida Constitution. Homestead property generally receives strong protection from creditors and has specific rules about how it can be devised at death under F.S. § 732.4015, particularly if you're married or have minor children. Homestead also interacts with Florida's Save Our Homes assessment cap and portability rules under F.S. § 193.155, which can affect property taxes for a surviving spouse or heir. These homestead rules are one of the most Florida-specific parts of estate planning, and they trip up a lot of people who assume their out-of-state plan already covers it. It usually doesn't.

⚠ A common mistake I see: Snowbirds and retirees who moved to Sarasota often keep an estate plan drafted in their prior home state. That plan may not properly address Florida homestead rules, Florida's witness requirements for wills, or Florida's restrictions on powers of attorney. A Florida-specific review is the only way to know for sure.

How Many Estate Planning Attorneys Are There in Sarasota, and How Do I Choose?

Sarasota has a genuinely deep bench of estate planning attorneys, with hundreds of listed attorneys across dozens of local firms in directories like Justia and LawInfo. That's good news for you as a consumer, but it also means the search results are crowded and not every listing reflects real, current experience.

When narrowing your search, focus on:

  • Whether the attorney is licensed and in good standing with The Florida Bar
  • Whether estate planning, probate, or elder law is a significant, ongoing part of their practice, not a small side offering
  • Whether they draft documents specifically for Florida law (wills, revocable trusts, Florida-compliant durable powers of attorney, healthcare surrogate designations, and living wills)
  • Whether they take time to explain your plan in plain language, and whether you feel comfortable asking questions

A short introductory conversation with a Sarasota attorney should give you a clear sense of whether they're a good fit before you commit to anything.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need a will or a trust, or both, in Sarasota, Florida?
It depends on your assets, family situation, and goals. A will is the foundation of most Florida estate plans, while a revocable trust is often added to avoid probate on certain assets or to manage property during incapacity. A Florida attorney can review your specific situation and recommend which combination makes sense.
How much does an estate planning attorney in Sarasota typically charge?
Fees vary by attorney and by the complexity of the plan, so it's best to ask for a clear fee structure during your initial consultation rather than assume a set price.
What changed in Florida probate law in 2026?
Effective July 1, 2026, Florida raised the threshold for summary administration from $75,000 to $150,000 in non-exempt assets, and increased several small-estate dollar limits for handling modest assets without formal probate. A new statute also gives personal representatives a stronger legal tool to enforce their authority against uncooperative institutions.
Is my out-of-state will valid in Florida?
Florida generally recognizes wills validly executed under the law of the state where they were signed, but Florida has its own strict rules for trusts, powers of attorney, and homestead property that an out-of-state document may not satisfy. It's worth having any out-of-state estate plan reviewed by a Florida attorney after you establish residency here.
Does Florida have a state estate tax?
No. Florida does not impose a state estate tax or inheritance tax. Most families' concerns involve the federal estate tax exemption, which applies only to very large estates and is set separately by federal law.
How do I verify an attorney's board certification in estate planning?
You can check The Florida Bar's official online directory, which lists attorneys currently certified as specialists in Wills, Trusts and Estates Law, along with confirming their license status and disciplinary history.

The Truestead Takeaway

Choosing an estate planning attorney in Sarasota is really about confirming two things: that the attorney is a licensed, reputable Florida Bar member with real, ongoing experience in wills, trusts, and estates, and that your documents are built specifically for Florida's rules, including the state's witness requirements, homestead protections, and 2026 probate updates. If you already have an estate plan, especially one drafted before you moved to Florida or before July 2026, it's a sensible idea to have it reviewed rather than assume it still fits. That review, and the conversation that comes with it, is the natural next step, and it's exactly the kind of conversation I'd want my own family to have.
