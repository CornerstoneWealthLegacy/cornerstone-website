Florida Lady Bird Deeds

Lady Bird Deed vs. Quitclaiming the House to Your Children

Quick Answer

A straight quitclaim deed gives your house away today, with all the tax, creditor, and control problems that come with a present gift. A Florida lady bird deed lets you keep full ownership and control for the rest of your life and passes the house to your children only when you die, without probate.

By Arthur Simpson, Esq. · FL Bar #529265 Florida Estate Planning Attorney September 25, 2026

Frank's Plan, and Why It Worried Me

Frank is a composite I'm using to illustrate a conversation I have often, not an actual client. He's 82, lives in Deltona, and his neighbor told him the simplest way to keep the house out of probate was to quitclaim it to his three kids right now. Frank liked the sound of that. Simple and free, or close to it. What Frank didn't know is that a quitclaim deed done today is a present, irrevocable gift, while a Florida lady bird deed (also called an enhanced life estate deed) lets him keep full ownership and control, including the right to sell, mortgage, or change his mind, for as long as he lives. The house only passes to his children automatically at his death, with no probate required.

Eight Consequences, Side by Side

Here's how the two deeds actually compare on the issues that matter most to someone in Frank's position.

  • Control during life: A quitclaim deed hands the kids an ownership interest immediately. Frank would need all three children's consent to sell or refinance. A lady bird deed changes nothing while Frank is alive. He can sell, mortgage, or revoke it entirely, on his own signature.
  • Medicaid five-year lookback: A gift by quitclaim deed is a transfer for less than fair value and can trigger Medicaid's five-year lookback penalty if Frank ever needs nursing home care. Because a lady bird deed transfers nothing until death, it is not a countable gift during Frank's lifetime and does not trigger the lookback.
  • Homestead status: Frank's homestead exemption depends on him owning and living in the home. A lady bird deed preserves that because he remains the owner. A quitclaim deed that adds his children as current owners can jeopardize the exemption and raises real questions about whether the property still qualifies as his homestead.
  • Children's creditors and divorces: Once a quitclaim deed is recorded, each child owns a real interest in Frank's house today, an interest that a child's creditor can attach or a divorcing spouse can claim. A lady bird deed gives the children nothing until Frank dies, so their creditors and spouses have nothing to reach while Frank is alive.
  • Capital gains basis: This is the one I stress most. A lifetime quitclaim gift means the kids inherit Frank's original purchase price as their tax basis. If Frank paid a modest sum decades ago and the house is worth far more now, they could owe capital gains tax on that entire gap when they sell. Property that passes through a lady bird deed instead gets a stepped-up basis at Frank's death, valued as of the date he dies, which can eliminate most or all of that capital gains exposure.
  • Save Our Homes property tax cap: Florida's Save Our Homes cap limits how much a homestead's assessed value can rise each year. A present transfer by quitclaim deed can be treated as a change of ownership that breaks the cap, potentially resetting the assessment to full market value. A lady bird deed does not transfer present ownership, so the cap is not disturbed during Frank's lifetime.
  • Documentary stamp tax: If Frank's house carries a mortgage, quitclaiming it to his children can trigger Florida documentary stamp tax on the outstanding loan balance, even if the kids never formally assume the debt. A lady bird deed transfers no present interest, so the Department of Revenue's position is that only the minimal recording stamp applies, not stamp tax on the mortgage.
  • Undoing it: A quitclaim deed is done. To get the house back, Frank would need all three children to voluntarily deed it back to him, an awkward conversation if any relationship changes or a child refuses. A lady bird deed can be revoked or changed by Frank alone, by recording a new deed, for as long as he is competent to do so.

What About Frank's Wife, or a Future Spouse?

One detail Frank's neighbor never mentioned: if Frank were married, Florida law would not let him quitclaim or otherwise convey his homestead without his spouse joining the deed, regardless of whose name is on the title. The same joinder rule applies to a lady bird deed on homestead property. This protection exists to keep one spouse from signing away the family home without the other's knowledge, and it applies no matter which type of deed is used.

⚠ A Word on Uneven Deeds Some title companies hesitate to insure a lady bird deed that names only one child as beneficiary while leaving out siblings, since it can create hard feelings and future title questions. If Frank wants all three children to share the house, the deed should say so clearly and equally.

Why the Lady Bird Deed Fit Frank's Goal

Frank's actual goal was never to give his house away today. He wanted to avoid probate and make sure his three children split the house evenly after he's gone, without a court proceeding. A lady bird deed accomplishes exactly that, while a quitclaim deed accomplishes something much bigger and harder to undo: an immediate, present gift with tax and creditor consequences Frank never intended. Every deed still needs to be signed by Frank in front of two witnesses and a notary, then recorded in Volusia County where the property sits, whether it's a quitclaim deed or a lady bird deed. Frequently Asked Questions

Is a lady bird deed the same thing as an enhanced life estate deed?
Yes. Florida attorneys and title companies use the terms interchangeably to describe the same instrument.
Does Florida have a transfer-on-death deed instead?
No. Florida does not offer a statutory transfer-on-death deed for real estate the way some other states do. The lady bird deed, built on common law and long-standing title practice, is the tool Florida homeowners use to accomplish a similar result.
Can Frank still sell the house after signing a lady bird deed?
Yes. He keeps full control during his lifetime, including the right to sell, mortgage, lease, or revoke the deed, without needing permission from his children.
Will a lady bird deed avoid Florida documentary stamp tax if there's a mortgage on the house?
Because a lady bird deed transfers no present ownership interest, the Florida Department of Revenue's position is that no stamp tax applies to the mortgage balance, only the minimal recording fee. A present transfer by quitclaim deed, by contrast, can trigger stamp tax on an outstanding mortgage.
What happens if Frank needs nursing home care after signing a lady bird deed?
Because the deed transfers no interest until death, it is not treated as a disqualifying gift for Medicaid's five-year lookback period, and it does not expose the home to a Medicaid estate recovery lien in the way an outright gift can.
Is there a Florida statute that specifically authorizes lady bird deeds?
No. There is no Florida statute creating the lady bird deed. It is recognized through common law, Florida title standards, and decades of accepted practice among title insurers and real estate attorneys.

The Truestead Takeaway

Frank's instinct wasn't wrong, he just reached for the wrong tool. A quitclaim deed gives the house away today and carries real, permanent consequences: lost control, a Medicaid lookback problem, homestead and Save Our Homes exposure, a child's creditor or divorce reaching the property, a lost step-up in basis, and possibly documentary stamp tax on a mortgage. A lady bird deed lets Frank keep everything he has now and still get his kids the house without probate later. Every family's homestead, mortgage, and marital situation is different, so before signing anything, have a Florida attorney review your specific deed and title.
