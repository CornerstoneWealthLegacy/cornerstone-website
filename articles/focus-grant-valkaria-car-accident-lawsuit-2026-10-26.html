Grant-Valkaria Car Accident Lawsuit: What Florida Law Says

Quick Answer

A Grant-Valkaria car accident lawsuit follows the same statewide Florida rules as any other car crash claim in Brevard County: you generally have a limited window to file suit, your own PIP coverage pays first regardless of fault, and you can only sue the at-fault driver for pain and suffering if your injury meets Florida's serious injury threshold. There is no special local law for Grant-Valkaria, but local road conditions often shape how fault gets proven.

By Arthur Simpson, Esq. · FL Bar #529265 Florida Personal Injury Attorney October 26, 2026

Is there a special law for a Grant-Valkaria car accident lawsuit?

No. In my practice, I get this question a lot from folks in Grant-Valkaria, Micco, and the surrounding Brevard County communities: is there something different about filing a lawsuit here versus Melbourne or Palm Bay? The honest answer is no. A Grant-Valkaria car accident lawsuit is governed entirely by the same Florida statutes and court rules that apply anywhere in the state. What changes from town to town isn't the law, it's the facts: the roads, the traffic patterns, and the evidence available to prove what actually happened.

Grant-Valkaria's semi-rural character, with US-1 running through it as a major corridor for commuters and commercial truck traffic, means certain accident patterns show up more often here: rear-end collisions from commuter traffic, crashes at intersections with limited lighting, and weather-related wrecks when heavy rain causes flooding on low-lying roads. None of that changes the legal rules. It just changes what your attorney needs to investigate and document.

How long do I have to file a car accident lawsuit in Florida?

Florida law sets a statute of limitations, a strict deadline, for filing a car accident lawsuit. Florida's negligence statute of limitations changed significantly with the 2023 tort reform legislation (HB 837), and the rules that apply depend on when your accident occurred. Because this deadline is strict and unforgiving, and because it can bar your claim entirely if missed, I always tell people: do not try to calculate this deadline yourself from something you read online.

⚠ Don't guess at your deadline The filing deadline for a car accident claim depends on exactly when your crash happened and the specific facts of your case. Confirm your current deadline with a Florida attorney as soon as possible after a crash, rather than relying on an estimate.

What I can tell you with confidence is this: the clock starts running from the date of the accident, evidence disappears quickly, and insurance companies are not going to remind you when your time is running short. The sooner you have a Florida attorney confirm your actual deadline, the more options you preserve.

Florida's no-fault system: how PIP affects a Grant-Valkaria claim

Florida is a no-fault insurance state, which surprises a lot of people after their first accident. Every driver registered in Florida, including everyone in Grant-Valkaria, is required to carry Personal Injury Protection (PIP) coverage, with a $10,000 minimum. After a crash, your own PIP policy pays your initial medical bills and a portion of lost wages, regardless of who caused the accident.

  • PIP pays first, before you look to the at-fault driver's insurance
  • It covers a percentage of your medical expenses and lost wages, up to your policy limit
  • It applies even if the other driver was 100% at fault

The idea behind no-fault is to get medical bills paid quickly without waiting to sort out blame. But $10,000 doesn't go far after a serious crash, emergency room visits, imaging, physical therapy, and lost work time can exceed that limit fast. That's where the next question becomes critical.

When can I sue the other driver? Florida's serious injury threshold

Because Florida is a no-fault state, you cannot simply sue the other driver for pain and suffering any time you're hurt in a crash. Florida Statutes § 627.737 sets a threshold: to step outside the no-fault system and pursue a claim against the at-fault driver for non-economic damages, your injury generally must meet one of these categories:

  • Significant and permanent loss of an important bodily function
  • A permanent injury within a reasonable degree of medical probability
  • Significant and permanent scarring or disfigurement
  • Death

If your injuries meet this threshold, you may be able to pursue the at-fault driver (and their insurance) for both economic damages (medical bills beyond PIP, lost wages, future medical care, property damage) and non-economic damages (pain and suffering, emotional distress, loss of enjoyment of life). Whether a specific injury qualifies is a medical and legal determination that should be made with your doctor's records and an attorney's review, not guessed at from a checklist online.

What if I was partly at fault for the accident?

Florida uses a comparative negligence system, meaning fault in a car accident can be divided between the parties involved based on each person's contribution to causing the crash. Florida's comparative negligence law was also affected by the 2023 tort reform, which shifted the state toward a modified comparative negligence standard with a bar at a certain fault percentage for the plaintiff.

What this means for you: If you were partially responsible for an accident, that doesn't automatically prevent you from recovering compensation, but it can reduce the amount you're entitled to, and in some circumstances bar recovery altogether depending on your percentage of fault. Because the exact current rule and percentage threshold matter enormously to your case, this is something a Florida attorney needs to evaluate based on your specific accident facts.

In practice, this means the insurance company will often try to shift blame onto you, even partially, because it directly reduces what they have to pay. Strong evidence, like police reports, witness statements, and dashcam or surveillance footage, matters enormously here.

What evidence matters most in a Grant-Valkaria crash case?

Grant-Valkaria's roads create some specific evidentiary challenges. US-1 carries heavy commercial and commuter traffic through the area, intersections can have limited lighting at night, and heavy rain creates flooding on certain stretches of road. All of this can factor into who's found at fault and how.

  • Skid marks and road debris fade or get cleared within days
  • Witness memories become less reliable as time passes
  • Surveillance and dashcam footage is often automatically overwritten within days or weeks
  • Vehicle damage patterns can help reconstruct how the collision occurred
  • Weather and lighting conditions at the time of the crash can support or undercut a fault argument

This is why I tell clients that the first days after a crash matter just as much as the lawsuit itself. Evidence that would have proven your case can simply disappear if no one preserves it quickly.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need a lawyer for a car accident in Grant-Valkaria, or can I handle it myself?
You're not required to hire an attorney, but insurance companies have adjusters and lawyers working to minimize payouts, and Florida's serious injury threshold and comparative negligence rules can be complicated to navigate alone. Many Florida personal injury attorneys, including our firm, review accident cases at no upfront cost to you.
What if the at-fault driver doesn't have insurance?
Florida does not require drivers to carry bodily injury liability coverage in the same way many states do, which means uninsured and underinsured drivers are a real risk on roads like US-1. If you carry uninsured/underinsured motorist (UM/UIM) coverage on your own policy, that coverage may be available to you, and this should be reviewed with an attorney.
Does my PIP coverage pay for everything after a crash?
No. Florida's minimum PIP coverage is $10,000, and it only covers a percentage of medical bills and lost wages, not the full amount and not pain and suffering. Serious accidents often exceed PIP limits quickly, which is when pursuing the at-fault driver becomes relevant.
How long does a car accident lawsuit typically take in Florida?
Timelines vary widely depending on the severity of injuries, whether liability is disputed, and whether the case settles or goes to trial. There's no fixed timeline under Florida law, and your attorney can give you a more realistic estimate once they understand your specific case.
Is Grant-Valkaria part of Brevard County for court purposes?
Yes. Grant-Valkaria is located in Brevard County, Florida, and lawsuits arising from accidents there are typically filed in Brevard County's court system, following the same Florida rules of civil procedure used statewide.

If you've been in a car accident in Grant-Valkaria, the law that applies is Florida law, not anything unique to your zip code, but the facts of local roads, traffic, and weather can shape how your case gets proven. Because filing deadlines changed under Florida's 2023 tort reform and because comparative negligence rules can significantly affect what you recover, don't rely on general information, including this article, to make decisions about your specific situation. The sensible next step is to have a Florida attorney review your accident, your injuries, and your insurance coverage so you understand your actual deadline and your actual options before time or evidence slips away.
