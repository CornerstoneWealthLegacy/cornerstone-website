Nursing Home Medicaid in Flagler County: Where to Apply and What to Expect

Quick Answer

Flagler County families apply for nursing home Medicaid the same way every Florida county does: a financial application through DCF's ACCESS system and a medical level-of-care review through CARES, with ElderSource (the local Aging and Disability Resource Center) as the best first phone call for guidance.

By Arthur Simpson, Esq. · FL Bar #529265 Florida Elder Law Attorney October 6, 2026

The Two Decisions Every Florida Family Faces, Including Leonard's

Leonard is 86 and lives in Palm Coast. He is a composite example, not an actual Truestead client, but his situation is one I see often: a widower whose health has declined, a son in Atlanta trying to manage things by phone, and a family that has never dealt with the Department of Children and Families, CARES, or a nursing home business office before. For a family like Leonard's, getting from "he needs nursing home care" to "Medicaid is approved and paying" runs through two separate decisions, made by two separate state agencies.

The first is medical: does Leonard actually need nursing-facility-level care? That determination is made by CARES, the Comprehensive Assessment and Review for Long-Term Care Services program operated by Florida's Department of Elder Affairs. CARES is a federally required screening for anyone seeking Medicaid payment for nursing home placement or certain home and community-based waiver services. A nurse or assessor reviews the person's functional and medical needs, at no cost to the family, and recommends the least restrictive setting that is still appropriate, whether that is a nursing facility or services at home.

The second is financial: does Leonard meet Florida's income and asset limits for Medicaid? That determination belongs to DCF, through the ACCESS Florida system. Applications are filed online, and DCF schedules the eligibility interview by phone rather than requiring an in-person visit to an office. Both pieces, medical and financial, have to come together before Medicaid starts paying the nursing home. Your Front Door: ElderSource and the Elder Helpline

Before Leonard's son even opens the ACCESS website, the single most useful call a Flagler County family can make is to ElderSource, the Aging and Disability Resource Center (ADRC) that serves Flagler County as part of a seven-county Northeast Florida region covering Baker, Clay, Duval, Flagler, Nassau, St. Johns, and Volusia counties. ElderSource operates the regional Elder Helpline, staffed Monday through Friday during business hours, and its job is to point families toward the right services, whether that is home-delivered meals, a home-care Medicaid waiver, caregiver support, or simply an explanation of how CARES and DCF fit together.

For families whose loved one is not yet in a nursing facility but is struggling at home, ElderSource is also the entry point for Florida's home and community-based Medicaid waiver, the program that pays for in-home care instead of institutional placement. That waiver has a statewide waitlist, and ElderSource can tell a Flagler County family where a parent currently stands on it. For a family like Leonard's, where nursing home placement already looks likely, ElderSource can still save time by explaining what CARES will be looking for and what paperwork to have ready.

Local Tip: Call ElderSource early, even before a hospital discharge. A warm handoff from a hospital discharge planner to CARES and ElderSource often moves faster than starting cold from home.

What Care Costs in the Palm Coast Area, in Plain Terms

Private-pay nursing home care in the Palm Coast, Bunnell, and Flagler Beach area is billed daily, and the full private rate is substantially higher than what Medicaid actually pays the facility once a resident is approved. I cannot confirm a specific current daily or monthly rate for Flagler County facilities in the research available as of this writing, and local rates shift from year to year and facility to facility, so I won't guess at a number here. What matters for planning purposes is the structure: a family paying privately pays the facility's full private rate, while a family on Medicaid pays only a required monthly contribution toward cost of care (sometimes called patient responsibility), with Medicaid covering the balance directly to the facility at the Medicaid rate.

That gap between the private rate and the Medicaid rate is exactly why timing matters. A family that understands the application and look-back rules early, rather than after months of private-pay spending, generally has more room to plan. Our firm's separate articles on spend-down and the look-back period go into that math in detail.

Finding a Certified Bed and Having the "Medicaid Pending" Conversation

Not every nursing facility accepts Medicaid, and not every bed within a Medicaid-certified facility is Medicaid-certified. The state maintains a facility locator through FloridaHealthFinder that lets a family search licensed and Medicaid-certified nursing homes by county, including in Flagler County. I'm not going to name specific facilities here, since that list changes and a family's choice should come from direct research and a tour, but the locator is the right public tool to confirm, facility by facility, whether a given home accepts Medicaid residents and has availability.

Once Leonard's family identifies a facility, the next conversation is with that facility's business office about "Medicaid pending" status. Many Florida nursing homes will admit a resident before Medicaid eligibility is finalized, with the understanding that the family (or the resident's funds) covers charges until approval comes through, at which point Medicaid payments apply retroactively to the appropriate date under Florida's rules. Business offices handle this constantly and can explain their own facility's specific documentation requirements, deposit expectations, and how they coordinate with the pending application. This is also where the difference between skilled rehabilitation days (typically following a hospital stay, often covered short-term by Medicare) and long-term custodial care (the ongoing care Medicaid is built to pay for) becomes very real to a family, since those are billed and covered under entirely different rules.

Three Local Wrinkles Families in Flagler County Run Into

  • Hospital-to-rehab-to-long-term-care patterns. A common path for Flagler County seniors is a hospitalization, followed by a short Medicare-covered rehab stay, followed by a recognition that the person cannot safely return home. Families often don't start thinking about Medicaid until that rehab stay is ending, which compresses the planning timeline considerably.
  • Seasonal and part-time residency. Palm Coast and Flagler Beach draw seasonal residents and families who split time between Florida and a northern state, as Leonard's son does with his trips from Atlanta. Florida Medicaid generally requires Florida residency, and a part-time or seasonal living pattern can raise questions during the DCF interview that a full-time Florida resident wouldn't face.
  • Long-distance family coordination. When the primary decision-maker lives out of state, as is common in Flagler County, gathering financial documents, scheduling the CARES assessment, and handling the DCF phone interview all have to happen without anyone physically present locally for much of it. This is exactly the kind of situation where a durable power of attorney, set up correctly in advance, makes the paperwork side of Medicaid planning far less stressful.

A Realistic Timeline for a Flagler County Family

For a family like Leonard's, the sequence generally looks like this: a first call to ElderSource or directly to CARES to start the level-of-care assessment, submission of the financial application through ACCESS, a scheduled phone interview with a DCF eligibility worker, and then a waiting period while DCF gathers verification of income, assets, and medical need. Florida's administrative standard calls for DCF to decide a complete application within 45 days, though that clock depends on how quickly the family supplies complete documentation and how complicated the financial picture is.

If the CARES assessment and the DCF financial review both come back favorably, Medicaid eligibility is approved, and the facility can bill Medicaid going forward (and, in Medicaid-pending situations, retroactively). If DCF denies the application, the family has the right to request a hearing through Florida's Office of Appeal Hearings, which reviews DCF eligibility decisions independently.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does Leonard need to go to a DCF office in Flagler County to apply?
No. Florida Medicaid applications are filed statewide through the ACCESS Florida online system, and DCF schedules the required eligibility interview by phone rather than requiring an in-person office visit.
What does CARES actually decide?
CARES, run by the Department of Elder Affairs, decides whether a person's medical and functional needs meet Florida's standard for nursing-facility-level care, which is separate from and in addition to DCF's financial eligibility review.
Is ElderSource the same thing as DCF or CARES?
No. ElderSource is the Aging and Disability Resource Center serving Flagler County and the surrounding region; it helps families find and understand services and waivers but does not make the DCF financial determination or the CARES medical determination itself.
How do we find out if a specific Flagler County nursing home accepts Medicaid?
The state's FloridaHealthFinder facility locator lets families search licensed, Medicaid-certified nursing homes by county, and the facility's own business office can confirm current bed availability and Medicaid-certified bed status.
What does 'Medicaid pending' mean at a nursing home?
It generally means the facility admits the resident before Medicaid eligibility is finalized, with charges handled by the family or the resident's funds in the meantime, and Medicaid payment applying once approval comes through.
When should a family involve a Florida elder law attorney?
Generally as early as possible once nursing home placement looks likely, since look-back rules, spend-down strategy, and asset protection options are far more flexible before significant private-pay spending or asset transfers have already occurred.

The Truestead Takeaway

Leonard's situation is a composite, but it reflects what I see constantly in Flagler County: a family that is medically and emotionally ready to act but has never touched the machinery of CARES, DCF, or a nursing home business office. The good news is that the machinery is the same everywhere in Florida, and Flagler County families have a strong local resource in ElderSource to help translate it. The honest next step for a family in Leonard's position is to start the CARES and ACCESS processes promptly, confirm Medicaid-certified bed availability directly with facilities, and have a Florida elder law attorney review the financial picture before much private-pay spending occurs, since that review is what turns a confusing process into a manageable one.
