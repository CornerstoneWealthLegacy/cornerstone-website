Florida Probate Attorney Fee Calculator: What You'll Actually Pay

Quick Answer

Florida law sets a presumed-reasonable attorney fee schedule based on the estate's value — generally starting around 3% of the first $1 million — but this schedule is a default, not a mandatory price tag, and most straightforward Florida estates are actually handled for a flat fee well below it.

By Arthur Simpson, Esq. · FL Bar #529265 Florida Estate Planning Attorney July 31, 2026

How a Florida Probate Attorney Fee Calculator Actually Works

When people search for a "florida probate attorney fee calculator," what they're really looking for is a way to estimate what an estate will owe in attorney fees before they commit to hiring anyone. In my practice, I tell clients the honest answer up front: Florida has a statutory formula, but it's a starting point for negotiation, not a bill that gets handed to you automatically.

Florida Statutes § 733.6171 sets out a tiered fee schedule based on what's called the compensable value of the estate — generally the inventory value of the probate assets plus any income the estate earns during administration. Homestead property is typically excluded from that calculation, which matters a great deal for Florida families since the home is often the largest asset.

The statutory schedule is presumed reasonable, and it generally works like this:

  • A flat fee for very small estates (roughly $1,500 for estates under $40,000)
  • Approximately 3% on the first $1 million of compensable value
  • Declining percentages (roughly 2.5%, then 2%) on value above $1 million, up to higher thresholds
  • As low as 1% on the portion of very large estates above $10 million

So for a $500,000 estate, running that math against the 3% tier produces a statutory fee in the neighborhood of $15,000. That number often surprises people — and it should, because in my experience it is rarely what a Florida family actually pays for a routine, uncontested administration.

Presumed Reasonable Doesn't Mean Mandatory

This is the single most important thing for a Florida reader to understand: the § 733.6171 schedule describes a fee that a court will presume is reasonable if it's challenged — it is not a fee the attorney is entitled to charge automatically. Florida law requires the attorney to disclose this distinction to the personal representative before fees are calculated under the statutory schedule.

Florida law also expressly allows the attorney, the personal representative, and the people who will bear the cost of the fee to agree on a different compensation arrangement — including a flat fee or hourly rate — under § 733.6171(2)(a). If that alternative arrangement is properly disclosed and no one objects under the applicable Florida Probate Rule, it governs instead of the percentage schedule.

What this means in practice: Most routine Florida probate administrations — where there's no litigation, no contested will, no complicated tax issue, and no real estate sale fight — are handled by experienced probate attorneys for a flat fee, commonly in the range of a few thousand dollars, rather than a percentage of estate value. The statutory percentage schedule tends to apply mainly to larger or more complex estates, or when a fee dispute actually goes before a judge.

Summary Administration: A Faster, Cheaper Path for Smaller Estates

Not every Florida estate needs full, formal administration. Florida offers a streamlined process called summary administration for smaller estates or older deaths, and it typically costs far less than formal probate because there's no personal representative to formally administer the estate long-term.

An important, recent development affects this threshold: for deaths occurring on or after July 1, 2026, summary administration is available for estates with a value of $150,000 or less. For deaths before that date, the older $75,000 threshold still applies. Summary administration is also available, regardless of estate size, where the decedent has been dead for more than two years.

Because summary administration involves less court supervision and a shorter process, Florida probate attorneys frequently handle it for a flat fee — often in the range of a few thousand dollars — rather than under the full percentage schedule that applies to formal administration.

What Adds Cost Beyond the Base Fee

Whether an estate is billed by flat fee or by the statutory schedule, certain services are typically considered extraordinary and billed separately, on top of the base fee. These commonly include:

  • Will contests or other litigation among beneficiaries
  • Contested claims from creditors
  • Sale of real estate owned by the estate
  • Federal estate tax return preparation or tax controversies
  • Contested guardianship or elective share disputes tied to the estate

Court filing fees, publication costs for creditor notices, appraisal fees, and bond premiums (if a bond is required) are separate from attorney fees entirely and should be discussed with your attorney as part of the overall estimate.

⚠ A Word of Caution on Online Calculators Generic online "probate fee calculators" that simply multiply an estate value by a percentage can be misleading. They rarely account for the homestead exclusion, the difference between summary and formal administration, or the fact that Florida law favors negotiated flat fees for most routine estates. Treat any online estimate as a rough starting point — the only reliable number comes from a Florida probate attorney reviewing the actual estate.

How to Get a Real Fee Estimate for Your Florida Estate

What I tell Florida families is this: before you accept a percentage-based fee, ask two questions. First, is this estate eligible for summary administration? Second, will the attorney handle this on a flat-fee basis given the actual work involved, rather than defaulting to the statutory schedule? A reputable Florida probate attorney should be able to answer both clearly, in writing, at the outset.

Compensable value, homestead status, whether there's a will, how many beneficiaries are involved, and whether any disputes are brewing all affect the real-world cost far more than a simple percentage formula ever could.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is the Florida statutory probate fee schedule mandatory?
No. Under Florida Statutes § 733.6171, the percentage-based schedule is presumed reasonable if challenged in court, but it is not mandatory. Florida law allows the attorney, personal representative, and affected parties to agree to a different fee arrangement, such as a flat fee.
How much does a typical Florida probate attorney charge?
For routine, uncontested estates, many Florida probate attorneys charge a flat fee that is often well below what the statutory percentage schedule would produce, since the bulk of the work is administrative rather than litigation. Larger or contested estates are more likely to be billed under the statutory schedule or hourly.
Does the probate attorney fee include the value of my home?
Generally, no. Florida homestead property is typically excluded from the 'compensable value' used to calculate statutory probate attorney fees, though the specifics depend on how the homestead passes and should be confirmed with an attorney.
What is summary administration and does it cost less?
Summary administration is a streamlined Florida probate process available for smaller estates or when the decedent has been dead more than two years. It typically costs significantly less than formal administration because it involves less court supervision and paperwork.
What is the new summary administration threshold in Florida?
For deaths on or after July 1, 2026, summary administration is available for Florida estates valued at $150,000 or less. For deaths before that date, the prior threshold of $75,000 still applies.
Are there costs besides attorney fees in a Florida probate case?
Yes. Court filing fees, required creditor notice publication costs, appraisal fees, and any bond premiums are separate from attorney fees and should be included in a full cost estimate from your attorney.

The Truestead Takeaway

A Florida probate attorney fee calculator can give you a rough sense of the statutory schedule, but the number it produces is a legal ceiling, not necessarily what you'll pay — Florida law expressly allows flat-fee and alternative arrangements that better reflect the actual work involved, especially for smaller or uncontested estates and those newly eligible for summary administration. The most useful next step is a direct conversation with a Florida probate attorney who can tell you, based on your specific estate's value, assets, and complexity, what a fair fee actually looks like.
