Lady Bird Deed Attorney: How Enhanced Life Estate Deeds Work in Florida

Quick Answer

A Florida lady bird deed (enhanced life estate deed) lets you keep full ownership and control of your home during your lifetime, including the right to sell or mortgage it, while automatically transferring it to your chosen beneficiaries at death without probate. It is not created by a specific Florida statute, but it is well recognized under Florida common law and routinely accepted by Florida title insurance companies.

By Arthur Simpson, Esq. · FL Bar #529265 Florida Real Estate Attorney October 9, 2026

What does a lady bird deed attorney actually do?

When Floridians search for a lady bird deed attorney, what they usually need is someone who can confirm whether this tool fits their situation, draft the deed correctly, and make sure it will not create problems for their family later. In my practice, that means reviewing how the property is titled, whether a spouse or minor child is involved, how the deed interacts with any existing trust or will, and confirming the deed language will satisfy both the county recorder and a future title insurer.

A lady bird deed, sometimes called an enhanced life estate deed, is a type of deed that reserves a life estate for the current owner, but with an important twist: unlike a traditional life estate deed, the owner keeps the power to sell, mortgage, lease, or even revoke the arrangement entirely during their lifetime, without needing permission from the people named to inherit the property. At the owner's death, the property passes directly to the named beneficiaries (called remaindermen) outside of probate.

Is a lady bird deed actually legal in Florida?

This is one of the most common questions I get, and the honest answer deserves some nuance. Florida does not have a statute that specifically creates or authorizes the lady bird deed. There is no "Florida Lady Bird Deed Act." Instead, the instrument rests on long-standing Florida common law, including the Florida Supreme Court's 1917 decision in Oglesby v. Lee, which upheld a deed reserving lifetime possession along with the power to resell the property.

Beyond that case law, lady bird deeds are executed under Florida's general deed framework, F.S. § 689.01, which governs how deeds must be signed, witnessed, and recorded. Execution requires the grantor's signature in the presence of two subscribing witnesses, notarization, and recording in the county where the property is located. Since January 1, 2024, Florida law (F.S. § 695.26) has also required that the deed include the signer's printed name beneath the signature and the grantor's post office address on the face of the document, a formatting requirement that a careless DIY form can easily miss.

Perhaps the most practical proof that lady bird deeds work in Florida is this: Florida title insurance companies routinely accept properly drafted lady bird deeds and issue title policies based on them. In real estate practice, a title insurer's willingness to insure is often the clearest real-world signal that a deed creates valid, marketable title.

Does a lady bird deed work on Florida homestead property?

Florida's homestead protections are some of the strongest in the country, and they come with real restrictions on how homestead property can be devised (left by will) when the owner is survived by a spouse or minor child. Article X, Section 4 of the Florida Constitution and F.S. § 732.4015 make a devise that violates these restrictions void, not just challengeable.

The prevailing view among Florida estate planning attorneys, and the way the instrument is generally used in practice, is that a lady bird deed on homestead property is not a "devise" at all, and so it does not run into those restrictions. The reasoning: the owner retains full ownership, control, and the power to revoke during life, and the transfer happens automatically by the deed's own terms at death rather than through a will.

⚠ Spousal Joinder Matters If the homestead property is owned by a married person, Florida law generally requires the spouse to join in any deed affecting that homestead, including a lady bird deed. A deed executed without the required spousal joinder on homestead property can be voidable. This is exactly the kind of issue a lady bird deed attorney should catch before the deed is signed and recorded, not after.

How does a lady bird deed affect Medicaid planning?

Many Floridians come to me specifically because they have heard that a lady bird deed can help with Medicaid planning, and as Florida law currently stands, that is generally accurate. Florida's Medicaid estate recovery program, authorized under F.S. § 409.9101, recovers benefits paid by filing a claim against the probate estate under Chapter 733. Because property that passes through a lady bird deed goes directly to the named beneficiaries at death and never becomes part of the probate estate, it generally falls outside the reach of that recovery claim.

It is worth noting that Florida has chosen not to expand estate recovery to reach non-probate assets, which is not true in every state. This is one reason the lady bird deed has become such a popular planning tool here specifically, and why out-of-state approaches to similar deeds do not always translate directly to Florida.

What are the real pros and cons of using a lady bird deed?

A lady bird deed is not the right fit for every family or every piece of property, and a good lady bird deed attorney will walk through both sides with you rather than simply handing you a form.

  • Benefits: avoids probate on the property, keeps the owner in full control (sell, refinance, or revoke) during life, allows a step up in tax basis for beneficiaries at death, and is generally recognized as protecting against Florida Medicaid estate recovery.
  • Limitations and risks: it only controls the one asset named in the deed, it does not avoid probate for other assets, it can create confusion if beneficiary designations conflict with other estate planning documents, and if drafted without attention to homestead and spousal rules, it can be challenged or voidable.
Why DIY forms are risky here Because there is no official state-approved lady bird deed form, and no statute spelling out exactly what language is required, the deed's validity depends entirely on correct drafting, correct execution under F.S. § 689.01 and § 695.26, and correct handling of homestead and spousal issues. A generic online form will not know whether your property is homestead, whether you are married, or how this deed should coordinate with your existing trust or will.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need a lady bird deed attorney, or can I use an online form?
Because Florida has no official lady bird deed statute or state-approved form, correct drafting and execution depend on an attorney's familiarity with F.S. § 689.01, the 2024 signature and address requirements under F.S. § 695.26, and Florida homestead and spousal rules, all of which a generic online form cannot account for.
Can I sell or refinance my house after signing a lady bird deed?
Yes. One of the defining features of a lady bird deed, compared to an ordinary life estate deed, is that the owner keeps full control, including the right to sell, mortgage, or revoke the deed during life without needing permission from the named beneficiaries.
What happens to the lady bird deed if I change my mind?
Because the owner retains full ownership and control during life, a lady bird deed can generally be revoked or changed by the owner, which is a key difference from an irrevocable transfer.
Does a lady bird deed avoid probate for my whole estate?
No. A lady bird deed only affects the specific property described in that deed. Other assets, such as bank accounts, vehicles, or additional real estate, will still need their own planning to avoid probate.
Will a lady bird deed protect my home from Florida Medicaid estate recovery?
Under current Florida law, property passing through a lady bird deed generally avoids becoming a probate asset, and Florida's Medicaid estate recovery program recovers through a claim against the probate estate, so this structure is commonly used for that purpose. Your specific situation should still be reviewed, since estate recovery rules can be fact-specific.
Is a lady bird deed better than putting my house in a trust?
It depends on your goals. A lady bird deed is simpler and less expensive for a single piece of real estate, while a revocable trust under F.S. Chapter 736 can address multiple assets and more complex family situations. A Florida attorney can help you compare the two based on your actual property and family circumstances.

The Truestead Takeaway

In my practice, the lady bird deed is one of the most useful and underappreciated tools available to Florida homeowners, but its power comes from careful, Florida-specific drafting, not from a generic template. Because the instrument rests on common law and title company practice rather than a dedicated statute, the details, homestead status, spousal joinder, execution formalities, and how it coordinates with your broader estate plan, are exactly where things go right or wrong. If you are considering a lady bird deed for your Florida home, the sensible next step is to have your specific property and family situation reviewed by a Florida attorney before anything is signed or recorded.
