Indian Harbour Beach Car Accident Lawsuit: What Florida Law Actually Says

Quick Answer

If you were hurt in a car accident in Indian Harbour Beach, Florida law generally gives you two years from the date of the crash to file a lawsuit, you must have a serious injury to sue the at-fault driver outside the no-fault system, and your recovery can be reduced, or barred entirely, depending on your own percentage of fault.

By Arthur Simpson, Esq. · FL Bar #529265 Florida Personal Injury Attorney November 8, 2026

What an "Indian Harbour Beach Car Accident Lawsuit" Really Means

There's no special court or separate set of laws just for Indian Harbour Beach. When someone searches for an Indian Harbour Beach car accident lawsuit, what they usually want to know is how a Florida injury claim works when the crash happened on local roads like State Road A1A, Eau Gallie Boulevard, or one of the residential streets that feed into them. Indian Harbour Beach sits in Brevard County, and crash volume here reflects a lot of seasonal tourist traffic, commuters heading to Patrick Space Force Base, and the narrow, busy barrier-island geography that leaves little margin for error.

A lawsuit over an Indian Harbour Beach car accident is filed under the same statewide Florida Statutes that govern every car accident claim in the state, with venue (the court location) typically in the Eighteenth Judicial Circuit, which covers Brevard County. What changes from case to case isn't the law, it's the facts: how the crash happened, who was at fault, how serious the injuries are, and what insurance is available.

How Long Do You Have to File? The Current Statute of Limitations

This is the single most important deadline in any Florida car accident case, and it changed significantly in recent years. Under Florida Statute § 95.11(4)(a), the statute of limitations for a personal injury lawsuit arising from a car accident is generally two years from the date of the crash, for accidents that occurred on or after March 24, 2023. Accidents that happened before that date are generally still governed by the prior four-year deadline.

If a crash results in a death, Florida's wrongful death statute of limitations, found at § 95.11(4)(d), generally runs two years from the date of death, which is not always the same date as the accident itself.

⚠ Don't rely on memory of the old deadline Florida's personal injury statute of limitations was cut from four years to two years as part of 2023 tort reform (HB 837). If you or a family member were in a crash and you've been assuming you have years to decide, confirm your actual deadline with a Florida attorney. Missing the filing deadline generally bars the claim permanently, regardless of how strong it is.

Florida's No-Fault System and the "Serious Injury" Threshold

Florida is a no-fault insurance state, which means your own Personal Injury Protection (PIP) coverage is generally the first source of payment for medical bills and lost wages after a crash, regardless of who caused it. But PIP coverage is limited, and it doesn't compensate you for pain and suffering.

To step outside the no-fault system and bring a lawsuit directly against the at-fault driver, Florida law requires that you meet the "serious injury" threshold described in Florida Statute § 627.737. Generally, this means showing one of the following:

  • Significant and permanent loss of an important bodily function
  • Permanent injury within a reasonable degree of medical probability
  • Significant and permanent scarring or disfigurement
  • Death

Whether an injury meets this threshold is a medical and legal question that depends heavily on documentation from treating physicians, which is one reason prompt, consistent medical treatment after any crash matters so much.

What If You Were Partly at Fault?

Many real-world crashes involve some dispute about who did what. Florida follows a modified comparative negligence rule, codified at § 768.81. Under this rule:

  • If you are found 50 percent or less at fault for the accident, you can still recover damages, but your award is reduced by your percentage of fault.
  • If you are found more than 50 percent at fault, Florida law generally bars you from recovering any damages at all.

This makes early evidence gathering, such as police reports, witness statements, dashcam footage, and photos of the scene, especially important in any Indian Harbour Beach car accident claim, since fault percentages are often contested by insurance companies.

Reporting the Crash and Building Your Case

Under Florida Statute § 316.065, a car accident generally must be reported to law enforcement if it results in injury, death, or property damage that appears to exceed a statutory threshold. In Indian Harbour Beach, that typically means contacting the Indian Harbour Beach Police Department or, depending on the location, the Brevard County Sheriff's Office. The resulting crash report becomes an important piece of evidence in any later claim or lawsuit.

Practical note In my practice, I tell clients that the strength of a car accident case is usually built in the first days and weeks after the crash, through medical treatment records, the official crash report, and documentation of property damage and lost income. Waiting too long to seek treatment or to preserve evidence can make an otherwise solid case harder to prove later.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need to file my Indian Harbour Beach car accident lawsuit in a specific court?
Car accident lawsuits arising from crashes in Indian Harbour Beach are generally filed in the Eighteenth Judicial Circuit Court for Brevard County, though smaller claims may fall under county court jurisdiction depending on the amount in dispute.
How long do I actually have to sue after a car accident in Florida?
For accidents on or after March 24, 2023, Florida law generally allows two years from the date of the crash to file a personal injury lawsuit under Florida Statute § 95.11(4)(a). Accidents before that date may still fall under the older four-year rule, so it's worth confirming your specific deadline with an attorney.
Can I sue the other driver even though Florida is a no-fault state?
Yes, but only if your injuries meet the 'serious injury' threshold defined in Florida Statute § 627.737, such as a significant permanent injury, permanent loss of a bodily function, significant scarring, or death. Otherwise, your medical bills and lost wages are generally handled through your own PIP coverage.
What happens if the other driver says the accident was partly my fault?
Florida follows modified comparative negligence under § 768.81. If you're found 50 percent or less at fault, your damages are reduced by your fault percentage, but if you're found more than 50 percent at fault, you generally cannot recover damages at all.
What if my loved one died in a car accident in Indian Harbour Beach?
Florida's wrongful death statute of limitations under § 95.11(4)(d) generally runs two years from the date of death, which may be different from the date of the accident itself. These cases involve their own specific rules about who can file and what damages are available.
Is a lawsuit always necessary after a car accident?
No. Many car accident claims resolve through insurance negotiations without ever becoming a filed lawsuit. A lawsuit typically becomes necessary when liability is disputed, injuries are serious, or an insurer won't offer a fair settlement.

The Truestead Takeaway

An Indian Harbour Beach car accident lawsuit follows the same Florida Statutes that apply anywhere in the state, but the deadlines are shorter than many people realize, the serious injury threshold matters more than most drivers expect, and comparative fault can significantly change what you recover. If you or a family member were hurt in a crash on A1A or anywhere else in Indian Harbour Beach, the sensible next step is to have your specific accident date, injuries, and insurance situation reviewed by a Florida attorney before any deadline passes.
