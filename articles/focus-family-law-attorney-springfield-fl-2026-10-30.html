Family Law Attorney Springfield FL: A Florida Resident's Guide to Divorce, Time-Sharing, and Support

Quick Answer

Florida family law (divorce, time-sharing, child support, and alimony) is governed by Chapter 61 of the Florida Statutes and applies the same way statewide, so whether you are near Panama City's Springfield community or anywhere else in Florida, what matters most is finding a Florida-licensed attorney, ideally Florida Bar board certified in marital and family law, who regularly practices in your local circuit.

What Florida Family Law Actually Covers

Family law in Florida is broader than just divorce. It generally includes:

  • Dissolution of marriage (divorce), including contested and uncontested cases
  • Parenting plans and time-sharing, which is the modern legal term replacing "custody"
  • Child support, calculated under statutory guidelines
  • Alimony (spousal support), which has strict limits under current law
  • Paternity actions for unmarried parents establishing legal rights and obligations
  • Modifications and enforcement of existing support or time-sharing orders
  • Domestic violence injunctions (restraining orders)

Every one of these areas is governed by Chapter 61, Florida Statutes, which is the backbone of Florida family law practice.

Alimony in Florida: What Changed and What Didn't

There's been a lot of noise online about alimony reform, so let me be clear about what's actually true. Florida eliminated permanent alimony through legislation that took effect in 2023, and that remains the law today. Under Section 61.08, Florida Statutes, courts can no longer award permanent, open-ended alimony in new divorce cases. Instead, alimony is now time-limited, with duration generally tied to the length of the marriage, and courts must evaluate factors like need, ability to pay, and the standard of living established during the marriage.

Good to know: Despite viral social media claims, there was no new Florida alimony formula or automatic equal time-sharing law enacted for 2026. The framework set in 2023 remains the controlling law. If you see headlines claiming otherwise, verify with a licensed Florida family law attorney before relying on them.

Child Support and Time-Sharing: The Current Framework

Child support in Florida is calculated under guidelines set out in Section 61.30, Florida Statutes. These guidelines consider both parents' incomes, the number of overnights each parent has with the children, health insurance costs, and childcare expenses. Courts have discretion to deviate from the guideline amount, but they increasingly require clear, documented justification before doing so.

On the paternity side, procedural updates affecting support establishment for unmarried parents took effect in mid-2026, but the underlying guideline framework under Section 61.30 has not changed. If you have a paternity or support case in progress, it's worth confirming with your attorney whether any procedural update affects your filing.

As for "custody," Florida dropped that term years ago in favor of parenting plans and time-sharing schedules, which spell out exactly when each parent has the children and how major decisions (education, healthcare, religion) will be made. Florida courts also continue to apply established rules for equitable distribution of marital assets and debts under Chapter 61.

How to Choose the Right Family Law Attorney

Not every Florida lawyer who handles a divorce is equally qualified. The Florida Bar offers a formal credential called board certification in marital and family law, and it is genuinely meaningful. To earn it, an attorney must have practiced law for at least five years, devote a substantial portion of their practice to marital and family law, complete specialized continuing legal education, and pass a rigorous written exam covering substantive law, procedure, evidence, and ethics. Fewer than seven percent of Florida lawyers hold this certification.

When evaluating a family law attorney, whether near Springfield, FL or elsewhere in the state, I'd suggest asking:

  • Are you licensed by The Florida Bar and in good standing?
  • Do you regularly appear in the local circuit court where my case will be filed?
  • Are you board certified in marital and family law, or how much of your practice is dedicated to this area?
  • How do you typically communicate with clients during a case, and what does the process look like start to finish?
⚠ A note on fast-moving court changes: Florida circuit courts continue expanding e-filing and virtual hearings for family division matters. Procedures for filing motions to modify or enforce support can vary by circuit, so confirm current local rules with your attorney or the clerk of court before a filing deadline.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is Springfield, FL its own judicial circuit?
No. Springfield is a small city in Bay County, Florida, part of the Panama City area, and family law cases there are typically handled in the 14th Judicial Circuit. Florida's family law statutes apply the same way statewide, but filing and procedure happen at the circuit level.
Does Florida still allow permanent alimony?
No. Since 2023, Florida courts can no longer award permanent alimony in new divorce cases. Under Section 61.08, Florida Statutes, alimony is now time-limited, with duration generally tied to the length of the marriage.
What is the difference between custody and time-sharing in Florida?
Florida law no longer uses the term custody. Instead, courts establish a parenting plan and time-sharing schedule that spells out when each parent has the children and how major decisions will be made.
How is child support calculated in Florida?
Child support is calculated under statutory guidelines in Section 61.30, Florida Statutes, based on both parents' incomes, the time-sharing schedule, and costs like health insurance and childcare. Courts need clear justification to deviate from the guideline amount.
What does board certified in marital and family law mean?
It's a credential from The Florida Bar indicating an attorney has at least five years of experience, a substantial family law practice, completed specialized continuing education, and passed a specialty exam. Fewer than seven percent of Florida attorneys hold it.
Were there major Florida family law changes in 2026?
Some procedural updates to paternity-related support processes took effect in mid-2026, and courts continue expanding e-filing and virtual hearings, but there was no new alimony formula or automatic equal time-sharing law enacted in 2026 despite online claims to the contrary.
