Estate Planning Attorney in Sewall's Point, Florida: What You Need to Know

Quick Answer

If you live in Sewall's Point, your estate plan is governed by the same statewide Florida statutes as everyone else in Martin County and beyond: you need a will, a durable power of attorney, a healthcare surrogate designation, and a living will, and depending on your assets, a revocable trust may make sense too. Recent 2026 changes to Florida's probate law also make it easier for many smaller estates to avoid full administration.

By Arthur Simpson, Esq. · FL Bar #529265 Florida Estate Planning Attorney September 23, 2026

Why Sewall's Point Residents Need Florida-Specific Estate Planning

Sewall's Point is a small, tucked-away town between the St. Lucie and Indian Rivers, and many of the families I meet with there share a common profile: a homestead property that has appreciated significantly over the years, a mix of investment and retirement accounts, and often a second home or family somewhere else entirely. None of that changes the legal framework, though. There is no special "Sewall's Point" body of estate law. Whether you live on Sewall's Point Road or across the bridge in Stuart, your will, trust, and incapacity documents are governed by the same Florida Statutes that apply to every resident of the state.

What matters is finding an estate planning attorney who understands both the statewide rules and the local realities: Martin County's probate court, the way homestead property interacts with the Save Our Homes cap, and how waterfront and investment property common in this area should be titled and protected.

What Documents Should Every Sewall's Point Resident Have?

In my practice, I tell clients there are four foundational documents nearly every Florida adult should have, regardless of net worth:

  • Last Will and Testament, valid in Florida when it is in writing, signed by someone at least 18 and of sound mind, and signed by two witnesses in each other's presence (F.S. § 732.502).
  • Durable Power of Attorney (F.S. Chapter 709), which lets someone you trust manage your finances if you become incapacitated.
  • Healthcare Surrogate Designation (F.S. § 765.202), naming who can make medical decisions for you.
  • Living Will (F.S. § 765.301), stating your wishes about end-of-life care.

Many families in Sewall's Point also add a revocable living trust (governed by F.S. Chapter 736) as the centerpiece of their plan, particularly if they own real estate in multiple states, want to avoid probate, or simply prefer privacy and a smoother transition for their heirs. A trust doesn't replace a will; most trust-based plans still include a short "pour-over" will to catch any assets that were never retitled into the trust.

Florida's 2026 Probate Changes: What Changed and Why It Matters

Florida made several practical updates to its probate laws effective July 1, 2026, and they're worth knowing about even if you already have a plan in place.

  • The state raised several small-estate dollar thresholds, which means more modest estates can now qualify for summary administration rather than full, formal probate.
  • Personal representatives now have a new statutory tool, F.S. § 733.6125, to compel uncooperative banks and financial institutions to honor valid estate authority, something that used to cause real delays for grieving families.
  • Florida courts also gained broader flexibility to appoint curators when someone needs to manage estate assets before a personal representative is formally appointed.
  • The amount of a decedent's federal income tax overpayment that can be claimed without opening a full estate administration increased from $2,500 to $5,000.

Together, these changes are designed to move more Florida families through the probate process faster and with fewer court appearances, particularly for smaller and mid-sized estates common among longtime Sewall's Point homeowners.

Good to know: None of these 2026 updates changed the basic requirements for a valid will (F.S. § 732.502) or how revocable trusts work under F.S. Chapter 736. The changes are about administration efficiency, not the core rules for creating your plan.

Homestead, Waterfront Property, and Why Titling Matters Here

Florida's homestead protections, rooted in Article X, Section 4 of the Florida Constitution, are some of the strongest in the country, shielding a primary residence from most creditors and capping how much your property's assessed value can increase each year through the Save Our Homes provision (F.S. § 193.155). For Sewall's Point residents, whose waterfront and near-waterfront homes have often appreciated substantially, this protection is valuable, but it also creates planning complexity.

Florida law places specific restrictions on how a homestead can be devised, or left, in a will (F.S. § 732.4015). If you are married, or if you have minor children, there are limits on your ability to leave your homestead to just anyone you choose. I've reviewed plans prepared elsewhere that overlooked this entirely, leading to unintended results for a surviving spouse. This is exactly the kind of issue where local, Florida-specific review matters far more than a generic template.

⚠ A common mistake: Simply naming a beneficiary on a deed or leaving homestead property to someone other than a spouse in a will, without accounting for Florida's homestead devise restrictions, can create legal complications for your family after you're gone. This is worth having reviewed rather than assumed.

Don't Forget Digital Assets in Your 2026 Plan

A growing part of estate planning in 2026 involves assets that didn't exist, or weren't significant, a decade ago: cryptocurrency, online business accounts, intellectual property, and even valuable social media presences. Florida trusts and powers of attorney can and should include specific authority for a digital executor, someone empowered to access, manage, or close these accounts. Without that language, your family may face real practical roadblocks even after a will or trust is otherwise in order.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need a local Sewall's Point attorney, or can any Florida estate planning lawyer help me?
Any attorney licensed by The Florida Bar can prepare a valid Florida estate plan, since the same statutes apply statewide. Many Sewall's Point residents prefer counsel familiar with Martin County's probate court and the local property landscape, but the legal requirements themselves don't change by town.
How much has the Florida summary administration threshold changed in 2026?
Florida raised several small-estate dollar thresholds effective July 1, 2026, expanding which estates can use the simpler summary administration process instead of full probate. The exact threshold that applies to your situation should be confirmed with a Florida probate attorney, since eligibility depends on the specific facts of the estate.
Is a revocable living trust necessary if I already have a will?
Not necessarily. A will alone is a complete plan for many Floridians, but a revocable trust can help avoid probate, maintain privacy, and simplify management of property in multiple states. Whether one makes sense depends on your assets and goals.
What happens to my homestead in Sewall's Point if I don't plan for it specifically?
Florida places specific restrictions on how a homestead property can be left in a will, particularly if you're married or have minor children (F.S. § 732.4015). Without proper planning, your homestead may not pass the way you intended, so this deserves specific review.
Did the 2026 changes affect how I sign my will or set up a trust?
No. The core requirements for executing a valid Florida will (F.S. § 732.502) and creating a revocable trust (F.S. Chapter 736) have not changed. The 2026 updates focused on probate administration efficiency, not the rules for creating your plan.
What is the federal estate tax exemption for 2026, and does it affect most Sewall's Point families?
Beginning January 1, 2026, the federal estate, gift, and generation-skipping transfer tax exemption increased to $15 million per person. Because of this high threshold, most Florida families focus their planning on control, incapacity protection, and smooth administration rather than federal estate tax avoidance.

The Truestead Takeaway

Whether you live along the water in Sewall's Point or elsewhere on the Treasure Coast, your estate plan runs on the same Florida statutes as everyone else's, but the details of your homestead, your family situation, and Florida's 2026 probate updates can meaningfully change what plan is right for you. The sensible next step is to have your current documents, or lack of them, reviewed by a Florida-licensed attorney who can walk through your specific goals and property before assuming any template or out-of-state plan will hold up here.
