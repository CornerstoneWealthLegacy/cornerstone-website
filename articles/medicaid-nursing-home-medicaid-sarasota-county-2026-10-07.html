Nursing Home Medicaid in Sarasota County: Where to Apply and What to Expect

Quick Answer

A Sarasota County family applies for nursing home Medicaid through the state's ACCESS system run by the Department of Children and Families, while a separate state unit called CARES decides whether the parent medically needs nursing home level care. Both pieces have to come together before Medicaid starts paying.

By Arthur Simpson, Esq. · FL Bar #529265 Florida Elder Law Attorney October 6, 2026

The Two Decisions Every Sarasota County Family Faces

Louise is 89, lives in Venice, and has carried a long-term care insurance policy for years that is finally running low. Her children have never filed anything with a state agency before, and the acronyms alone (DCF, CARES, ACCESS) can feel like their own language. I want to slow down and show how this actually works in Sarasota County, because the process is the same everywhere in Florida, but it helps to see it applied to a real situation. Louise is a composite I use to illustrate how these pieces fit together, not an actual client, but her circumstances are typical of what I see from Venice, North Port, and Longboat Key families.

Every Florida nursing home Medicaid case runs on two separate tracks that have to both finish before benefits start:

  • The financial decision, made by the Department of Children and Families through its online ACCESS Florida system. This is where income, assets, and the look-back period get reviewed. (Truestead covers the look-back and spend-down rules in depth elsewhere in this series; here I will touch on them only as they relate to timing.)
  • The medical decision, made by CARES, the Comprehensive Assessment and Review for Long-Term Care Services program operated by the Florida Department of Elder Affairs. A CARES nurse or assessor determines whether the applicant truly needs a nursing facility level of care, as opposed to a lower level of support.

For Louise's family, this means two different sets of paperwork and two different reviewers, one financial and one medical, both of which have to say yes.

The Front Door: The Local Aging and Disability Resource Center

Before Louise's family ever touches a Medicaid application, the right first call in Sarasota County is to the Aging and Disability Resource Center for Southwest Florida, which also operates as the region's Area Agency on Aging. This is the designated local front door for seniors and families trying to figure out what help exists, including home and community-based waiver programs that let someone stay out of a nursing home longer if that is appropriate.

The statewide Elder Helpline connects callers anywhere in Florida, including Sarasota, Venice, and North Port, to this same regional network. For a family exploring in-home care, adult day programs, or the Medicaid waiver waiting list before a nursing home becomes necessary, this is where that conversation starts. For Louise, whose long-term care policy had been covering home health aides, this was the number her daughter called first, before anyone from the family had spoken to DCF or CARES directly.

It is worth understanding that the home and community-based waiver has a waiting list in Florida, and placement is not automatic or immediate. A nursing home Medicaid application, by contrast, does not go on that same waiting list once CARES has approved the level of care and DCF has approved the finances.

The Local Cost Picture

Families in Sarasota County are often surprised by the gap between private-pay nursing home rates and what Medicaid ultimately covers once a resident is approved. Recent data for the Sarasota area has placed private-pay skilled nursing costs in roughly the mid five-thousand-dollar range per month, though these figures vary by facility type, room configuration, and the level of care involved, and they should always be confirmed directly with a facility's business office rather than relied on as fixed. National median costs tracked by industry sources run considerably higher than local Sarasota figures, which is one reason local placement matters so much.

For Louise's family, this is the number that mattered most: her long-term care policy was paying a set daily benefit that no longer covered the full private-pay rate at the facility near Venice where she had been receiving rehab. That gap is exactly what pushes many Florida families toward Medicaid, not because they have done anything wrong, but because private-pay costs simply outpace most policies and most savings over time.

How Many Facilities Take Medicaid, and the 'Medicaid Pending' Conversation

Sarasota County has a number of Medicaid-certified nursing facilities, meaning facilities licensed to accept the Medicaid rate for at least some of their beds. The exact count shifts over time as facilities open, close, or adjust their certified-bed numbers, and the state's official facility locator is the most current source for that information at any given moment, rather than any fixed number printed here.

A certified bed is simply a bed that the facility has designated and approved to accept Medicaid payment. Not every bed in every building is certified, and not every facility accepts new Medicaid residents at every point in time, which is why the conversation with a facility's business office matters so much.

What 'Medicaid Pending' Means When a facility agrees to admit someone as 'Medicaid pending,' it means the facility is willing to accept the resident and begin care while the DCF financial decision and the CARES medical decision are still being processed. This is a business decision each facility makes on its own, and terms can vary, so it is worth asking the business office directly what pending status means for that specific facility and what happens if the application is delayed or denied.

For Louise, her social worker at the rehab facility in Venice raised Medicaid pending status directly with the family once it became clear her long-term care benefit was running out, which is a fairly typical hospital-to-rehab-to-Medicaid pattern in this part of Florida.

Three Local Wrinkles in Sarasota County

A few patterns show up repeatedly in Sarasota, Venice, North Port, and Longboat Key cases that families in other parts of Florida may not encounter in quite the same way:

  • Seasonal residents. Snowbirds who split time between Florida and another state need to establish genuine Florida residency before applying for Florida Medicaid, and the timing of a health crisis relative to the social season can complicate planning.
  • Hospital-to-rehab-to-long-term-care patterns. Many Sarasota County Medicaid cases begin with a hospital stay, move into a Medicare-covered rehab stay of up to one hundred days, and only then transition into the long-term custodial stay that Medicaid may eventually cover. Families often do not realize that Medicare rehab coverage and Medicaid long-term care coverage are entirely different programs with different rules until that transition point arrives.
  • Condo and manufactured-home ownership. Sarasota County has a large number of residents in condominiums and manufactured or mobile homes, including in areas of Venice and North Port, and homestead protection rules can apply differently depending on the type of property and how it is titled, which is worth reviewing with an attorney rather than assuming.

The Family's Timeline, From First Call to Approval

For a Sarasota County family like Louise's, the practical sequence usually looks something like this: a first call to the Aging and Disability Resource Center or the Elder Helpline to understand options, a referral or independent decision to move toward nursing home Medicaid, submission of the ACCESS Florida application online, a phone interview scheduled by DCF rather than an in-person office visit, gathering of financial documentation going back five years, and a separate CARES assessment of medical need that can happen before, during, or shortly after the financial application is filed.

⚠ Timing Varies DCF's administrative standard calls for a decision within forty-five days of a complete application, but that clock depends entirely on how quickly documentation comes in and how straightforward the financial picture is. A family with uncomplicated finances may move faster; a family untangling old accounts, a life insurance policy, or a jointly titled property may take longer. This is exactly why early planning matters more than speed at the end.

For Louise's family, involving a Florida elder law attorney made the most sense once it became clear her long-term care insurance would run out within a matter of months, well before the actual Medicaid application was filed. That lead time let her children gather five years of statements calmly rather than scrambling once a crisis hit.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do we apply for nursing home Medicaid at a Sarasota County DCF office?
Florida nursing home Medicaid applications are filed online through the statewide ACCESS Florida system rather than at a physical county office, and DCF schedules the required interview by phone.
What does CARES actually do for a Sarasota County applicant?
CARES, part of the Florida Department of Elder Affairs, sends a nurse or assessor to determine whether the applicant medically needs nursing facility level of care, which is a separate decision from DCF's financial review.
Is the Aging and Disability Resource Center the same as applying for Medicaid?
No. The ADRC for Southwest Florida and the Elder Helpline are information and referral resources, especially useful for home and community-based waiver programs, but the actual Medicaid application still goes through DCF's ACCESS system.
What is the difference between Medicare rehab days and Medicaid long-term care?
Medicare can cover a limited number of days of skilled rehabilitation after a qualifying hospital stay, but once that coverage ends, long-term custodial care is a different program entirely, and Medicaid eligibility has to be established separately for ongoing nursing home costs.
What does it mean if a Sarasota facility says a resident can be admitted 'Medicaid pending'?
It means the facility is willing to begin care before the DCF and CARES decisions are final, but the specific terms are set by each facility's business office and should be discussed directly before admission.
When should a Sarasota County family involve a Florida elder law attorney?
Generally as soon as it becomes clear that private-pay funds, like a long-term care insurance benefit, will not cover care indefinitely, since early review of assets and timing tends to produce smoother results than waiting until a crisis hits.

The Truestead Takeaway

Louise's situation, a composite built from patterns I see across Venice, North Port, and the rest of Sarasota County, shows why this process works best when a family understands the two separate tracks early: CARES deciding medical need and DCF deciding financial eligibility. The Aging and Disability Resource Center and Elder Helpline are the right first call for options and waiver information, while the ACCESS Florida application is the actual Medicaid filing channel. Facility counts, local rates, and waiting lists all shift over time, so treat the specific numbers here as a snapshot from the date this was written rather than a permanent fact, and have your family's particular finances and timeline reviewed by a Florida elder law attorney before a crisis forces the decision.
