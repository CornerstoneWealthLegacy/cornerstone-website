Carrollwood Injury Lawyer: A Florida Attorney's Plain-Language Guide

Quick Answer

If you're hurt in an accident in or around Carrollwood, Florida law generally gives you two years from the date of injury to file a lawsuit, requires your own auto insurance to cover your initial medical bills under the state's no-fault system, and reduces (but does not necessarily eliminate) your recovery if you were partly at fault. The right Carrollwood injury lawyer for your situation is one who is honest with you about these deadlines and rules from the first conversation.

By Arthur Simpson, Esq. · FL Bar #529265 Florida Personal Injury Attorney October 7, 2026

What Does "Carrollwood Injury Lawyer" Actually Mean?

In my practice, I find that most people searching for a "Carrollwood injury lawyer" aren't looking for a special type of law. Carrollwood is a well-established community in the Tampa area of Hillsborough County, and the phrase simply means a personal injury attorney who handles cases for people who live, work, or were hurt in that area. There's no separate "Carrollwood" legal code. The law that applies to your claim is Florida law, the same statutes that apply whether the accident happened on Dale Mabry Highway, Gunn Highway, Ehrlich Road, or a restaurant parking lot a few miles away.

What matters far more than geography is the type of case you have (car accident, slip and fall, dog bite, workplace injury, or a death in the family caused by someone else's negligence) and how quickly you get good legal guidance. Carrollwood has a genuinely active personal injury bar, with everything from large advertising firms to smaller boutique practices, and most offer free initial consultations with no upfront fee, working instead on a contingency basis where the attorney is paid a percentage of any recovery.

How Long Do I Have to File a Claim in Florida?

This is the single most important question I get, and it has changed in recent years, so I want to be direct about it. Florida's legislature passed House Bill 837 in 2023, which shortened the general statute of limitations for most negligence-based personal injury claims (car accidents, slip and falls, premises liability) from four years down to two years from the date of injury, under Florida Statute 95.11. This two-year framework has remained the controlling rule since that reform took effect and continues to apply today.

  • Most negligence injury claims (car crashes, falls, most accident cases): generally two years from the date of the incident.
  • Intentional torts (assault, battery, false imprisonment): a longer period applies under a separate provision of the same statute.
  • Wrongful death claims brought by a personal representative under Florida Statute 768.20 have their own timing rules.
  • Claims against a government entity (a city, county, or state agency) involve additional notice requirements under Florida's sovereign immunity statute, Florida Statute 768.28, and can require formal notice well before the lawsuit deadline.
⚠ Don't rely on an old deadline. If you or a family member were told "you have four years" based on something read a few years ago, that is no longer accurate for most injury cases after the 2023 reform. Deadlines also vary by case type and defendant, so you should have a Florida attorney confirm the specific deadline that applies to your situation as soon as possible.

What If I Was Partly at Fault for My Own Accident?

Florida used to allow an injured person to recover damages even if they were mostly responsible for an accident, with their recovery simply reduced by their percentage of fault. That changed with the 2023 reform as well. Florida now follows a modified comparative negligence rule under Florida Statute 768.81. In practical terms, if you are found more than 50% at fault for causing your own injury, you are generally barred from recovering damages at all. If you're found 50% or less at fault, your damages are reduced by your percentage of fault, but you can still recover.

This makes the early investigation of a case (photos, witness statements, police or incident reports, surveillance video) genuinely important, because the insurance company's first move in almost every claim is to argue that you share some of the blame. A Carrollwood injury lawyer who understands how local intersections, apartment complexes, and retail parking lots are laid out can be helpful in pushing back on those arguments with specific, local knowledge.

How Does Florida's No-Fault Car Insurance Law Affect My Claim?

Florida is a no-fault state for car accidents, which surprises a lot of people after a crash. Under Florida Statute 627.736, drivers are required to carry Personal Injury Protection (PIP) coverage, with a minimum of $10,000, regardless of who caused the accident. This means that after most car accidents, your own PIP coverage pays a portion of your initial medical bills and lost wages, up to your policy limits, before you look to the at-fault driver's insurance.

PIP has strict notice and treatment deadlines built into the statute, and missing them can jeopardize your benefits. If your injuries are serious, permanent, or exceed what PIP covers, you may also have a separate claim against the at-fault driver, which is where comparative negligence and the two-year filing deadline both come into play.

What Damages Can Be Recovered, and Are There Caps?

Florida injury claims typically allow recovery for medical expenses, lost income, and pain and suffering, and in cases involving a death, the decedent's survivors and estate may recover damages through a wrongful death action under Florida Statute 768.20. In cases involving especially reckless or intentional conduct, punitive damages may also be available, though Florida law caps most punitive damage awards at the greater of $500,000 or three times the compensatory damages awarded, with some exceptions for particularly egregious conduct.

A note on attorney selection: Florida allows attorneys to earn Board Certification in Civil Trial Law through The Florida Bar, a credential that requires additional experience, peer review, and testing beyond a standard law license. It is not required to practice personal injury law, but it is one objective way to evaluate an attorney's trial experience when you're comparing options.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much time do I actually have to file a personal injury lawsuit in Florida?
For most negligence-based injury claims, Florida law generally allows two years from the date of the injury, following the 2023 tort reform (HB 837). Certain claims, such as those against government entities or wrongful death actions, have different timing rules, so you should confirm your specific deadline with a Florida attorney.
Can I still recover money if I was partly responsible for my accident?
Generally yes, as long as you are found 50% or less at fault, your damages are simply reduced by your percentage of fault under Florida's modified comparative negligence rule. If you are found more than 50% at fault, Florida law generally bars recovery.
Do I have to use my own car insurance first after a Florida car accident?
Yes. Florida is a no-fault state, meaning your own Personal Injury Protection (PIP) coverage, required at a minimum of $10,000, typically pays a portion of your initial medical bills regardless of fault before any claim against the other driver is pursued.
How do most Carrollwood personal injury lawyers charge for their services?
The great majority of Florida personal injury attorneys, including those serving Carrollwood, work on a contingency fee basis, meaning there is generally no upfront cost and the fee is a percentage of any settlement or verdict recovered.
Is there a cap on how much I can recover in pain and suffering or punitive damages?
Florida does not generally cap standard pain and suffering damages in most personal injury cases, but punitive damages are capped by statute at the greater of $500,000 or three times the compensatory damages awarded, with limited exceptions.
What should I bring to a free consultation with a Carrollwood injury lawyer?
Any police or incident reports, photos of the scene or injury, insurance information, medical records or bills you've received so far, and a written timeline of what happened are all genuinely helpful starting points.

The Truestead Takeaway

What I'd want my own family to understand is this: the law in Florida has shifted meaningfully in the last few years, with a shorter filing deadline and a stricter comparative negligence rule, and those changes can quietly cost an injured person their claim if they wait too long or assume outdated rules still apply. Whether you were hurt in a car accident on one of Carrollwood's busy roads, a fall at a local business, or a workplace incident, the sensible next step is the same: get your situation reviewed by a Florida attorney promptly, while evidence is fresh and deadlines are still well in front of you, rather than behind you.
