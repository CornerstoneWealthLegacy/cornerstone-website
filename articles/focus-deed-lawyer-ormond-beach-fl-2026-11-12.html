Deed Lawyer in Ormond Beach, FL: What You Actually Need to Know

Quick Answer

Florida law does not require you to hire a lawyer to prepare or record a deed, but the execution rules are strict (two witnesses, specific printed-name and address requirements, proper acknowledgment), and a mistake can cloud your title for years. Most Ormond Beach property owners are well served by having a Florida real estate attorney prepare and review the deed before it's recorded with the Volusia County Clerk.

By Arthur Simpson, Esq. · FL Bar #529265 Florida Real Estate Attorney November 12, 2026

Do You Actually Need a Deed Lawyer in Ormond Beach, FL?

In my practice, this is the first question almost every client asks, and the honest answer is that Florida law doesn't make it mandatory. Section 689.01, Florida Statutes, governs how real property is conveyed in this state, and nothing in that statute requires an attorney to prepare or sign off on the deed itself.

What the statute does require is strict compliance with execution formalities. That's really the whole ballgame. A deed that looks fine on its face but is missing a witness signature, an address, or the proper acknowledgment isn't just an inconvenience, it can be legally defective. For a transaction involving your home, often the largest asset a Florida family owns, that's not a risk worth taking to save a modest preparation fee.

Florida's Deed Execution Rules: The Two-Witness Requirement

Here's what I tell Florida clients plainly: a deed conveying an interest in Florida real property must be signed by the grantor in the presence of two subscribing witnesses, as required under F.S. § 689.01. This is one of the most commonly overlooked requirements, especially by people using downloaded deed templates or out-of-state forms that don't account for Florida's rules.

On top of the witness signatures, Florida law (F.S. § 695.26) has required, since January 1, 2024, that each witness's printed name and post office address appear beneath their signature. Clerks across Florida, including the Volusia County Clerk of Court, routinely reject deeds that are missing this information. A rejected deed means the transfer isn't recorded, which can create real problems if you're mid-sale, mid-refinance, or trying to settle an estate.

⚠ Common Mistake I regularly see deeds drafted from generic online templates that only account for one witness, or that leave off the witness address entirely. Under current Florida law, both witnesses and their printed names and addresses are required, and the acknowledgment must also satisfy F.S. § 695.03. Any of these gaps can delay or derail your recording.

What's New: Remote Witnessing and the Statutory Quitclaim Form

Florida has modernized some of its deed rules in recent years, which is genuinely useful for Ormond Beach families dealing with snowbird owners, out-of-state heirs, or relatives who can't easily travel. Under F.S. § 689.01, witnesses may now appear through audio-video communication technology and sign electronically, provided the applicable conditions are met. This remote witnessing option can be a real convenience when family members are scattered across the country, but the technical requirements still have to be followed precisely.

Separately, Florida has had a statutory quitclaim deed form available under F.S. § 689.025 since July 1, 2023. Having a standardized form doesn't eliminate the need for careful preparation, but it does give Floridians and their attorneys a clear, legally recognized starting point for simple quitclaim transfers, such as adding a family member to title or clearing up a name discrepancy.

Types of Deeds Ormond Beach Property Owners Commonly Need

Not every transfer calls for the same instrument, and choosing the wrong one can have consequences for liability, title insurance, and even Medicaid planning down the road. In my practice, the deeds I prepare most often for Volusia County clients include:

  • Warranty deeds, which provide the strongest guarantees to a buyer and are standard in most arm's-length sales
  • Quitclaim deeds, often used between family members, in divorce settlements, or to clear up a title defect
  • Lady bird (enhanced life estate) deeds, a Florida planning tool that lets an owner retain full control during life while avoiding probate at death
  • Special warranty deeds, common in certain commercial and bank-owned transactions

Each of these interacts differently with homestead protections under Article X, Section 4 of the Florida Constitution, with the devise rules under F.S. § 732.4015, and with Florida's Save Our Homes tax benefit under F.S. § 193.155. Choosing the right deed type isn't just a paperwork question, it can affect your property taxes, your creditors' rights, and your estate plan.

What a Deed Lawyer in Ormond Beach, FL Actually Does for You

When clients ask what they're really paying for, I explain it this way: a deed lawyer isn't just typing up a form, they're confirming that the legal description matches the public record, that the grantor has the capacity and authority to convey the property, that the execution satisfies F.S. §§ 689.01, 695.03, and 695.26, and that the recording will go through cleanly with the Volusia County Clerk of Court on the first try.

Why This Matters Title defects caused by a faulty deed often aren't discovered until years later, when you try to sell, refinance, or pass the property to your heirs. At that point, fixing the problem can involve a quiet title action, which is far more expensive and time-consuming than having the deed done correctly the first time.

A local attorney also understands Volusia County's specific recording practices and can coordinate the transfer with any related estate planning, such as a revocable trust under F.S. Chapter 736, a will under F.S. Chapter 732, or a durable power of attorney under F.S. Chapter 709 if someone else is signing on the owner's behalf.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is it legal to prepare my own deed in Florida without a lawyer?
Yes, Florida law does not require an attorney to prepare a deed, but it must still meet the strict execution and recording requirements under F.S. §§ 689.01, 695.03, and 695.26, and errors can create serious title problems.
How many witnesses does a Florida deed need?
A Florida deed conveying real property must be signed in the presence of two subscribing witnesses under F.S. § 689.01, and each witness's printed name and address must also appear on the document under F.S. § 695.26.
What happens if my deed is missing a witness signature or address?
The Volusia County Clerk of Court can reject the deed for recording, and if it somehow gets recorded anyway, the defect can create title problems that may need to be resolved later, sometimes through a quiet title action.
Can witnesses sign a Florida deed remotely?
Yes, current Florida law under F.S. § 689.01 allows witnesses to appear through audio-video communication technology and sign electronically, provided the statutory conditions are satisfied.
What's the difference between a warranty deed and a quitclaim deed?
A warranty deed guarantees clear title and is typical in standard sales, while a quitclaim deed transfers whatever interest the grantor has without guarantees, and is commonly used between family members or to clear up title issues; Florida has had a statutory quitclaim form under F.S. § 689.025 since July 1, 2023.
Should I use a deed lawyer near Ormond Beach or is any Florida attorney fine?
The execution rules are statewide, so any Florida-licensed real estate attorney can prepare a valid deed, but a local attorney familiar with Volusia County's recording practices can often help the process go more smoothly.

The Truestead Takeaway

Florida doesn't force you to hire a lawyer to transfer a deed, but the law's technical demands, two witnesses, printed names and addresses, proper acknowledgment, and accurate legal descriptions, leave very little room for error. I've seen families spend far more fixing a defective deed years later than they would have spent getting it done right the first time. If you're buying, selling, adding a family member to title, or using a deed as part of your estate plan, it's worth having a Florida real estate attorney review or prepare the document before it's recorded with the Volusia County Clerk of Court.
