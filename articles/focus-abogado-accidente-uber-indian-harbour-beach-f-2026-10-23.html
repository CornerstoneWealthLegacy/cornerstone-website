Quick Answer

If you were hurt in an Uber or Lyft accident in Indian Harbour Beach, Florida law gives you two years from the crash date to file a negligence lawsuit, and which insurance policy applies depends on the driver's app status at the moment of impact, not when the claim is reported.

That confusion is normal. Rideshare accidents are not handled like ordinary car accidents in Florida. Whether Uber's million-dollar policy applies, whether the driver's personal insurance applies, or whether a lower coverage tier applies all depends on one thing: what the driver's app was doing at the exact moment of the crash.

In my practice, I tell Indian Harbour Beach clients that the first step is always figuring out the driver's status at impact. Was the app off? Was it on but no ride accepted? Was a passenger already in the car? Each answer points to a different insurance policy, and sometimes a different legal strategy entirely.

How Florida's Rideshare Insurance Law Actually Works

Florida Statute § 627.748 creates a tiered insurance structure specifically for transportation network companies (TNCs) like Uber and Lyft. The coverage that applies depends on which "period" the driver was in when the accident happened:

  • App on, no ride accepted yet: The statute requires minimum liability coverage of $50,000 per person for bodily injury, $100,000 per accident for total bodily injury, and $25,000 for property damage.
  • Ride accepted through passenger drop-off: Once a driver accepts a ride request until the last passenger exits the vehicle, Florida law requires the TNC to provide $1 million in primary liability coverage for death, bodily injury, and property damage. This coverage applies regardless of the driver's personal auto policy.

Because the amount of available coverage can swing dramatically between these tiers, the exact timestamp of the driver's app activity often becomes the single most important fact in a rideshare injury claim.

Why timing matters: Florida Statute § 627.748(8)(d) requires the TNC to provide precise log-on and log-off timestamps for the 12 hours before and after an accident when requested by an involved party or insurer. That data is often the key to unlocking the correct insurance tier.

Who Is Liable: The Driver, Uber, or Both?

Florida law classifies rideshare drivers as independent contractors, not employees of Uber or Lyft. That classification matters because it generally shields the rideshare company from being held vicariously liable simply because its driver caused a crash. The driver can still be held personally liable for ordinary negligence, including speeding, distracted driving (looking at the app), running red lights, or impaired driving, just as with any other motorist.

There are narrower paths to holding the rideshare company itself directly responsible, such as claims involving negligent hiring or retention of a driver the company knew or should have known was unsafe. These claims are harder to prove and depend heavily on the specific facts.

A Recent Florida Court Ruling Narrowed Liability Against Rideshare Companies

In May 2026, Florida's Fourth District Court of Appeal issued a decision in Haddad v. Lyft Florida, Inc. that reinforced how broadly Florida law protects rideshare companies from direct liability. The court examined section 627.748(18), the portion of the statute addressing vicarious liability, and held that a claim against the rideshare company only needs a causal connection to use of the vehicle while the driver is logged into the app to fall within the statute's protective scope.

Commentators have described this liability shield as "very broad." Practically speaking, this ruling makes it more difficult for an injured person to sue Uber or Lyft directly outside of specific negligent-hiring theories. It does not, however, change a driver's personal liability for causing the crash, nor does it change the insurance coverage requirements under § 627.748. If anything, it makes identifying the correct insurance policy, rather than suing the rideshare company itself, the central focus of most claims.

The Two-Year Deadline to File a Claim

Florida law gives someone injured in a rideshare accident, whether as a passenger, another driver, or a pedestrian struck by a rideshare vehicle, two years from the date of the crash to file a negligence lawsuit. This two-year window reflects a statutory change Florida made in 2023 that shortened the previous deadline for negligence claims generally.

⚠ Do not wait to confirm your deadline: Deadlines can be affected by who is being sued, the injured person's age, and other case-specific factors. If you were hurt in an Uber or Lyft accident, confirm your specific filing deadline with a Florida attorney rather than relying on a general rule.

What This Means for Indian Harbour Beach Residents and Visitors

Indian Harbour Beach sits in Brevard County, where rideshare use is common among residents, beachgoers, and visitors traveling to and from local attractions, restaurants, and the Space Coast's hotels. A rideshare accident here can involve a Florida-licensed driver, an out-of-state visitor, or a vehicle insured under a policy issued elsewhere. Sorting out which insurance applies, and whether the claim should be directed at the driver's personal policy, the TNC's contingent coverage, or the TNC's $1 million policy, takes a careful review of the facts, the app data, and the police report.

In my practice, I remind clients that the company's insurer is not working for the injured person. The insurer's goal is to resolve the claim for as little as possible, and the tiered coverage structure gives adjusters plenty of room to argue over which policy, if any, should respond.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need a lawyer in Indian Harbour Beach specifically for an Uber accident claim?
You do not need a lawyer based in Indian Harbour Beach itself, but you want an attorney familiar with Brevard County courts and Florida's rideshare insurance statute, since local experience helps when gathering police reports, medical records, and app timestamp data.
What if the Uber driver was not logged into the app when the crash happened?
If the app was off, the claim is generally treated like an ordinary car accident, meaning the driver's personal auto insurance policy would typically apply rather than Uber's or Lyft's commercial coverage.
Can I sue Uber directly after an accident in Florida?
Generally no, because Florida law treats rideshare drivers as independent contractors and a 2026 appellate decision (Haddad v. Lyft Florida, Inc.) reinforced broad liability protections for rideshare companies; direct claims against the company are typically limited to narrow theories like negligent hiring.
How long do I have to file a lawsuit after an Uber accident in Florida?
Florida law generally allows two years from the date of the crash to file a negligence lawsuit, following a 2023 change to Florida's statute of limitations, but you should confirm your exact deadline with an attorney since individual circumstances can affect timing.
How much insurance coverage is available if I was a passenger in the Uber?
If the driver had already accepted your ride request and you were in the vehicle, Florida law generally requires the rideshare company to provide up to $1 million in primary liability coverage for that period.
What information should I gather after a rideshare accident?
Try to document the driver's name and app screen (if possible), take photos of the scene and vehicles, get a police report, and seek medical attention promptly, since these records help establish both liability and the applicable insurance tier.

The Truestead Takeaway

Rideshare accidents in Florida involve a layered insurance system that depends entirely on what the driver's app was doing at the moment of the crash, and a 2026 appellate ruling has made it even harder to pursue the rideshare company directly rather than the correct insurance policy. If you were hurt in an Uber or Lyft accident in Indian Harbour Beach, the sensible next step is to have your specific facts, including the driver's app status and your filing deadline, reviewed by a Florida attorney before you accept any settlement offer.
