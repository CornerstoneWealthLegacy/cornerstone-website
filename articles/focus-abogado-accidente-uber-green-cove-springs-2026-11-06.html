How Florida Law Treats Uber and Lyft Accidents

Florida Statute 627.748 created a specific regulatory framework for transportation network companies (TNCs) like Uber and Lyft. This statute, sometimes called Florida's "Uber/Lyft law," sets minimum insurance requirements that apply depending on what the driver was doing at the moment of the crash.

Unlike a traditional taxi company, Uber and Lyft classify their drivers as independent contractors rather than employees. That distinction matters a great deal in an accident claim, because it generally means you cannot simply sue "Uber" the way you might sue an employer for an employee's on-the-job negligence. Instead, Florida's framework relies on layered insurance coverage that kicks in based on the driver's app status.

What Insurance Coverage Applies to Your Crash?

Under Florida law, the insurance coverage available after a rideshare accident depends on exactly what the driver was doing when the collision happened:

  • Driver logged into the app, no ride accepted yet: Florida law requires contingent liability coverage of at least $50,000 per person for bodily injury, $100,000 per incident for bodily injury, and $25,000 for property damage.
  • Driver en route to pick up a passenger, or actively transporting a passenger: Florida law requires at least $1 million in liability coverage for bodily injury, death, and property damage.
  • Driver logged off the app entirely: The rideshare company's commercial policy does not apply, and the driver's personal auto insurance governs, just as in any ordinary car accident.
Why This Matters: Identifying the driver's precise app status at the time of impact can mean the difference between a claim capped near $25,000 to $50,000 and one backed by $1 million in coverage. This is often the single most important fact an attorney investigates early in a rideshare case.

How Much Time Do You Have to File a Claim in Florida?

Florida Statute 95.11 governs the statute of limitations for negligence claims, which includes most car and rideshare accident cases. Florida's negligence statute of limitations changed as part of the state's 2023 tort reform legislation (House Bill 837), and the rules that currently apply to injury claims differ from what may have applied to crashes that happened before that reform took effect.

⚠ Don't Guess at Your Deadline: Because the filing deadline depends on exactly when your accident occurred, and because Florida also shifted to a modified comparative negligence standard that can bar recovery if you are found more than 51% at fault, you should not rely on general internet guidance (including this article) for your specific deadline. A Florida attorney can confirm the exact filing window and fault rules that apply to your crash date.

What I tell every client, rideshare case or not, is this: the earlier you have your case reviewed, the more options you typically preserve. Evidence disappears, memories fade, and insurance companies move fast to lock in their position.

An attorney familiar with this corridor understands how claims move through these local channels, from obtaining the police report to understanding how local insurance adjusters and courts typically handle rideshare liability disputes.

What an Attorney Actually Does in a Rideshare Case

In my experience, rideshare claims require more investigative legwork than a standard two-car accident because there are more potential layers of coverage and more parties who might dispute responsibility. A thorough review typically involves:

  • Pulling the rideshare company's trip data to establish the driver's exact app status at the time of the crash
  • Identifying every applicable insurance policy, including the rideshare company's layered coverage and any personal auto policies involved
  • Evaluating whether another driver, not the rideshare driver, caused the crash, since many rideshare accident claims actually involve a third-party at-fault driver
  • Calculating the full value of medical expenses, lost income, and other damages under Florida's current comparative negligence framework

None of this requires you to have all the answers before you call. It simply requires timely action so the right evidence can be gathered before it disappears.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I sue Uber directly after an accident in Green Cove Springs?
Generally no, because Florida classifies rideshare drivers as independent contractors rather than Uber employees. Claims typically proceed against the driver and through the layered insurance coverage structure established under Florida law, though every case depends on its specific facts.
What if the Uber driver was at fault and I was a passenger?
As a passenger, you were not at fault for the crash, and Florida's rideshare insurance framework generally provides up to $1 million in liability coverage when the driver was actively transporting you at the time of the collision, subject to the facts of your case.
What if another driver hit the Uber I was riding in?
In that situation, the at-fault third-party driver's own insurance, along with Florida's uninsured/underinsured motorist rules, may come into play in addition to the rideshare driver's own policy, depending on the circumstances.
How long do I have to file a claim after a rideshare accident in Florida?
Florida Statute 95.11 governs negligence claim deadlines, but the exact time limit depends on when your accident occurred, especially given the 2023 tort reform changes to Florida's negligence laws. You should confirm your specific deadline with a Florida attorney rather than relying on a general rule.
Does it matter if I was partly at fault for the accident?
Florida now uses a modified comparative negligence standard, meaning your recovery can be reduced by your percentage of fault and barred entirely if you are found more than 51% responsible. How fault is allocated is often a central issue in rideshare accident claims.
Do I need a local Green Cove Springs or Clay County attorney, or can any Florida lawyer handle my claim?
Florida-licensed attorneys can generally handle claims statewide, but familiarity with local police agencies, courts, and claims practices in Clay County can be genuinely useful when investigating and pursuing a rideshare accident case arising in Green Cove Springs.
