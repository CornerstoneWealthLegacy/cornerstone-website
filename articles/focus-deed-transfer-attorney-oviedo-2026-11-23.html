Deed Transfer Attorney Oviedo: What Florida Law Actually Requires

Quick Answer

Florida law does not require you to hire an attorney to transfer real estate, but a deed must be signed, witnessed, notarized, and recorded exactly right under Florida Statutes Chapter 689, and homestead or spousal issues can void a transfer if they're missed, which is why most Oviedo homeowners choose to have a deed transfer attorney prepare and review it.

By Arthur Simpson, Esq. · FL Bar #529265 Florida Real Estate Attorney November 23, 2026

Do I Need a Deed Transfer Attorney in Oviedo?

In my practice, this is the first question nearly every Oviedo client asks, and the honest answer is no, you are not legally required to hire a lawyer to transfer Florida real estate. Under F.S. § 689.01, a deed is valid if it meets the statutory formalities, and nothing in Florida law mandates attorney involvement.

But "not required" is different from "not advisable." Deeds are permanent public records that affect title for as long as the property exists. A mistake in a legal description, a missing witness signature, or an overlooked spousal joinder requirement does not just cause a delay, it can create a cloud on title that costs far more to fix later than it would have cost to do correctly the first time. That is the real value a deed transfer attorney in Oviedo brings: not a legal requirement, but protection against an expensive, hard-to-reverse error.

What Florida Law Requires for a Valid Deed

Florida deed transfers are governed primarily by Chapter 689 of the Florida Statutes. To be valid and recordable, a deed generally must include:

  • The grantor's (seller's or giver's) signature, properly executed
  • Two subscribing witnesses to the grantor's signature, as required under § 689.01
  • A notary acknowledgment confirming the signer's identity
  • A legally sufficient property description
  • Compliance with § 695.26, which requires the preparer's and each signing party's name and post office address to be printed, typed, or stamped on the document before it can be recorded

A quitclaim deed, the type most commonly used for transfers between family members, must meet these same execution standards. Florida even has a statutory quitclaim deed form, available since July 1, 2023 under § 689.025, which can simplify preparation when it fits the situation.

Good to know: Once a deed is recorded in the Seminole County public records, it is very difficult and often expensive to unwind. That is why careful preparation on the front end matters so much.

Homestead and Spousal Joinder: The Mistake I See Most Often

This is where self-prepared deeds go wrong more than anywhere else. Under Article X, Section 4 of the Florida Constitution, Florida's homestead protections are unusually strong, and they directly affect how a homestead property can be transferred.

If the property is the owner's homestead, a spouse generally must join in the deed, even if that spouse's name is not on the title at all. A deed that conveys homestead property without the required spousal joinder can later be challenged and declared void. I have seen well-meaning families add a child to the deed or transfer property into a trust without realizing a spouse's signature was legally necessary, and it only surfaces as a problem years later when someone tries to sell or refinance.

⚠ Before you sign: If the property is your primary residence and you are married, do not assume you can sign alone. Spousal joinder on homestead property is a constitutional requirement, not a formality, and skipping it can unravel the entire transfer.

Lady Bird Deeds, Trusts, and Why Florida Has No TOD Deed

Many Oviedo clients ask about a "transfer on death" deed after hearing about it from friends in other states. Florida has not adopted that approach: there is no Florida transfer-on-death deed statute for real estate.

Instead, Florida property owners who want to avoid probate on real estate typically use one of two tools:

  • A lady bird deed (an enhanced life estate deed), which lets the owner keep full control during life, including the right to sell or mortgage the property, while automatically passing it to named beneficiaries at death, outside of probate
  • A revocable living trust (governed by F.S. Chapter 736), which can hold the property and distribute it according to the trust terms without probate

Each has different tax, Medicaid, and homestead implications, and the right choice depends on your family situation, your mortgage, and your broader estate plan. This is a case where a quick conversation with a Florida attorney often saves far more time and money than it costs.

What a Deed Transfer Actually Costs in Oviedo

Costs vary by transaction, but as a general range, having an attorney prepare and record a straightforward deed (such as adding a spouse or family member) typically runs in the few-hundred-dollar range for attorney fees, plus Seminole County recording fees charged per page, and any applicable documentary stamp tax.

On documentary stamp tax: under F.S. § 201.02, transfers between spouses are generally exempt from the tax as long as no consideration beyond an existing mortgage balance changes hands. Transfers to other family members, or sales for value, may trigger documentary stamp tax calculated on the consideration paid or mortgage assumed. Your attorney or closing agent can calculate this precisely for your transaction.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I legally need a deed transfer attorney in Oviedo, FL?
No. Florida law under F.S. § 689.01 does not require an attorney to execute a valid deed, but the formalities (two witnesses, notarization, proper recording language) must be followed exactly, which is why many owners choose to have an attorney prepare it.
Can I just download a quitclaim deed form online?
Florida does have a statutory quitclaim deed form available since 2023 under § 689.025, but it still must be properly completed, witnessed, notarized, and must account for issues like homestead spousal joinder, which generic online forms often miss.
Does adding my spouse to my Oviedo home's deed require anything special?
If the home is your homestead, Florida's constitutional homestead protections under Article X, Section 4 can affect how the deed must be structured, and transfers between spouses are generally exempt from documentary stamp tax under F.S. § 201.02.
Can I transfer my house to my kids now and avoid probate later?
It's possible, but outright gifting a deed has tax, Medicaid, and control tradeoffs. Many Florida families instead use a lady bird deed or a revocable living trust to pass real estate outside of probate while retaining control during life.
Does Florida have a transfer-on-death deed like some other states?
No. Florida has not adopted a transfer-on-death or beneficiary deed statute for real estate; Floridians use lady bird deeds or living trusts to accomplish a similar probate-avoidance goal.
What happens if a deed is recorded with an error?
Errors in a recorded deed, such as a missing witness or incorrect legal description, can cloud title and may require a corrective deed or even legal action to fix, which is generally far more costly than having the original deed reviewed before recording.

The Truestead Takeaway

A deed transfer feels simple on the surface, but it is one of the few documents in Florida law that is nearly permanent once recorded, and the rules around witnesses, notarization, homestead, and spousal rights are unforgiving of small mistakes. In my practice, I tell Oviedo clients that the question isn't whether the law requires a lawyer, it's whether the stakes of this particular transfer (a family home, a homestead property, a trust funding) are high enough that a careful review is worth it. If you're planning a deed transfer in Seminole County, it's worth having your specific situation reviewed by a Florida attorney before you sign anything.
