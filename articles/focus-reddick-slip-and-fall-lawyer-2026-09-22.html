Reddick Slip and Fall Lawyer: What Marion County Residents Need to Know

Quick Answer

If you were hurt in a fall on someone else's property in Reddick, Florida law generally requires you to prove the property owner knew (or reasonably should have known) about the dangerous condition, and you now have two years from the date of the fall to file a lawsuit if the injury occurred after March 24, 2023. Your specific situation should be reviewed by a Florida attorney before that deadline approaches.

Looking for a Reddick Slip and Fall Lawyer? Here's the Law That Controls Your Claim

Reddick is a small, close-knit community in Marion County, part of Florida's Fifth Judicial Circuit. It doesn't have its own courthouse or its own body of law. A slip and fall that happens at a Reddick feed store, farm supply business, church, restaurant, or private property is governed by the same Florida statutes and case law that apply anywhere else in the state, filed and litigated through the Marion County court system.

The legal analysis is the same regardless of setting: it comes down to notice, fault, and timing.

What Florida Law Actually Requires You to Prove

Florida's primary slip and fall statute, F.S. § 768.0755, applies specifically to injuries caused by a transitory foreign substance, meaning something like spilled liquid, dropped food, ice, or debris on a floor. Under this law, you as the injured person must show that the business or property owner had either:

  • Actual knowledge of the dangerous condition, or
  • Constructive knowledge, meaning the condition existed long enough that the owner should have discovered it through reasonable care, or the condition occurred with regularity and was therefore foreseeable

It's important to understand that not every fall is a "transitory foreign substance" case. Section 768.0755(2) preserves Florida's older common-law duty of care for hazards that are a permanent or structural part of the property, such as a cracked walkway, a broken handrail, uneven flooring, or a poorly lit stairwell. Those cases are analyzed under general premises liability principles rather than this specific statute, though the underlying question of what the owner knew or should have known remains central either way.

Why notice matters so much: Simply falling on someone's property does not automatically make them liable. Florida law does not treat property owners as insurers of everyone who walks through the door. You have to connect the dangerous condition to something the owner knew about, or reasonably should have known about, and failed to fix or warn about within a reasonable time.

The 2023 Tort Reform Changed Your Filing Deadline

This is the single most important update for anyone in Reddick considering a slip and fall claim today. In March 2023, Florida enacted House Bill 837, a sweeping tort reform law that changed how personal injury cases, including premises liability and slip and fall claims, are handled statewide.

One of the most significant changes was to the statute of limitations, the deadline for filing a lawsuit. For negligence claims, including slip and fall cases, arising from incidents that occurred on or after March 24, 2023, Florida law now generally allows two years from the date of injury to file suit, down from the four years that applied to older claims.

⚠ Don't assume you have four years. If your fall happened before March 24, 2023, the older four-year window may still apply to your claim. If it happened on or after that date, the shorter two-year deadline almost certainly applies. Because missing a filing deadline can permanently bar your claim regardless of how strong it is, anyone who was hurt in a fall in Reddick or elsewhere in Marion County should have their timeline confirmed by a Florida attorney as soon as possible rather than assuming which deadline applies.

How Shared Fault Affects Your Compensation

Florida also changed its approach to shared fault as part of the 2023 tort reform. Property owners and their insurers routinely argue that the injured person was distracted, wearing inappropriate footwear, ignored a warning sign, or otherwise contributed to their own fall. How that shared fault is handled can significantly affect the value of a claim.

Because the rules around comparative negligence have shifted in recent years and the details matter a great deal to the outcome of a case, I'd rather be honest with you than guess: if you've been told you were "partly at fault" for your own fall, don't assume that ends your claim. Have a Florida attorney walk through exactly how the current comparative fault framework applies to your specific facts before you accept an insurance adjuster's characterization of the accident.

What to Do After a Fall in Reddick

Whether the fall happened at a local business, a farm, or someone's private property, the same practical steps generally protect your rights while the facts are still fresh:

  • Report the fall to the property owner, manager, or occupant, and ask that it be documented in writing
  • Photograph the hazard, the surrounding area, and your injuries before conditions change
  • Get names and contact information for anyone who witnessed the fall
  • Seek medical evaluation promptly, even if injuries seem minor at first
  • Keep records of medical treatment, missed work, and out-of-pocket expenses
  • Avoid giving a recorded statement to an insurance company before speaking with an attorney

What I tell Florida clients is that early documentation often makes the difference between a claim that can be proven and one that comes down to a swearing match months later.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need a lawyer who is physically located in Reddick?
No. Reddick cases are handled in the Marion County court system under Florida state law, so what matters most is a Florida-licensed attorney experienced in premises liability, not a physical office in Reddick itself.
How long do I have to file a slip and fall lawsuit in Florida?
For falls occurring on or after March 24, 2023, Florida generally allows two years from the date of injury to file suit. Falls before that date may still fall under the older four-year deadline. Confirm your exact deadline with a Florida attorney.
What if the store says they didn't know about the spill?
You can still potentially recover if you show the store should have known through reasonable inspection, or that the type of hazard occurred regularly enough to be foreseeable. This is the constructive knowledge standard under F.S. § 768.0755.
Does it matter if the hazard was a wet floor versus a broken step?
Yes. Wet floors and spills fall under Florida's transitory foreign substance statute, while structural issues like broken steps, cracked pavement, or broken handrails are generally evaluated under Florida's older common-law premises liability duty of care.
What if I was partly to blame for my own fall?
Florida's comparative negligence rules affect how compensation is calculated when both the property owner and the injured person share some fault. Because these rules changed with recent tort reform, it's important to have a Florida attorney evaluate exactly how shared fault applies to your case.
What kinds of falls are common around Reddick specifically?
Given the area's rural and equestrian character, Truestead sees fall injuries tied to farm and agricultural properties, uneven outdoor terrain, and local commercial businesses, in addition to the more typical wet floor and parking lot cases seen statewide.

The Truestead Takeaway

If you were hurt in a fall in Reddick, the law that applies is Florida's statewide premises liability framework, not a local rule, and the most important thing right now is figuring out which statute of limitations applies to your date of injury and whether the property owner had notice of the hazard. Given how much the 2023 tort reform changed both filing deadlines and fault rules, I'd encourage anyone with a recent fall to have their timeline and facts reviewed by a Florida attorney sooner rather than later, so nothing important slips past a deadline while you're still recovering.
