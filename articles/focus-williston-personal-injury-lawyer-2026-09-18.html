Williston Personal Injury Lawyer: What Levy County Residents Need to Know

Quick Answer

A Williston personal injury lawyer helps Levy County residents pursue compensation after car accidents, falls, and other injuries caused by someone else's negligence. Florida law generally gives you two years from the date of injury to file suit, and your recovery can be reduced, or barred entirely, if you were more than 50% at fault, so timing and evidence matter from day one.

How Long Do I Have to File a Florida Injury Claim?

This is the single most important question, and the answer changed in recent years. Under Florida Statute 95.11(5)(a), as amended by 2023 tort reform legislation, the general statute of limitations for negligence claims (including most car accident and premises liability cases) is two years from the date of the incident, for injuries occurring on or after March 24, 2023. If your injury happened before that date, the older four-year deadline may still apply.

⚠ Don't Rely on Old Information A lot of content online still references Florida's former four-year deadline. That rule no longer applies to most post-2023 accidents. Because deadlines can vary based on the type of claim (for example, claims against a government entity have shorter notice requirements), you should always confirm your specific deadline with a Florida attorney rather than assuming.

What If I Was Partly at Fault for the Accident?

Florida also shifted from a pure comparative negligence system to a modified comparative negligence standard as part of the same 2023 reform. In practical terms, if you are found to be 50% or less at fault for your own injury, you can still recover damages, reduced by your percentage of fault. But if you are found more than 50% responsible, Florida law generally bars you from recovering anything at all.

This makes early investigation critical. Insurance adjusters know this rule well, and they will often look for any argument that shifts blame onto you, even partially, to reduce or eliminate your claim. Documentation, witness statements, and photos from the scene can make a meaningful difference in how fault gets allocated.

How Does PIP and Car Insurance Work for Williston Drivers?

Florida has long operated under a no-fault auto insurance system. Currently, registered vehicle owners must carry a minimum of $10,000 in Personal Injury Protection (PIP) coverage. After a crash, you generally file first with your own PIP carrier regardless of who caused the accident. PIP is designed to cover 80% of reasonable medical expenses, 60% of lost wages, and certain other costs, up to policy limits.

There has been discussion of legislative changes to Florida's mandatory PIP requirement affecting policies going forward, and Florida's no-fault framework has been the subject of ongoing legislative attention in recent years. Because insurance requirements can shift, I always tell clients to confirm the coverage rules in effect on the date of their accident with their insurer or an attorney, rather than relying on what a friend's policy required a few years ago.

Why This Matters Even when PIP covers your initial medical bills, it often isn't enough to cover serious injuries. A personal injury claim against the at-fault driver may still be necessary to recover the rest of your medical costs, lost wages, and pain and suffering, particularly if your injury meets Florida's threshold for a claim beyond PIP.

Are There Caps on What I Can Recover in Florida?

Generally, Florida does not cap compensatory damages, such as medical expenses, lost wages, and pain and suffering, in most standard personal injury cases involving private individuals or businesses. This is good news for injured Floridians pursuing a legitimate claim.

That said, there are important exceptions. Claims against government entities (a city, county, or state agency) are subject to specific damage limits and strict, short notice deadlines under Florida's sovereign immunity laws. Medical malpractice claims also involve their own separate procedural rules and, in some circumstances, different limits. If your injury involves a government vehicle, a public building, or a medical provider, the standard rules and timelines may not apply, and you should have your case reviewed promptly.

How Do Personal Injury Lawyer Fees Work in Florida?

Most Florida personal injury attorneys, including those serving Williston, work on a contingency fee basis, meaning you pay nothing upfront and the fee comes out of any settlement or verdict. Contingency fees for personal injury cases commonly range in the mid-thirties to low-forty percent range, and can vary depending on case complexity and whether the case proceeds to litigation or trial. Florida law requires that any contingency fee agreement be documented in writing and clearly explained before you sign.

  • Ask what percentage applies before and after a lawsuit is filed
  • Ask who pays case costs (expert witnesses, filing fees) if you don't win
  • Get the fee agreement in writing before your first document is signed

A reputable attorney will walk through this with you plainly, with no pressure, before you commit to representation.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need a lawyer based in Williston, or can I use an attorney from Gainesville or Ocala?
You are not required to hire a lawyer physically located in Williston. Many Levy County residents work with attorneys based in nearby Gainesville, Ocala, or elsewhere in North Central Florida, and Florida attorneys routinely handle cases across county lines.
How long do I have to file a personal injury lawsuit in Florida?
For most negligence claims arising from incidents on or after March 24, 2023, Florida's statute of limitations is generally two years from the date of injury. Older incidents may fall under a previous four-year rule, and certain claim types have shorter deadlines, so you should confirm your specific timeline with an attorney.
What happens if I was partially at fault for my accident?
Under Florida's modified comparative negligence rule, you can still recover damages if you are found 50% or less at fault, with your recovery reduced by your percentage of fault. If you are found more than 50% at fault, Florida law generally bars recovery entirely.
Will my own car insurance cover my medical bills first?
Yes, in most cases. Florida's no-fault system currently requires drivers to carry Personal Injury Protection (PIP) coverage, which typically pays a portion of medical expenses and lost wages regardless of fault, before any separate claim against the at-fault driver is pursued.
Is there a cap on how much I can recover for pain and suffering in Florida?
Generally no, Florida does not cap compensatory damages in most standard personal injury cases against private parties. Claims against government entities and certain medical malpractice claims can involve different limits and rules.
How much does it cost to hire a personal injury lawyer?
Most Florida personal injury lawyers work on contingency, meaning there's no upfront fee and payment comes from a percentage of your settlement or verdict, typically in the mid-thirties to low-forty percent range depending on the case.
