Estate Planning in Shady Hills, Florida: What Residents Need to Know in 2026

Quick Answer

Estate planning in Shady Hills works the same way it does across Pasco County and all of Florida: residents use the same wills, revocable trusts, powers of attorney, and healthcare documents governed by Florida statute, and Florida's probate rules just changed in significant ways effective July 1, 2026.

By Arthur Simpson, Esq. · FL Bar #529265 Florida Estate Planning Attorney September 8, 2026

Is There Anything Special About Estate Planning in Shady Hills?

In my practice, I get some version of this question from clients in every unincorporated Pasco County community: is there something different about estate planning here versus, say, Land O' Lakes or New Port Richey? The honest answer is no. There is no separate body of law called "Shady Hills estate planning." Shady Hills residents use exactly the same tools every other Floridian uses: a last will and testament, possibly a revocable living trust, a durable power of attorney, a healthcare surrogate designation, and a living will, all governed by the same statewide statutes.

What does matter locally is that your plan should be reviewed by someone who understands how it applies to your specific circumstances: whether you own homestead property in Pasco County, whether you have out-of-state family, whether you're part of a blended family, or whether you're planning for long-term care. The law is statewide. The application is personal.

The Building Blocks: Wills, Trusts, and Powers of Attorney

Every solid Florida estate plan rests on a handful of core documents. Here's what each one does:

  • Last Will and Testament: Directs how your assets pass at death and names a personal representative. Florida has strict execution formalities under Chapter 732 and F.S. § 732.502, including witness and notarization requirements, so a will that would be valid in another state may not automatically work in Florida.
  • Revocable Living Trust: Governed by Florida Statutes Chapter 736, a revocable trust can help your estate avoid probate on assets titled in the trust's name, and it offers privacy since trust administration generally isn't a public court record the way probate is.
  • Durable Power of Attorney: Under F.S. Chapter 709, this document lets someone you choose manage your financial affairs if you become incapacitated, without the need for a court-supervised guardianship.
  • Healthcare Surrogate Designation: Under F.S. § 765.202, this names someone to make medical decisions for you if you can't make them yourself.
  • Living Will: Under F.S. § 765.301, this expresses your wishes about end-of-life medical treatment.

For most Shady Hills families, the right combination of these documents depends on the value and type of assets involved, whether you own real estate, and whether you want to avoid probate altogether or are comfortable with Florida's now-streamlined probate process.

Florida Probate Just Got Easier: The July 2026 Reforms

If you haven't looked at your estate plan in a few years, here's the freshest reason to take another look. Florida enacted significant probate reform through CS/HB 1337 and its companion CS/SB 1500, effective July 1, 2026. These changes came out of the Florida Supreme Court's own Workgroup on Uncontested Probate Proceedings and are aimed at making routine estate administration faster and less expensive for ordinary families.

The headline change involves summary administration, the simplified probate process available for smaller estates under F.S. §§ 735.201 through 735.2063. Before July 1, 2026, an estate generally had to be valued at $75,000 or less (after subtracting exempt property) to qualify. That threshold has now been raised to $150,000, meaning many more Shady Hills estates can skip full, formal administration and use the faster summary process.

The law also raised the threshold for disposition of personal property without administration (a process for very small estates with no real property, under F.S. §§ 735.301 and 735.304) from $10,000 to $20,000 in nonexempt personal property.

What this means for you: If your estate's value was previously right on the edge of qualifying for summary administration, it may now clearly qualify, which can mean a faster, less costly process for your family after you pass.

New Tools for Personal Representatives Dealing With Banks

One of the quieter but genuinely useful changes in the 2026 reform is a brand-new statute, F.S. § 733.6125, titled "Proceedings to enforce authority." I've seen this problem play out for years: a personal representative has valid letters of administration, but a bank or financial institution drags its feet, demands paperwork the law doesn't require, or simply won't cooperate. Before this statute, the personal representative's main option was a full lawsuit, which is slow and expensive relative to the problem.

Now, a personal representative can bring a targeted enforcement proceeding instead, and if they prevail, the court must award taxable costs, including attorney fees. That fee-shifting provision gives institutions a real incentive to honor valid letters of administration promptly rather than creating unnecessary friction.

The 2026 law also gave courts more flexibility to appoint curators when needed, allowing appointment at any time with whatever notice to interested persons the court considers appropriate under the circumstances.

Federal Tax Numbers for 2026 (And Why Most Floridians Don't Need to Worry)

Florida has no state estate tax or inheritance tax, and that hasn't changed. On the federal side, the numbers for 2026 are worth knowing even though they affect relatively few families. The federal estate and gift tax basic exclusion amount is $15,000,000 per individual for 2026, and the annual gift tax exclusion is $19,000 per recipient per donor (married couples who both participate in gift-splitting can give up to $38,000 per recipient).

Because these federal exemption amounts are so high, most estate planning in Florida today, including for Shady Hills families, is focused less on federal tax avoidance and much more on control: making sure the right people can make decisions if you're incapacitated, making sure your homestead and other property pass the way you intend, and making the administration process after your death as smooth as possible for the people you leave behind.

⚠ Don't assume your old plan still fits: Florida's rules around probate thresholds, powers of attorney, and homestead property shift over time even when nothing in your family has changed. A plan drafted five or ten years ago may no longer reflect the most efficient path available to your family today.

Homestead and Florida's Constitutional Protections

For many Shady Hills homeowners, the house is the single largest asset in the estate, and Florida's homestead protections are unusually strong. Article X, Section 4 of the Florida Constitution shields homestead property from most creditor claims, and Article VII, Section 6 (along with F.S. § 196.031) provides the homestead property tax exemption. Save Our Homes assessment limitations and portability, under F.S. § 193.155, can also significantly affect what your heirs inherit and how property taxes are recalculated after a transfer.

Homestead property also comes with restrictions on how it can be devised in a will, particularly when you're survived by a spouse or minor children, under F.S. § 732.4015. These rules can override what a will says if the will doesn't account for them correctly, which is why homestead property deserves specific attention in any Florida estate plan, not a one-size-fits-all approach.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need a special estate planning attorney because I live in Shady Hills?
No. Estate planning law is set at the state level, so the same Florida statutes apply whether you live in Shady Hills, Land O' Lakes, or anywhere else in Pasco County. What matters is finding an attorney familiar with Florida law and willing to review your specific assets and family situation.
What is the new summary administration threshold in Florida for 2026?
As of July 1, 2026, an estate can generally qualify for Florida's simplified summary administration process if its value, after subtracting exempt property, does not exceed $150,000, up from the prior $75,000 threshold.
Does Florida have a state estate tax?
No. Florida does not impose a state estate tax or inheritance tax. Federal estate tax only applies to very large estates, with a federal exclusion amount of $15,000,000 per individual for 2026.
What happens if a bank won't honor my letters of administration?
Effective July 1, 2026, a new Florida statute, F.S. § 733.6125, allows a personal representative to bring a targeted court proceeding to enforce their authority, and a prevailing personal representative is entitled to an award of costs and attorney fees.
Should I still have a will if I have a revocable trust?
Generally yes. Most Florida estate plans that include a revocable trust also include a "pour-over" will to catch any assets that weren't titled in the trust's name, and your specific documents should be reviewed together to make sure they work as intended.
How often should I review my Florida estate plan?
There's no fixed legal requirement, but it's wise to review your plan after major life events (marriage, divorce, birth, death, or a move) and periodically otherwise, since Florida's probate, homestead, and incapacity planning rules can change even when your family circumstances haven't.

The Truestead Takeaway

There is no unique legal doctrine for estate planning in Shady Hills, but Florida law itself just changed in ways that genuinely help ordinary families: higher probate thresholds, a faster path for small estates, and new tools to force stubborn financial institutions to cooperate. If your will, trust, or powers of attorney haven't been reviewed since before July 2026, or in several years generally, it's a sensible time to have a Florida attorney take a fresh look at how your plan holds up under current law.
