Estate Planning Homosassa: What Citrus County Residents Need to Know in 2026

Quick Answer

Estate planning in Homosassa follows the same Florida statutes that apply statewide, but if you own property here your estate will be probated in Citrus County, at the courthouse in Inverness — and a 2026 change to Florida law just made small-estate probate easier for many local families.

By Arthur Simpson, Esq. · FL Bar #529265 Florida Estate Planning Attorney August 12, 2026

What Makes Estate Planning in Homosassa Different?

I get this question a lot from folks who've just moved to Homosassa, or who've lived here for decades and are finally getting around to "doing the paperwork." The good news: Florida estate planning law doesn't change from one county to the next. The same statutes that apply in Miami or Orlando apply here in Citrus County. What is local is where your estate gets handled after you pass away — and that's the Citrus County Clerk of Court in Inverness, part of Florida's Fifth Judicial Circuit.

That matters more than people expect. If your will, trust, or beneficiary designations are outdated or don't match how you actually hold title to your Homosassa home, your family could end up navigating a Citrus County probate case they weren't expecting — even if you thought you'd "handled it" years ago with a form from the internet or an out-of-state attorney.

The Core Documents Every Homosassa Resident Should Have

In my practice, I tell clients a complete Florida estate plan generally rests on four pillars:

  • A Last Will and Testament — governed by Florida Statutes Chapter 732. To be valid, you must be at least 18 (or legally emancipated) and of sound mind, you must sign at the end of the document (or direct someone to sign for you in your presence), and two witnesses must sign in your presence and each other's presence.
  • A Revocable Living Trust — often used alongside a will to help avoid probate for the assets placed in it and to give a trusted person immediate authority if you become incapacitated.
  • A Durable Power of Attorney — under Florida Statutes Chapter 709, this lets someone you name handle your financial and legal affairs if you're unable to.
  • A Designation of Health Care Surrogate and Living Will — under Florida Statutes Chapter 765, these let you name someone to make medical decisions for you and state your wishes about end-of-life care.

None of this is exotic or unusual — it's the standard toolkit I'd want for my own family. The details of how these documents interact, though, depend heavily on your specific assets, your family situation, and whether you own real estate (particularly homestead property) here in Homosassa.

Big News for 2026: Florida Just Made Small-Estate Probate Easier

If you searched for estate planning in Homosassa this year, you should know about a real, meaningful change. Florida's Legislature passed CS/HB 1337, now Chapter 2026-57, Laws of Florida, based on recommendations from the Florida Supreme Court's Workgroup on Uncontested Probate Proceedings. The Governor approved it on April 29, 2026, and it took effect July 1, 2026.

Here's what changed:

  • The threshold for summary administration — Florida's simplified, faster probate process — rose from $75,000 to $150,000 in estate value, excluding exempt property. This means many more Homosassa estates, especially modest ones, may now qualify for the faster, less expensive process instead of full formal administration.
  • The value of certain intestate estates (where someone died without a will) that can be disposed of without any administration increased from $10,000 to $20,000.
  • The law also clarified how a court-appointed personal representative can access a decedent's safe-deposit box.
Why This Matters Locally: Many Homosassa homes, boats, and modest investment accounts fall well within the new $150,000 summary administration threshold. For families who might have faced a lengthier formal probate under the old rules, this change could mean a simpler, faster process — but eligibility still depends on the details of the estate, including debts and how title is held.

Homestead, Your Homosassa Property, and Estate Planning

Homosassa sits in an area where many residents own their primary residence outright or are close to it, and Florida's homestead protections are some of the strongest in the country. Article X, Section 4 of the Florida Constitution protects a homestead from most creditor claims, and Article VII, Section 6 provides the property tax homestead exemption, administered through Florida Statutes § 196.031.

But homestead property comes with a wrinkle many people don't expect: Florida Statutes § 732.4015 restricts how you can leave homestead property in your will if you're survived by a spouse or minor children. You generally cannot simply leave your homestead to whomever you choose if you have a surviving spouse — the law provides specific rules to protect them. This is one of the most common surprises I encounter with clients who assumed their will would control their home the way they intended.

If you've owned your Homosassa property for years, or you've remarried, added a family member to the deed, or moved here from out of state, it's worth having your plan reviewed specifically with your homestead status in mind.

Federal Estate and Gift Tax Numbers for 2026

Most Homosassa families never come close to owing federal estate tax, but it's worth knowing the numbers. For 2026, the federal estate and gift tax exemption (the basic exclusion amount) is $15,000,000 per individual, meaning a married couple can often preserve roughly $30,000,000 with proper planning. The annual gift tax exclusion for 2026 is $19,000 per recipient, per donor — meaning you can give up to that amount to as many individuals as you like in a year without needing to file a gift tax return.

⚠ A Note on Older Plans: If your will, trust, or powers of attorney were drafted more than five to ten years ago — especially if you moved to Florida from another state — it's worth having them reviewed. Outdated documents, old exemption amounts referenced by dollar figure, or powers of attorney that don't comply with current Florida requirements can create real complications for your family down the road.

Frequently Asked Questions

Where is probate handled for someone who lived in Homosassa?
Probate for Homosassa residents is filed with the Citrus County Clerk of Court at the courthouse in Inverness, the county seat, within Florida's Fifth Judicial Circuit.
Do I need a living trust, or is a will enough?
It depends on your assets, family situation, and goals. A will alone generally requires probate; a properly funded revocable living trust can help many assets avoid probate. This is a decision best made after reviewing your specific situation with a Florida attorney.
What is summary administration, and does the 2026 change affect me?
Summary administration is Florida's simplified probate process for smaller estates. As of July 1, 2026, the threshold rose from $75,000 to $150,000 (excluding exempt property), so more estates — including many in Homosassa — may now qualify for this faster process.
Can I leave my Homosassa home to anyone I choose in my will?
Not always. Florida Statutes § 732.4015 restricts how homestead property can be devised if you're survived by a spouse or minor children, regardless of what your will says. This is a common area where review with an attorney is worthwhile.
Does Florida have a state estate tax?
No. Florida does not impose a state estate or inheritance tax. Federal estate tax only applies to very large estates — for 2026, the federal exemption is $15,000,000 per individual.
I already have a will from another state. Is it valid in Florida?
Florida generally recognizes wills validly executed under the law of the state where they were signed, but out-of-state documents — especially powers of attorney and healthcare directives — often don't align well with Florida's specific statutory requirements. A review is the safest way to confirm your documents will work as intended here.

The Truestead Takeaway

Estate planning in Homosassa runs on the same Florida statutes as the rest of the state, but where you own property and how your local estate would move through Citrus County probate matters — and the 2026 changes to Florida's summary administration threshold are a genuine, useful development for many families here. If your will, trust, power of attorney, or healthcare documents haven't been reviewed recently — or you've never had them prepared at all — the sensible next step is a conversation with a Florida attorney who can look at your actual assets, your family situation, and how the current law applies to you.
