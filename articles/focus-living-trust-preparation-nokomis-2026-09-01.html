Living Trust Preparation in Nokomis, Florida: What You Actually Need to Know

Quick Answer

Living trust preparation in Nokomis follows the same Florida law that applies statewide: your trust must be a written document that names ascertainable beneficiaries and a trustee with real duties, and it must be properly signed, witnessed, and funded to work. There's nothing Nokomis-specific about the legal requirements — what matters is getting the Florida homestead rules, funding, and coordinating documents right.

By Arthur Simpson, Esq. · FL Bar #529265 Florida Estate Planning Attorney September 1, 2026

What Living Trust Preparation Actually Means in Nokomis

In my practice, I get a lot of calls from folks in Nokomis and along the Sarasota County coast asking whether there's something different about setting up a living trust here compared to anywhere else in Florida. The honest answer is no — the law is the same whether you live off Albee Farm Road or in downtown Tampa. What makes Nokomis clients unique isn't the statute; it's the assets. Many of my Nokomis clients own coastal or waterfront homes that have appreciated significantly, have a homestead that's benefited for years from Save Our Homes tax caps, or split time between Florida and a northern state as seasonal residents. Getting living trust preparation right in Nokomis means applying standard Florida trust law carefully to those specific situations.

A Florida revocable living trust is created under Chapter 736 of the Florida Statutes, sometimes called the Florida Trust Code. Under Florida Statute § 736.0402, a valid trust needs a lawful purpose, ascertainable beneficiaries, and a trustee who actually has duties to perform — it can't just be an empty shell of a document. Any trust holding real property must be in writing.

How a Living Trust Must Be Signed in Florida

Execution formalities matter, and this is where I see self-prepared documents run into trouble most often. A Florida living trust agreement should be signed by the grantor (the person creating the trust), witnessed, and notarized. Any adult of sound mind — generally 18 or older — can create a living trust as long as they have the mental capacity to understand what they're signing at the time they sign it.

Because most Nokomis clients also want a companion pour-over will to catch anything left outside the trust, that will must independently satisfy Florida's separate execution requirements under Chapter 732 and F.S. § 732.502 — meaning two witnesses signing in the proper sequence, typically with notarization for self-proving purposes. A trust and a will are different documents with different signing rules, and both need to be done correctly for your plan to hold up.

Homestead and Your Florida Trust: What to Watch For

Florida's homestead protections are some of the strongest in the country, and they interact with living trusts in ways that trip up a lot of well-intentioned DIY plans. Transferring your homestead into a properly drafted revocable living trust generally does not trigger reassessment or cause you to lose your homestead exemption, as long as the trust reserves your beneficial interest in the property for life and you continue living there as your primary residence. Florida law treats a home held this way as equivalent to the owner's own interest for exemption purposes.

⚠ Watch for this The most common problems I see in self-prepared or poorly drafted trusts involving Nokomis homes are: transferring the homestead into the trust without the language needed to preserve the tax exemption, retirement accounts still naming an outdated beneficiary instead of (or alongside) the trust, no pour-over will to catch stray assets, and trust provisions that unintentionally conflict with Florida's homestead devise restrictions under F.S. § 732.4015. Any one of these can undo the very protection you were trying to create.

Florida's homestead protections come from both the state constitution (Art. X, § 4 and Art. VII, § 6, Fla. Const.) and statute, and the devise restrictions in particular limit how you can leave your homestead if you're survived by a spouse or minor children — regardless of what your trust says. This is exactly the kind of interaction that deserves a careful, individualized review rather than a template.

Funding Your Trust: The Step Most People Skip

Signing the trust document is only half the job. A living trust only controls the assets that are actually titled in its name — a process called funding. This typically means:

  • Retitling your Nokomis home and any other Florida real estate into the trust's name via a new deed
  • Retitling bank and brokerage accounts, or adding the trust as a payable-on-death designation where appropriate
  • Updating beneficiary designations on life insurance and retirement accounts to coordinate with (not necessarily point directly to) the trust

An unfunded trust is one of the most common estate planning failures I see. The document may be perfectly drafted, but if your home is still titled in your individual name when you pass away, it still has to go through probate — defeating the whole purpose of setting up the trust in the first place.

Why People Choose a Living Trust Over a Will in Florida

Unlike a will, which must go through Florida's probate court process after death, assets properly held in a funded revocable living trust generally pass to your beneficiaries without court involvement. That means a faster, more private transfer and less exposure to the time and expense of formal probate administration — something that matters to many Nokomis retirees and seasonal residents who want to keep things simple for their families.

A trust also offers continuity if you become incapacitated: your successor trustee can step in and manage trust assets without a court-supervised guardianship proceeding, which is a meaningful benefit alongside a properly executed durable power of attorney (Chapter 709) and healthcare documents (F.S. § 765.202 and § 765.301).

2026 Update: Why Older Trusts May Need a Second Look

Recent Development Federal tax law changed the estate tax exemption significantly through the One Big Beautiful Bill Act, permanently raising the federal exemption to a much higher level per individual. This is genuinely good news for most families, but it creates a hidden problem for trusts drafted years ago.

Many older revocable trusts were written with automatic "formula clauses" that split assets between a surviving spouse and a restrictive credit shelter (or "bypass") trust based on whatever the federal estate tax exemption happened to be at the time. With the exemption now dramatically higher, those old formulas can accidentally lock away far more money in a restricted trust than the surviving spouse actually needs or wants, rather than passing outright. If your Nokomis living trust was drafted more than a few years ago and includes this kind of formula language, it's worth having it reviewed to confirm it still reflects your intentions under current law.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is there anything unique about living trust preparation specifically in Nokomis, Florida?
No — Nokomis follows the same statewide Florida trust and homestead law as the rest of the state. What's often unique is the property itself, such as appreciated coastal homes or seasonal residency situations, which require careful attention within standard Florida rules.
Do I still need a will if I have a living trust in Florida?
Yes. Most Florida estate plans pair a revocable living trust with a pour-over will, which catches any assets accidentally left outside the trust and directs them into it, and must be separately signed and witnessed under Florida law.
Will putting my Nokomis home into a trust affect my homestead exemption?
Generally no, as long as the trust is properly drafted to reserve your beneficial interest for life and you continue to live in the home as your primary residence. This is a common area for errors in self-prepared trusts, so the deed and trust language should be reviewed carefully.
How much does it cost to prepare a living trust with a Florida attorney?
Attorney-prepared trust packages in Florida commonly range from roughly $1,500 to $4,500, typically including the trust agreement, a pour-over will, power of attorney, healthcare surrogate designation, and living will, though costs vary by complexity and firm.
I already have an older living trust — does the 2026 federal tax law change mean I need to update it?
It might. If your trust contains older formula clauses tied to prior federal estate tax exemption amounts, those provisions could now distribute assets differently than you intend given the significantly higher current exemption, so a review is worthwhile.
What happens if I sign my trust but never transfer my assets into it?
An unfunded trust generally cannot avoid probate for those assets, because only property actually titled in the trust's name is governed by it. Funding — retitling real estate and accounts — is a necessary follow-up step after signing.

The Truestead Takeaway

Living trust preparation in Nokomis doesn't require a different legal playbook than anywhere else in Florida — it requires the standard Florida Trust Code framework applied carefully to your specific home, accounts, and family situation, with special attention to homestead rules, proper funding, and coordinated documents like a pour-over will and power of attorney. If you already have an older trust, the recent federal estate tax changes are a good reason to have it reviewed rather than assume it still works the way you intended. Whether you're starting fresh or revisiting an existing plan, the sensible next step is a review with a Florida estate planning attorney who can look at your actual deed, accounts, and family circumstances — not a generic template.
