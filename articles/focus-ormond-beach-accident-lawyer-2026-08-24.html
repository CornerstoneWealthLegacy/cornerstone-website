Ormond Beach Accident Lawyer: What You Need to Know Under Florida Law

Quick Answer

An Ormond Beach accident lawyer is a Florida-licensed attorney who handles car crash, slip-and-fall, and other negligence injury claims in Volusia County; most Florida injury claims now must be filed within two years of the accident, but the exact deadline depends on when your accident happened, so it's worth having a Florida attorney confirm your specific date.

By Arthur Simpson, Esq. · FL Bar #529265 Florida Personal Injury Attorney August 24, 2026

What an Ormond Beach Accident Lawyer Actually Does

In my practice, I tell clients that a personal injury lawyer's job comes down to three things: figuring out who was legally at fault, documenting how the injury has affected your life, and dealing with insurance companies so you don't have to do it while you're trying to heal. An Ormond Beach accident lawyer handles this work specifically for people injured in and around Volusia County — car and motorcycle wrecks on A1A or I-95, slip-and-falls at local businesses, and other negligence-based injuries.

Local familiarity does matter. A lawyer who regularly appears in Volusia County courts, knows how the Ormond Beach Police Department and Florida Highway Patrol document crashes, and understands common local accident patterns can move your claim more efficiently than someone unfamiliar with the area. But the underlying law — the rules that actually determine whether you get compensated and how much — is Florida statutory and common law, and it applies the same way whether your case is filed in Daytona Beach, DeLand, or anywhere else in the state.

The Statute of Limitations: Why the Date of Your Accident Matters So Much

This is the single most important legal fact for anyone searching for an accident lawyer in 2026. Florida's statute of limitations — the deadline to file a lawsuit — changed significantly with the tort reform law (HB 837) that Governor DeSantis signed on March 24, 2023, amending Florida Statute § 95.11(5)(a).

  • Accidents on or after March 24, 2023: The deadline to file most negligence-based personal injury lawsuits is generally two years from the date of the incident.
  • Accidents before March 24, 2023: The prior four-year statute of limitations generally still applies.

Because the rule depends on exactly when your accident occurred, and because there are exceptions and nuances (for example, different rules can apply to claims against government entities, wrongful death claims, or cases involving minors), you should not rely on a general rule of thumb to calculate your own deadline. A Florida attorney can confirm the specific filing deadline that applies to your situation.

⚠ Don't wait to find out your deadline. Missing a filing deadline typically means losing the right to recover compensation entirely, regardless of how strong your case is. If you were hurt in an accident, treat the date of the incident as critical information and have it reviewed promptly.

Florida's Comparative Negligence Rule: What If You Were Partly at Fault?

What I tell Florida clients worried about shared blame is this: being partly at fault doesn't automatically bar you from recovering compensation. Florida uses a comparative negligence system, meaning a jury (or the insurance adjuster, informally) assigns a percentage of fault to each party involved, and any damages award is reduced by your own percentage of fault.

Under the modified comparative negligence framework that HB 837 put into place, a plaintiff who is found to be more than 50% at fault for their own injury is generally barred from recovering damages at all. If you're found 50% or less at fault, you can typically still recover, reduced by your share of responsibility. This is a meaningful shift from Florida's prior pure comparative negligence rule, and it makes an early, honest assessment of fault more important than ever.

How to Choose the Right Accident Lawyer in Ormond Beach

Not every personal injury firm is the right fit for every case. Here's what I'd want my own family to check before signing with any attorney:

  • Active Florida Bar license: Every attorney practicing in Florida must be licensed by The Florida Bar and is subject to the Rules Regulating the Florida Bar. You can verify any attorney's license status, and check for public disciplinary history, through The Florida Bar's free online search.
  • Experience with your type of claim: Auto accidents, slip-and-falls, negligent security, and product liability cases each involve different evidence and different defendants — ask directly about the firm's experience with cases like yours.
  • Local court familiarity: A firm that regularly handles Volusia County cases understands local court procedures, judges, and typical insurance company tactics in the area.
  • Clear fee structure: Most Florida personal injury firms work on contingency, meaning you pay nothing unless they recover compensation for you — but get the percentage and any cost arrangements in writing.
  • Direct communication: Ask who will actually handle your case day-to-day, and how often you can expect updates.
A note on pre-suit notice requirements: Certain types of claims — including some against government entities and medical negligence claims — require a formal pre-suit notice before a lawsuit can even be filed, with its own separate deadline. This is another reason a general internet deadline shouldn't substitute for a review of your specific facts.

What to Do Right After an Accident in Ormond Beach

Regardless of where your accident happened in Florida, the same basic steps protect both your health and your legal options:

  • Seek medical evaluation promptly, even if injuries seem minor — some injuries take days to fully present, and prompt treatment also creates a medical record tying your injury to the accident.
  • Report the accident to the appropriate agency (police, property owner, or employer) and get a copy of any official report.
  • Photograph the scene, vehicles, visible injuries, and any hazardous conditions before they change.
  • Get contact and insurance information from all involved parties and witnesses.
  • Be cautious with early settlement offers or recorded statements from insurance adjusters before you understand the full extent of your injuries.
  • Keep records of medical bills, lost wages, and how the injury has affected your daily life.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long do I have to file a personal injury lawsuit in Florida?
For most accidents occurring on or after March 24, 2023, Florida law generally allows two years from the date of the accident to file a negligence-based lawsuit. Accidents before that date may still fall under the prior four-year deadline. Because exceptions apply, confirm your specific deadline with a Florida attorney.
What happens if I was partly at fault for my accident?
Florida's comparative negligence rule allows you to recover damages reduced by your percentage of fault, as long as you are found 50% or less responsible for the accident. If you're found more than 50% at fault, you're generally barred from recovering compensation.
How do I know if an accident lawyer is actually licensed in Florida?
The Florida Bar maintains a free public license search where you can confirm an attorney's active status and check for any public disciplinary history before hiring them.
Does it cost money to talk to an Ormond Beach accident lawyer?
Most Florida personal injury firms offer free initial consultations and work on contingency, meaning they only get paid if they recover compensation for you — but always confirm the fee arrangement in writing before signing.
What types of cases does an Ormond Beach accident lawyer typically handle?
Common case types include car and motorcycle accidents, slip-and-falls, negligent security claims, product liability, and other injuries caused by another party's negligence.
Do I still have a case if my accident happened a while ago?
It depends on exactly when the accident occurred, since the applicable statute of limitations changed for accidents on or after March 24, 2023. Have the specific date reviewed by a Florida attorney rather than assuming either the two-year or four-year rule applies.

If you've been hurt in an accident in or around Ormond Beach, the two facts that matter most right now are the exact date of your accident and how much fault, if any, might be assigned to you — both directly shape whether and how much you can recover under current Florida law.
