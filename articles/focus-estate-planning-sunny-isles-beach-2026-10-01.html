Estate Planning in Sunny Isles Beach, Florida: What You Need to Know in 2026

Quick Answer

Estate planning in Sunny Isles Beach follows Florida's statewide Probate Code (Florida Statutes Chapters 731 through 735) and trust law (Chapter 736), with special attention to homestead rules for beachfront and condo property, and important 2026 updates that raised probate thresholds and gave families new tools against uncooperative banks.

By Arthur Simpson, Esq. · FL Bar #529265 Florida Estate Planning Attorney October 1, 2026

What Makes Estate Planning Sunny Isles Beach Different

In my practice, I meet a lot of Sunny Isles Beach clients who assume their situation is unusual: a high-rise condo on Collins Avenue, a second home used only part of the year, family members scattered between Florida, New York, and overseas. The good news is that Florida law doesn't treat you differently because you live in a coastal condo tower instead of a single-family home inland. The same statewide rules apply, found in the Florida Probate Code (Florida Statutes Chapters 731 through 735) and the Florida Trust Code (Chapter 736).

What does change in a community like Sunny Isles Beach is the practical complexity: condo deeds with multiple owners, rental income from seasonal tenants, foreign buyers and snowbird residents with ties to other states or countries, and valuable beachfront property that deserves careful title and tax planning. A good estate plan accounts for all of that, not just a generic template.

Wills, Trusts, and the Documents Every Plan Should Include

What I tell Florida clients, whether they live in Sunny Isles Beach or anywhere else in the state, is that a complete estate plan is rarely just a will. The core documents I recommend reviewing include:

  • Last Will and Testament, which must meet Florida's formal execution requirements under F.S. § 732.502, including two witnesses and specific signing procedures.
  • Revocable Living Trust under Florida Statutes Chapter 736, often used to avoid probate on real estate or investment accounts and to keep asset details private.
  • Durable Power of Attorney under Florida Statutes Chapter 709, so someone you trust can manage your finances if you become incapacitated.
  • Healthcare Surrogate Designation under F.S. § 765.202 and a Living Will under F.S. § 765.301, so your medical wishes are honored and your family isn't left guessing.

For condo owners in particular, I also look closely at how title is held. The way a deed is titled (individually, jointly with right of survivorship, or in a trust) often determines whether the property passes automatically or must go through probate.

Florida Homestead Rules for Beachfront and Condo Property

Florida's homestead protections, found in Article X, Section 4 of the Florida Constitution, are some of the strongest in the country, and they matter enormously for Sunny Isles Beach residents. Homestead status can shield your primary residence from most creditors and limits how you may devise the property by will if you're survived by a spouse or minor children, under F.S. § 732.4015.

Homestead also affects property taxes through the Save Our Homes assessment cap and portability rules under F.S. § 193.155, which can limit annual increases in assessed value and allow you to transfer accumulated savings to a new Florida homestead. These benefits generally require that the property be your permanent residence, so a seasonal condo used only part of the year may not qualify the same way. This is an area where I strongly encourage clients to have their specific facts reviewed, since the line between a vacation condo and a homestead residence depends on details like voter registration, driver's license address, and time spent in the state.

The 2026 Probate Law Changes Sunny Isles Beach Families Should Know

Florida made meaningful updates to the Probate Code effective July 1, 2026, and they're worth understanding whether you're planning ahead or currently settling a loved one's estate.

What Changed on July 1, 2026 The threshold for summary administration (a faster, simpler probate process) increased from $75,000 to $150,000 in non-exempt assets. Related thresholds also rose: the cap for small-estate disposition without administration increased from $10,000 to $20,000, the decedent income-tax refund claimable without administration increased from $2,500 to $5,000, and the amount financial institutions may release to family by affidavit increased from $1,000 to $2,000.

These increases mean more modest estates, including many held by Sunny Isles Beach retirees, can now qualify for summary administration rather than the longer formal administration process, potentially saving families time and expense.

Florida also added a new enforcement tool. Under new Florida Statute § 733.6125, a personal representative can now go to court when a bank or financial institution refuses to honor valid letters of administration or imposes unreasonable conditions. If the court finds the institution lacked a legitimate basis for stonewalling, the statute requires the institution to pay the personal representative's attorney's fees and costs. A related amendment to F.S. § 655.933 requires banks to grant a personal representative access to a decedent's safe deposit box upon presentation of valid letters of administration. These changes give families real leverage when a bank drags its feet during an already difficult time.

On the trust side, Florida Statute § 736.04117 now gives "authorized trustees" broader flexibility to modify an existing trust or move assets into a new trust, even when the new trust is created specifically for that purpose, often called "decanting." And F.S. § 736.1008(4) clarifies that a trustee's notice of intent to invade trust principal is not itself a "trust disclosure document," which affects certain notice and limitations rules for trustees and beneficiaries.

When Is Probate Required in Florida?

Florida probate is generally required when someone dies owning assets solely in their own name, with no co-owner holding rights of survivorship and no payable-on-death or transfer-on-death designation attached to the account. This is exactly the scenario a well-built estate plan is designed to avoid or simplify, by using joint ownership, beneficiary designations, and revocable trusts where appropriate.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need a Florida attorney for estate planning if I live in Sunny Isles Beach but have assets in another state?
Florida law governs your Florida assets and your Florida homestead, but out-of-state property may be subject to that state's laws as well, so your plan should be coordinated across jurisdictions with input from Florida counsel and, where needed, counsel in the other state.
Does a revocable living trust avoid probate in Florida?
Assets properly titled in the name of a revocable trust generally avoid probate because they pass according to the trust terms rather than through the court, though assets left outside the trust may still require probate.
What changed in Florida probate law on July 1, 2026?
The summary administration threshold rose from $75,000 to $150,000 in non-exempt assets, several related small-estate thresholds increased, and a new statute, F.S. § 733.6125, gives personal representatives a way to compel uncooperative banks to honor valid letters of administration, with fee-shifting if the institution acted without legitimate basis.
Is my Sunny Isles Beach condo protected as homestead property?
It may be, if the condo is your permanent Florida residence under Article X, Section 4 of the Florida Constitution, but seasonal or vacation use alone generally does not qualify, and this determination depends on specific facts that should be reviewed individually.
How do I update my estate plan after Florida's 2026 law changes?
If your plan includes a trust, a will, or references to prior administration thresholds, it's worth having a Florida attorney review the documents to confirm they still reflect current law and your current wishes.

The Truestead Takeaway

Estate planning in Sunny Isles Beach runs on the same Florida statutes that apply statewide, but coastal condo ownership, seasonal residency, and out-of-state family ties make careful, individualized planning especially important here. The 2026 increases to probate thresholds and the new bank-enforcement statute are genuinely good news for Florida families, but they don't replace the need for a plan built around your specific property, your specific family, and your specific goals. If your will, trust, or powers of attorney haven't been reviewed recently, or if you're not sure how homestead rules apply to your property, the sensible next step is a conversation with a Florida estate planning attorney.
