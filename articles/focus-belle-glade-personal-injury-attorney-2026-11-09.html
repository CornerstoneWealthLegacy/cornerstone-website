Belle Glade Personal Injury Attorney: What Florida Law Says About Your Claim

Quick Answer

If you're hurt in or around Belle Glade, Florida law generally gives you two years from the date of injury to file a lawsuit, bars recovery if you're more than 50% at fault, and limits certain medical-bill evidence at trial. How Long Do You Have to File a Claim in Florida?

This is the single most important question I get, and it's also the one where the law changed most recently. Florida's general negligence statute of limitations, found in F.S. § 95.11, was shortened by the 2023 tort reform legislation (HB 837). For causes of action accruing on or after March 24, 2023, Florida law generally allows two years from the date of injury to file a personal injury lawsuit, down from the four years that applied to older claims.

Because that reform is now a few years old, many accidents happening today fall squarely under the two-year rule, and the clock can run out faster than people expect. There are exceptions and nuances, including different rules for claims against government entities, medical malpractice, wrongful death, and cases involving minors, so the exact deadline for any individual case should always be confirmed with a Florida attorney rather than assumed.

⚠ Don't rely on old deadlines. If you or a family member remember hearing "you have four years" to file an injury claim, that was the law before March 24, 2023. Many accidents today fall under the shorter two-year window, so don't wait to have your specific date of injury and deadline confirmed.

Florida's Comparative Negligence Rule: What Happens If You're Partly at Fault

Florida used to allow injured people to recover some compensation even if they were mostly at fault for an accident. That changed under HB 837. Florida now follows a modified comparative negligence standard, codified at F.S. § 768.81: if a jury determines you were more than 50% responsible for your own injury, you are completely barred from recovering any damages at all, not just a reduced amount.

This makes the early investigation of an accident critical. Skid marks, witness statements, surveillance footage, and police reports can all affect how fault gets allocated, and that allocation can be the difference between a full recovery and nothing. This is one of the most consequential changes in modern Florida injury law, and it applies the same way whether the accident happened in Belle Glade, Pahokee, or anywhere else in the state.

What Compensation Can You Recover?

Florida personal injury damages are generally divided into two categories:

  • Economic damages: medical bills, future medical care, lost wages, and lost earning capacity.
  • Non-economic damages: pain and suffering, physical impairment, and mental anguish.

HB 837 also changed how medical bills are presented to a jury. Under F.S. § 768.0427, when past medical expenses have already been paid and satisfied, evidence of those costs is generally limited to the amounts actually paid, rather than the full amount originally billed, regardless of who paid them. This can meaningfully affect how a jury calculates the economic side of a claim, which is another reason an experienced attorney's early case valuation matters.

In rare cases involving especially reckless or intentional conduct, Florida law also allows punitive damages, which are generally capped under F.S. § 768.73 at the greater of three times the compensatory damages or a set statutory amount. Punitive damages are the exception, not the rule, in most injury cases.

What to Do After an Accident Near Belle Glade

What I tell Florida clients, whether they're calling from Belle Glade, Pahokee, South Bay, or anywhere in the Glades, is to focus on a few practical steps early:

  • Seek medical evaluation promptly, even if injuries seem minor at first.
  • Report the incident to the appropriate authority (law enforcement for a crash, a property owner or manager for a premises incident).
  • Keep records: photos, witness names, medical bills, and correspondence with insurers.
  • Avoid giving a recorded statement to an insurance adjuster before speaking with an attorney.
  • Have your specific accident date reviewed against Florida's current statute of limitations as soon as possible.
Why timing matters. Florida's two-year deadline compresses the time available to investigate a claim, gather evidence, and negotiate with insurers. The earlier a case is reviewed, the more options are typically available.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need a local Belle Glade attorney, or can any Florida injury attorney handle my case?
Florida personal injury law applies statewide, so a licensed Florida attorney can generally handle a Belle Glade case. Many clients prefer an attorney or firm with experience serving western Palm Beach County because of familiarity with local courts, medical providers, and insurance practices in the area.
How long do I have to file a personal injury lawsuit in Florida?
For most injuries occurring on or after March 24, 2023, Florida law generally allows two years from the date of injury to file suit under F.S. 95.11, though exceptions exist for certain claims. Your exact deadline should be confirmed with a Florida attorney based on your specific accident date.
What happens if I was partly responsible for the accident?
Florida follows modified comparative negligence under F.S. 768.81. If you are found 50% or less at fault, you can still recover damages, reduced by your percentage of fault, but if you are found more than 50% at fault, you cannot recover any compensation.
Will my medical bills be fully presented to a jury?
Not necessarily. Under F.S. 768.0427, when past medical expenses have already been paid, evidence at trial is generally limited to the amounts actually paid rather than the original billed amount, which can affect the calculated value of a claim.
Are there caps on how much I can recover?
Florida does not generally cap compensatory damages like medical bills or pain and suffering in most standard personal injury cases, but punitive damages, which are awarded only in cases of especially reckless conduct, are generally capped under F.S. 768.73.
What should I do first after being injured near Belle Glade?
Seek medical care, document the scene and your injuries, avoid giving recorded statements to insurance adjusters without guidance, and have your case and filing deadline reviewed by a Florida attorney as soon as reasonably possible.

If you've been hurt in or around Belle Glade, the most important thing to understand is that Florida's injury laws have changed in recent years, particularly the shortened filing deadline and the stricter comparative fault rule, and those changes can meaningfully affect what you're entitled to recover.
