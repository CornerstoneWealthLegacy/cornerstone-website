Florida Probate Attorney Silver Springs: What Families Need to Know in 2026

Quick Answer

Do You Actually Need a Lawyer for Florida Probate?

In nearly every case, yes. Under the Florida Probate Rules, a personal representative must be represented by a Florida-licensed attorney unless that personal representative happens to be the sole interested person in the estate, meaning there are no other heirs, beneficiaries, or creditors with a stake in the outcome. For most families, with multiple heirs or any complexity at all, that exception doesn't apply.

  • Formal administration almost always requires an attorney
  • Summary administration has more flexibility, but legal review is still strongly advisable
  • Disposition without administration (for very small, simple estates) has the fewest formal requirements

This isn't a technicality designed to generate legal fees. Probate involves notifying creditors, following strict statutory timelines, and distributing assets correctly under Florida law, and mistakes can create personal liability for the person serving as personal representative.

What Changed on July 1, 2026, and Why It Matters for Your Estate

Florida probate law went through a meaningful update in 2026 that directly affects how many Silver Springs and Marion County families will handle a loved one's estate going forward.

The Big Change: Effective July 1, 2026, the threshold for Florida's simplified summary administration procedure doubled from $75,000 to $150,000 in estate value (Florida Statutes § 735.201). This means far more modest, middle-class estates, think a home, a bank account, and some savings, can now avoid the longer, costlier formal administration process entirely.

Alongside that, the limit for disposition without administration, Florida's streamlined path for very small estates with only personal property, increased from $10,000 to $20,000 under Florida Statutes § 735.304. For a family dealing with a modest estate (a car, furniture, a small bank account), this can mean a much faster, simpler resolution without a full probate case.

There's also a new tool that benefits personal representatives directly. Florida Statutes § 733.6125, created by the 2026 legislation, gives a personal representative the right to go to court if a bank or financial institution refuses to honor valid Letters of Administration or demands conditions the law doesn't require. If the institution is found to have stonewalled without a legitimate legal basis, the statute requires that institution to pay the personal representative's attorney's fees and costs, a meaningful protection for families who have run into resistance from banks holding a deceased relative's accounts.

How Long Does Florida Probate Take, and What Should You Expect?

Florida law generally expects a personal representative to complete estate administration within 12 months of appointment, under Florida Statutes § 733.901, although many estates reasonably take longer depending on complexity, creditor claims, or disputes among beneficiaries. Along the way, the personal representative has specific notice obligations:

  • Notice to beneficiaries of the estate proceedings, as required under Florida Statutes § 733.212
  • Notice to creditors, allowing them a statutory window to file claims, under Florida Statutes § 733.2121
  • Ongoing duties to inventory, manage, and eventually distribute estate assets correctly

With the 2026 threshold increases now in effect, I'm seeing more Marion County estates qualify for the faster summary administration track, which can often resolve in a matter of months rather than a year or more, though every estate's timeline depends on its specific facts.

⚠ A Common Misstep: Families sometimes assume that because an estate seems small or simple, they can handle probate entirely without legal guidance. Even under the new, higher thresholds, determining whether an estate truly qualifies for summary administration or disposition without administration requires a careful look at asset types, titling, and outstanding debts, something best confirmed with a Florida probate attorney before filing anything with the court.

What This Means If You're Settling a Loved One's Estate Near Silver Springs

If you've recently lost a family member and are trying to figure out next steps, the 2026 changes are genuinely good news for many Marion County families. More estates now qualify for Florida's simpler, faster probate procedures, and personal representatives have stronger legal tools to deal with uncooperative financial institutions. But every estate is different, and the right path, summary administration, formal administration, or disposition without administration, depends on the specific assets, debts, and family situation involved.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the new summary administration threshold in Florida for 2026?
As of July 1, 2026, Florida Statutes § 735.201 allows summary administration for estates valued up to $150,000, doubled from the prior $75,000 threshold, giving more families access to this simpler, faster probate process.
Can I do Florida probate without a lawyer?
In most cases, no. Florida Probate Rule 5.030 requires a personal representative to be represented by a Florida-licensed attorney unless that person is the sole interested party in the estate, which is uncommon when there are multiple heirs or beneficiaries.
How long does probate take in Florida?
Florida Statutes § 733.901 generally expects estate administration to be completed within 12 months of the personal representative's appointment, though actual timelines vary depending on the estate's complexity, creditor claims, and whether any disputes arise.
What happens if a bank won't release a deceased relative's funds to the estate?
As of July 1, 2026, Florida Statutes § 733.6125 allows a personal representative to petition the court to enforce their authority, and if the financial institution is found to have refused without a legitimate legal basis, the institution must pay the personal representative's attorney's fees and costs.
Does Silver Springs have its own probate court?
No, Silver Springs is part of Marion County, and probate matters for the area are generally handled through the Marion County courthouse in Ocala, which is also where most local probate attorneys serving Silver Springs residents are based.
What's the difference between summary administration and formal administration?
Summary administration is a faster, simplified process available to qualifying smaller estates (now up to $150,000 under the 2026 changes), while formal administration is the standard, more involved process required for larger or more complex estates, typically involving a court-appointed personal representative overseeing the full process.

The Truestead Takeaway

Florida's 2026 probate reforms are a real win for many Silver Springs and Marion County families, expanding access to faster, less expensive probate procedures and giving personal representatives better tools to deal with uncooperative banks. But whether an estate truly qualifies for summary administration, disposition without administration, or needs full formal administration depends on the specific facts, and getting that determination right matters. If you're facing probate for a loved one's estate, the sensible next step is a conversation with a Florida probate attorney who can review the actual assets, debts, and family circumstances involved before you file anything with the court.
