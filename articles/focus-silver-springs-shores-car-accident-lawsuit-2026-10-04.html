Silver Springs Shores Car Accident Lawsuit: What Florida Law Says

Quick Answer

If you were hurt in a car accident in Silver Springs Shores, Florida law generally gives you two years from the crash date to file a lawsuit against the at-fault driver, but you must also seek medical treatment within 14 days to preserve your no-fault (PIP) insurance benefits. Whether you can step outside the no-fault system and sue depends on whether your injuries meet Florida's serious injury threshold.

By Arthur Simpson, Esq. · FL Bar #529265 Florida Personal Injury Attorney October 4, 2026

Is There an Actual "Silver Springs Shores Car Accident Lawsuit" Case?

I get this search a lot, and I want to answer it honestly: there is no single, named Silver Springs Shores car accident lawsuit that has set precedent or made statewide legal news. What that search phrase usually reflects is someone who was in a crash in this Marion County community, near I-75, US-441, or SR-464, and wants to know whether they have a case and what Florida law requires.

That said, the stakes behind this question are real. In April 2026, a fatal two-vehicle crash occurred at the intersection of Pine Road and Hemlock Road in Silver Springs Shores, involving a Hyundai Elantra and an Infiniti Q50. One occupant was ejected from a vehicle, and a passenger was killed. The Florida Highway Patrol retained one of the vehicles as part of its investigation. As of this writing, no published lawsuit tied to that crash has been reported, but it illustrates exactly the kind of serious injury and wrongful death claim that can arise on local roads here, and why understanding the legal framework matters before you talk to an insurance adjuster.

How Long Do I Have to File a Car Accident Lawsuit in Florida?

This is the single most important deadline in any Florida car accident case. Under Florida Statutes § 95.11(4)(a), the statute of limitations for most negligence claims, including car accidents, is two years from the date of the crash. This two-year window applies to accidents occurring on or after March 24, 2023, following the passage of House Bill 837, which cut the prior four-year deadline in half.

The clock starts running on the date of the collision itself, not when your symptoms worsen, not when you finish treatment, and not when an insurance company denies your claim. Missing this deadline generally means losing your right to sue entirely, regardless of how strong your case otherwise is.

⚠ Don't Wait to Confirm Your Deadline Because the law changed in 2023 and some older sources online still cite the previous four-year rule, anyone hurt in a Silver Springs Shores crash should confirm the exact filing deadline for their specific accident date with a Florida attorney rather than relying on general articles.

What Is PIP Coverage and Why Does the 14-Day Rule Matter?

Florida is a no-fault insurance state, meaning your own Personal Injury Protection (PIP) coverage is typically the first source of payment after a crash, regardless of who caused it. Florida law requires all registered vehicles to carry PIP, which generally covers 80% of reasonable medical expenses and 60% of lost wages up to your policy limits.

Here is the detail that trips up a lot of good people: you must seek initial medical treatment within 14 days of the accident for PIP benefits to apply. If you wait longer than that, even if your injuries are genuinely related to the crash, you can forfeit PIP coverage entirely. In my practice, this is one of the most common and most avoidable mistakes I see, especially when someone feels fine immediately after a collision and assumes they don't need to be checked out.

  • Seek medical evaluation within 14 days, even if injuries seem minor
  • Keep records of all treatment and follow-up care
  • Understand that PIP is separate from any lawsuit against the at-fault driver

When Can I Sue the At-Fault Driver Instead of Just Using PIP?

Florida's no-fault system is designed to resolve most minor accident claims through PIP without lawsuits. But when injuries are serious, Florida law allows an injured person to step outside the no-fault system and bring a full negligence claim against the at-fault driver.

To do this, your injuries generally must meet Florida's serious injury threshold, which includes significant or permanent loss of an important bodily function, permanent injury within a reasonable degree of medical probability, significant and permanent scarring or disfigurement, or death. If your injuries meet this standard, you may be able to pursue both economic damages (medical bills, lost income, rehabilitation costs) and non-economic damages (pain and suffering, loss of enjoyment of life, disfigurement, and in wrongful death cases, loss of companionship for surviving family members).

Why This Matters in Fatal Crashes When a crash results in a death, as in the April 2026 Pine Road and Hemlock Road collision, the no-fault PIP system no longer governs the case. Surviving family members may have the right to pursue a wrongful death claim under Florida law, which is a distinct legal process from an ordinary injury claim and should be reviewed with an attorney promptly.

What If I Was Partly at Fault for the Accident?

Florida used to follow a "pure" comparative negligence rule, meaning you could recover damages even if you were mostly responsible for your own injuries, just reduced by your percentage of fault. HB 837 changed this in 2023. Florida now follows a modified comparative negligence system: if you are found to be more than 50% at fault for the accident, you are barred from recovering any damages at all.

This makes the early investigation of a crash, including police reports, witness statements, and roadway conditions at intersections like those found throughout Silver Springs Shores, especially important. How fault gets allocated can determine not just how much you recover, but whether you recover anything.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need a lawyer for a car accident in Silver Springs Shores?
Not every accident requires a lawsuit, but if you suffered a serious injury, missed the 14-day PIP treatment window, or there's a dispute over fault, having a Florida attorney review your situation can help protect your rights before deadlines pass.
What if the accident happened before March 2023?
The statute of limitations that applies depends on the date of your specific accident, since the law changed with HB 837 in March 2023. Older crashes may still fall under the prior four-year deadline, so you should confirm your exact timeline with an attorney.
Can I still sue if I was partially at fault for the crash?
Yes, as long as you are found to be 50% or less at fault. If you are found more than 50% responsible, Florida's modified comparative negligence rule bars recovery entirely.
What damages can I recover in a Florida car accident lawsuit?
Generally, economic damages like medical bills and lost income, and non-economic damages like pain and suffering, disfigurement, and diminished quality of life, assuming your injuries meet Florida's serious injury threshold.
What happens if a family member died in a Silver Springs Shores crash?
Surviving family members may be able to bring a wrongful death claim under Florida law, which is a separate legal process from a standard injury claim and has its own requirements that should be reviewed with an attorney as soon as possible.
Is PIP coverage enough to cover my medical bills after a serious accident?
Often not. PIP covers only a percentage of medical expenses and lost wages up to your policy limits, which is why serious injury cases frequently require pursuing a claim against the at-fault driver once PIP is exhausted.

The Truestead Takeaway

A Silver Springs Shores car accident lawsuit isn't a single case you can look up, it's the legal path available to anyone seriously hurt on local roads once PIP coverage falls short and the injury meets Florida's serious injury threshold. The two most important things to remember are the 14-day window to seek treatment for PIP purposes and the two-year deadline to file suit under current Florida law. If you or a family member were involved in a crash in this area, especially one involving serious injury or a fatality, the right next step is to have your specific facts and timeline reviewed by a Florida attorney rather than relying on general deadlines you find online.
