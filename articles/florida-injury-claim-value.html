What Affects the Value of a Florida Personal Injury Claim

Quick Answer

No honest calculator can promise a settlement amount — value depends on your specific facts. But the major drivers are clear: medical expenses, lost income, the severity and permanence of the injury, who was at fault, and the available insurance.

By Arthur Simpson, Esq. · FL Bar #529265 Florida Personal Injury Attorney June 22, 2026

Why There's No Magic Number

Be wary of any "settlement calculator" that spits out a dollar figure. Every claim is different, and Florida law (and ethics rules) caution against predicting outcomes. What an attorney can do is identify the factors that move value and build the case to maximize them.

Economic Damages

These are the measurable, out-of-pocket losses:

  • Medical expenses — past and reasonably expected future care.
  • Lost wages and, for serious injuries, lost earning capacity.
  • Out-of-pocket costs tied to the injury.

Thorough documentation — bills, records, and proof of missed work — is what makes these damages stick.

Non-Economic Damages

These compensate for the human cost — pain and suffering, disfigurement, and loss of enjoyment of life. They're harder to quantify and depend heavily on the severity and permanence of the injury and how it affects daily living. (Remember: PIP does not pay these; you generally need a serious injury and a claim against the at-fault party.)

The Practical Limits: Fault and Insurance

⚠ Two factors can cap everything. First, fault: under Florida's modified comparative negligence rule (F.S. § 768.81), your share of fault reduces recovery, and more than 50% bars it. Second, insurance limits: a claim is often only as collectible as the available coverage — which is why uninsured/underinsured motorist coverage can matter enormously.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can you tell me what my case is worth?
Not from a questionnaire. Value is fact-specific and no ethical attorney or tool will promise a number up front. A consultation can identify the factors at play and a realistic range as the case develops.
What's the difference between economic and non-economic damages?
Economic damages are measurable losses like medical bills and lost wages. Non-economic damages compensate for pain and suffering, disfigurement, and loss of enjoyment of life.
How does fault affect my recovery?
Under F.S. § 768.81, your damages are reduced by your percentage of fault, and if you are more than 50% at fault you recover nothing.
Why do insurance limits matter so much?
A claim is generally only collectible up to the available coverage. If the at-fault party is underinsured, your own uninsured/underinsured motorist coverage may be the key to recovery.

The Truestead Takeaway

Anyone promising a dollar figure before reviewing your facts is guessing. What actually drives value is documentation, injury severity, fault, and available insurance. A free, no-pressure review will give you an honest read on your options — without the hype.
