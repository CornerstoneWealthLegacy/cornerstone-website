Quitclaim, Warranty, and Lady Bird Deeds: What Floridians Should Know Before They Sign

Quick Answer

In Florida, the type of deed you sign determines how much protection you get. A warranty deed offers the strongest title guarantees, a quitclaim deed offers none, and a lady bird (enhanced life estate) deed lets you keep full control of your home during life while passing it automatically at death — often avoiding probate.

By Arthur Simpson, Esq. · FL Bar #529265 Florida Real Estate Attorney June 24, 2026

Why does the type of deed matter so much in Florida?

In my practice, I see homeowners sign deeds without understanding what they're actually getting — or giving up. A deed is the legal instrument that transfers ownership of real property, but not all deeds promise the same thing. The difference between a warranty deed, a quitclaim deed, and a lady bird deed isn't just paperwork. It can decide whether you have any recourse if a title problem surfaces years later, whether your home sails through to your children, or whether it gets tangled up in probate.

Here's what I'd want my own family to know: the right deed depends entirely on what you're trying to accomplish. Buying from a stranger calls for one kind of protection. Adding a spouse calls for another. Planning your estate calls for something different still.

What does a warranty deed actually guarantee?

A general warranty deed is the gold standard when you're buying property. With it, the seller promises that they own the property free and clear, that they have the right to sell it, and that they'll defend your title against anyone's claim — even one that arose before they owned it. In other words, the seller stands behind the title and is on the hook if a defect appears.

Florida also recognizes a special warranty deed (sometimes called a limited warranty deed), where the seller only warrants against problems that arose during their own period of ownership — not before. You'll often see special warranty deeds in commercial transactions, bank sales, and some new-construction closings.

What I tell buyers A warranty deed and an owner's title insurance policy do different jobs. The deed gives you a promise from the seller; title insurance gives you a company that pays to defend or settle covered claims. Smart Florida buyers want both.

When is a quitclaim deed appropriate — and when is it risky?

A quitclaim deed transfers whatever interest the signer happens to have in the property — and makes no promises whatsoever. If the person signing owns nothing, you receive nothing, and you have no warranty to fall back on. That sounds alarming, but quitclaim deeds have legitimate, everyday uses among people who already trust one another.

In Florida, quitclaim deeds are commonly used to:

  • Add or remove a spouse from title after marriage or divorce
  • Transfer property between family members
  • Clear up a minor title cloud, such as a misspelled name or a long-resolved interest
  • Move property into a revocable living trust
⚠ Be careful Never accept a quitclaim deed from someone you don't know in an arm's-length purchase. You'd be buying a house with zero assurance the seller even owns it. And before any transfer involving a married person's primary residence, remember Florida's homestead protections — a spouse generally must join in the deed.

What is a lady bird deed, and why do Floridians love it?

A lady bird deed — formally an enhanced life estate deed — is one of the most useful estate-planning tools available to Florida homeowners. It lets you keep complete control of your home for the rest of your life: you can sell it, mortgage it, rent it, or change your mind entirely, all without anyone else's permission. Then, at your death, the property passes automatically to the people you named (the remainder beneficiaries) — typically without going through probate.

Because you retain full control during your lifetime, a lady bird deed generally does not count as a completed gift while you're alive, and it usually does not disturb your homestead tax exemption or your Save Our Homes assessment cap. Many of my clients choose it precisely because it's simple, revocable, and avoids the cost and delay of probate for the home — often their most valuable asset. Florida is one of only a handful of states that recognizes this kind of deed.

How do deeds interact with Florida homestead protections?

Florida's homestead rules — found in Article X, § 4 of the Florida Constitution and reinforced by statutes like F.S. § 732.4015 governing the devise of homestead — can override what a deed appears to do. If you're married, you generally cannot deed away or freely will your homestead without your spouse's participation, and minor children create additional restrictions on how homestead may pass.

This is why a deed that looks fine on its face can later be challenged. Before signing any deed touching a Florida residence, the homestead question should be addressed first. Note too that recording a deed in Florida generally triggers documentary stamp tax under F.S. § 201.02, though certain transfers — such as those into a revocable trust for no consideration — may be treated differently.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does a quitclaim deed remove me from the mortgage?
No. A quitclaim deed only transfers ownership interest. The mortgage is a separate contract with the lender, and you remain personally responsible for that debt until it's paid off or refinanced into someone else's name.
Will a lady bird deed avoid probate in Florida?
In most cases, yes — the property passes directly to the named remainder beneficiaries at your death without probate. Because situations vary, the deed and your overall plan should be reviewed by a Florida attorney.
Can I just download a deed form online and record it myself?
You can, but I'd caution against it. A small error — wrong legal description, missing spouse signature, an overlooked homestead issue, or improper notarization — can cloud your title for years. Florida deeds have specific execution and witness requirements.
Is a warranty deed always better than a quitclaim deed?
Not always — it depends on the goal. A warranty deed is best when buying from a non-family seller. A quitclaim deed is perfectly appropriate for trusted family transfers or moving property into your own trust.
Does putting my home in a trust affect my homestead exemption?
Often it does not, when done correctly with a properly drafted revocable trust, but the details matter. Transferring homestead into a trust should be handled carefully to preserve both the tax exemption and creditor protections.
Do I still need title insurance if I have a warranty deed?
Yes, I'd recommend it. A warranty deed gives you a promise from the seller, but title insurance gives you a company obligated to defend or pay covered claims — a far more practical safety net.

The Truestead Takeaway

The deed you sign quietly decides how much protection you have and how smoothly your property passes one day — so it deserves more thought than a quick signature at a closing table. If you're buying, insist on the right warranty and title insurance. If you're transferring among family or planning your estate, a quitclaim or lady bird deed may fit beautifully, but only if Florida's homestead rules are handled correctly first.
