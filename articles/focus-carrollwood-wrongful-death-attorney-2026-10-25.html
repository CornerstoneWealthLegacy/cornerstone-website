Carrollwood Wrongful Death Attorney: What Florida Families Need to Know

Quick Answer

If you lost a loved one because of someone else's negligence in or around Carrollwood, Florida law generally gives the decedent's personal representative two years from the date of death to file suit, with damages and procedures governed by Florida's Wrongful Death Act (Chapter 768). The right attorney for your family is one who knows this statute well, moves quickly to preserve evidence, and is honest with you about what Florida law does and doesn't allow in your specific situation.

What does a Carrollwood wrongful death attorney actually do?

When people search for a Carrollwood wrongful death attorney, they're usually looking for someone local who understands both the legal process and the practical reality of losing a family member suddenly and unfairly. In my practice, that means two things at once: knowing Florida's Wrongful Death Act inside and out, and being someone a grieving family can actually lean on while the case moves forward.

Carrollwood is an unincorporated community in Hillsborough County, part of the greater Tampa area. Wrongful death cases arising there are typically filed in the Hillsborough County Circuit Court. There's no special Carrollwood-only rule of law. What matters is that your attorney understands Florida's statewide Wrongful Death Act, found in Chapter 768 of the Florida Statutes, and knows how to apply it correctly from day one.

  • Identifying and working with the court-appointed personal representative of the estate, who is the only person legally authorized to bring the claim

  • Calculating the damages survivors and the estate are entitled to under the statute
  • Dealing with insurance companies, defense counsel, and, where applicable, government agencies

How does Florida's Wrongful Death Act work?

Florida's Wrongful Death Act provides that when a death is caused by a wrongful act, negligence, default, or breach of contract or warranty, the person or entity who would have been legally responsible for the injury remains responsible even though the injured person died. The lawsuit is not brought by the surviving family members directly. It is brought by the personal representative of the decedent's estate, who pursues the claim for the benefit of both the estate and the decedent's statutory survivors.

Survivors who may be entitled to recover damages generally include the surviving spouse, children, and parents, and in some circumstances other blood relatives or adoptive siblings who were partly or wholly dependent on the decedent. The specific damages available, such as loss of support and services, loss of companionship, and mental pain and suffering, depend on the survivor's relationship to the decedent and the facts of the case.

What is Florida's 'Free Kill' law, and is it still in effect?

This is one of the most important and least understood parts of Florida wrongful death law right now, and it's a topic every family evaluating a medical negligence death needs to understand. Under Florida Statute 768.21(8), when a death is caused by medical negligence, certain adult children and parents are barred from recovering noneconomic damages (such as pain and suffering or loss of companionship) that would otherwise be available. Specifically, this carve-out applies to adult children age 25 or older who lose a parent to medical negligence, and to parents who lose an adult child age 25 or older to medical negligence.

⚠ Still the Law in 2026 There has been significant legislative activity to repeal this rule, often called the 'Free Kill' law by advocates. A 2025 repeal bill passed both chambers of the Legislature but was vetoed by the Governor. A similar 2026 effort passed the Florida House but did not clear the Senate. As of today, the medical negligence carve-out in Section 768.21(8) remains in effect. If your family's loss involves a hospital, doctor, or other healthcare provider, this limitation may directly affect what damages are available, and it should be discussed candidly with your attorney early on.

Importantly, this limitation applies specifically to medical negligence cases. It does not apply the same way to wrongful death claims arising from car accidents, premises liability, workplace incidents, or other forms of ordinary negligence, where adult children and parents generally retain their full rights under the statute.

How long do I have to file a wrongful death claim in Florida?

Under Florida Statute 95.11(5)(e), a wrongful death action generally must be filed within two years of the date of the decedent's death. This is distinct from, and shorter than, the filing deadline for many other types of personal injury claims, so it's important not to assume the timeline that applies to a typical injury case also applies here.

There is a notable exception: when a death results from an act that would constitute murder or manslaughter, Florida law does not impose the standard time limit, and the civil wrongful death action may be brought at any time. No criminal arrest, charge, or conviction is required for this exception to apply. This exception applies to claims against a natural person responsible for the death, not to related claims against businesses or government entities, which remain subject to their own deadlines.

If the death resulted from medical malpractice, a different framework applies: generally two years from when the malpractice was discovered or reasonably should have been discovered, subject to an outer limit of four years from the date of the incident, with some additional exceptions. Because these rules can be fact-specific and have been affected by recent tort reform legislation, anyone facing this situation should have the applicable deadline confirmed directly by a Florida attorney rather than relying on a general rule of thumb.

What if the death was caused by a government entity or employee?

Wrongful death claims against a city, county, state agency, or other government body in Florida are subject to sovereign immunity limits under Florida Statute 768.28. As of now, those statutory caps remain $200,000 per person and $300,000 per incident. There was legislative movement in 2026 to raise these caps significantly, but that increase did not take effect, meaning the current, lower caps continue to apply to claims against government entities.

Why Early Action Matters Claims against government entities also come with strict pre-suit notice requirements and short deadlines separate from the general wrongful death statute of limitations. Evidence such as 911 call recordings, dashcam footage, incident reports, and witness statements can also disappear quickly after an accident. Consulting an attorney promptly, rather than waiting, gives your case the best chance of being built on solid, well-preserved evidence.

Frequently Asked Questions

Who is legally allowed to file a wrongful death lawsuit in Florida?
Only the personal representative of the decedent's estate can file the lawsuit. That person pursues the case on behalf of both the estate and the decedent's eligible survivors, such as a spouse, children, or parents, as defined under Florida's Wrongful Death Act.
What damages can my family recover in a Florida wrongful death case?
Depending on your relationship to the decedent, recoverable damages can include lost support and services, loss of companionship and protection, mental pain and suffering, and funeral and medical expenses, among others. The specific categories available depend on the survivor's relationship to the decedent and, in medical negligence cases, may be limited by Section 768.21(8).
Is Florida's 'Free Kill' law still in effect in 2026?
Yes. Despite repeal efforts in both the 2025 and 2026 legislative sessions, the medical negligence damages limitation in Florida Statute 768.21(8) remains current law as of this writing.
How long do I have to file a wrongful death claim after a car accident or other negligence?
Generally two years from the date of death under Florida Statute 95.11(5)(e), though exceptions and related deadlines (such as pre-suit notice for government claims) can apply. Confirm your specific deadline with a Florida attorney as soon as possible.
Does it matter that the death happened in Carrollwood specifically?
Carrollwood itself doesn't have special wrongful death laws. Cases arising there are typically governed by statewide Florida law and generally filed in Hillsborough County Circuit Court, so what matters most is finding an attorney who knows Florida's Wrongful Death Act thoroughly.
What if the person responsible for the death was never criminally charged?
A criminal charge or conviction is not required to bring a civil wrongful death claim. The civil case is independent, and even the murder/manslaughter exception to the filing deadline does not require an arrest or conviction to apply.

The Truestead Takeaway

Losing a family member because of someone else's negligence is devastating, and Florida law tries to give surviving families a path to accountability and compensation, but that path has real deadlines and real limitations, including the two-year filing window and the ongoing medical negligence carve-out in Section 768.21(8). Every family's situation is different, especially when it involves a healthcare provider or a government entity, so the sensible next step is to have your specific facts reviewed by a Florida wrongful death attorney who can explain exactly how these rules apply to your case.
