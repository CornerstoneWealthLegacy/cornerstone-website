Signing Checklist for Your Four Documents

Florida execution requirements. A document signed incorrectly is not valid — follow each step exactly.

⚠ Most important: Your Durable Power of Attorney must be signed in front of two witnesses AND a notary public at the same time. If you skip the notary, or use fewer than two qualified witnesses, the power of attorney is void. Plan to sign with a notary present.

1. Health Care Surrogate Designation (Fla. Stat. § 765.202)

Sign before TWO adult witnesses.

  • At least one witness must NOT be a spouse or blood relative of the young adult.
  • The person named as surrogate should not act as a witness.
  • Notary is not required, but is fine to add.

2. HIPAA Authorization (45 CFR § 164.508)

Signed and dated by the young adult.

  • No witnesses or notary required.
  • Give a copy to the young adult's doctors / student health center; keep one with the surrogate.

3. Durable Power of Attorney (Fla. Stat. § 709.2105)

Sign before TWO witnesses AND a notary public — all present together.

  • Both witnesses watch the young adult sign, then sign themselves.
  • The notary completes the acknowledgment and applies the notary seal.
  • The named agent should not serve as a witness or the notary.
  • Banks may also ask for their own form — present this DPOA and ask.

4. FERPA Consent / Waiver (20 U.S.C. § 1232g)

Signed by the student. No witnesses or notary required.

  • Your packet includes a completed FERPA consent — the student just signs and dates it.
  • Most colleges also require their own FERPA form in the student portal; complete that too.
  • Send a copy to the registrar / bursar as directed by the school.

Where to Find a Notary

Most banks and credit unions notarize for account holders (often free), as do UPS Stores, AAA offices, and many libraries and shipping stores. Remote online notarization is also available in Florida. Bring a valid photo ID.
