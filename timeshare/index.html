Timeshare Release

A Florida lawyer for owners facing a timeshare foreclosure notice.

If a trustee has sent you a Notice of Default and Intent to Foreclose, or a Notice of Trustee's Sale, on a Florida timeshare, Florida law gives you specific rights and a short clock. Truestead Law represents owners only. We seek a negotiated conveyance of the interest back to the resort's developer or managing entity with a written release, and we tell you plainly when you do not need us.

Quick Answer

What the notice means, and the clock it starts.

The association or the lender has appointed a trustee to foreclose its lien without a court, under Florida Statutes section 721.855 (unpaid assessments) or section 721.856 (a developer loan). The notice must state the amounts secured by the lien and a daily rate, tell you how to cure, and enclose an objection form. You may cure by paying the full secured amount in certified funds any time before the trustee issues the certificate of sale. There is no right of redemption after that.

You may not need a lawyer. Read this before you pay anyone.

Under sections 721.855(8)(c) and 721.856(8)(c), a completed trustee sale releases the owner from all amounts secured by the lien being foreclosed, and the lienholder may not seek a deficiency judgment after it. A trustee sale on a loan also cancels the note. If ending ownership is your only goal, you can let that process run, and you can ask the resort directly about its own hardship or deedback program. What the statute does not release is anything outside that lien: a separate loan, club dues, or assessments not included in the recorded claim of lien. A lawyer is useful when you want a negotiated release with written terms, when a loan and an assessment account both exist, when collectors are involved, or when you want to control what appears in the public record.

What We Do

Representation for Florida timeshare owners.

Notice and Account Review

We check the official records first (if a trustee's deed is already recorded, there is nothing to exit and no fee), then review your deed, statements, loan and every notice you received.

Representation Letter and Estoppel

Once you are represented, Florida law (§ 559.72(18)) bars anyone collecting the debt who knows it from communicating with you instead of us. We request the managing entity's estoppel certificate under § 721.15(7)(b), which it must provide within 30 days.

Written Advice on Every Path

The resort's own program, a negotiated conveyance with release, and the statutory trustee process, including what the objection form does and does not do. You choose, in writing.

Negotiated Deed Back to the Resort

We seek a conveyance to the developer or managing entity against a signed release of all assessments, loan balance, interest and fees, a waiver of any deficiency, recall from collections, and credit-reporting terms where the resort agrees. We never transfer your interest to a third party and we never take title ourselves.

Deed, Recording, Closing

Deed prepared to Florida formalities, signed by remote online notarization from anywhere, recorded, delivered to the managing entity within 15 days as § 721.15(7)(a) requires, with a closing letter and the signed release.

Inherited Timeshares

Heirs who have not accepted an inherited timeshare may disclaim it under Florida's Chapter 739, a written, witnessed and recorded disclaimer, before any payment, use or listing.

The fee is an advance fee held in trust. Costs are separate and are owed regardless of outcome. The association's estoppel certificate fee (capped at $150 by statute), county recording fees, documentary stamp tax on any debt released (70 cents per $100), notary fees, and any transfer or program fee the resort charges. We state each cost before it is incurred.

We do not advise anyone to stop paying. Whether to keep paying is your decision after written advice. Not paying can lead to foreclosure, collection, credit reporting, a money judgment if the lienholder proceeds judicially, and a Form 1099-C for cancelled debt.

FAQ

The notice must state the amounts secured by the lien and a per diem, explain how to cure, and enclose an objection form. A sale may occur no sooner than 30 days after the notice is perfected and 30 days after a notice of sale is recorded.

Signing it stops the trustee process for that default and leaves the lienholder only a judicial foreclosure, where a money judgment plus attorney's fees (assessments) or a deficiency (a loan) becomes legally available. Not signing preserves the no-deficiency protection of the trustee sale. It is a decision to make with advice. We never file one without your separate signed instruction.
