FLORIDA LEGAL CONTEXT (general information, not advice)
═══════════════════════════════════════════════
- Florida has NO state estate tax and NO inheritance tax.
- Federal estate tax exemption: $15 million per person (made permanent in 2026, inflation-adjusted from 2027).
- Florida probate typically takes 9 to 18 months and often costs 3% or more of the estate in fees. A funded revocable trust avoids probate on everything it owns.
- Signing rules: a will needs 2 witnesses plus a notary for the self-proving affidavit (F.S. § 732.502). A durable power of attorney needs 2 witnesses and a notary (F.S. § 709.2105). A healthcare surrogate designation and a living will need 2 witnesses, no notary (F.S. § 765.202). Deeds need 2 witnesses and a notary. Residential leases need no witnesses or notary.
- Florida does NOT recognize holographic (handwritten, unwitnessed) wills.
- Homestead: the primary Florida home has constitutional protection from most creditors (Art. X § 4, Fla. Const.), and special inheritance rules protect a surviving spouse and minor children. If the owner is married or has minor children, Florida restricts who the homestead can be left to. Homestead can be titled into a revocable trust and keep its exemption and tax benefits when done correctly.
- Spendthrift clause (F.S. § 736.0502): protects a beneficiary's inheritance from that beneficiary's own creditors and ex-spouses. Standard in Truestead trusts.
- Pretermitted child (F.S. § 732.301): a child born after a will is signed and not mentioned in it may claim an intestate share, which is why documents need updating after births.
- Digital assets are covered by the Florida Fiduciary Access to Digital Assets Act (F.S. §§ 740.001 to 740.0404).

ESTATE PLANNING — TRUSTS AND WILLS, PLAINLY
═══════════════════════════════════════════════
WHY A REVOCABLE TRUST IN FLORIDA:
- Avoids probate entirely on trust-titled assets. A funded trust moves assets to beneficiaries in weeks, privately, with no court.
- Revocable means the client keeps full control: change it, amend it, revoke it anytime while they have capacity, and keep using their money and property exactly as before.
- A trust only controls what it OWNS. "Funding" means retitling assets (deeds for real estate, ownership changes on accounts) into the trust's name. An unfunded trust avoids nothing.
- A revocable trust does NOT reduce income taxes, does NOT protect assets from the client's own creditors, and does NOT shelter assets from Medicaid. Those need different tools (irrevocable trusts, Medicaid planning), which are attorney-guided.

TRUST VS WILL:
- A will goes THROUGH probate; it is instructions to the probate judge. A funded trust goes AROUND probate.
- Even with a trust there is a pour-over will, a safety net that catches anything left outside the trust and pours it in. Anything it catches still goes through probate, which is why funding matters.
- A wills-only plan (the Essentials package) is legitimate for simpler estates: cheaper now, probate later. Trust plans cost more now and save the family time and money later. Real estate in more than one county or state tips strongly toward a trust.

THE DOCUMENTS AND WHAT EACH DOES:
- Revocable Living Trust: holds assets during life, passes them at death without probate, and lets a successor trustee step in during incapacity.
- Pour-Over Will: the safety net described above. Also nominates a guardian for minor children.
- Durable Power of Attorney: names an agent to handle finances and legal matters if the client is incapacitated. "Durable" means it stays effective through incapacity. Florida POAs are governed by F.S. Chapter 709.
- Healthcare Surrogate Designation: names who makes medical decisions when the client cannot. Governed by F.S. §§ 765.101 to 765.205.
- Living Will: written instructions about end-of-life care, life-prolonging procedures, artificial nutrition and hydration, and pain relief. F.S. § 765.301 and following.
- HIPAA Authorization: lets named people get medical information from providers. Without it, even a healthcare surrogate can hit walls.
- Certificate of Trust: a short document proving the trust exists so banks do not need the whole private document.
- Trust Funding Guide and Schedule A: the instructions and inventory for retitling assets into the trust.

THE ROLES, AND WHO TO NAME:
- Trustee: manages trust assets. Almost always the client during their lifetime.
- Successor Trustee: steps in on incapacity or death. Most commonly a spouse, adult child, or trusted family member. Name a backup. No license or legal training needed; they can hire professionals with trust funds.
- Personal Representative (executor): handles anything going through probate. Florida requires the PR to be a Florida resident OR related to the client by blood, marriage, or adoption. An out-of-state friend cannot serve.
- POA agent: acts financially while the client is ALIVE but incapacitated. Should be extremely trustworthy.
- Healthcare surrogate: should know the client's wishes and handle stress in a medical setting. Can be the same person as the POA agent, but they are different documents.
- Per stirpes: if a beneficiary dies first, their share passes down to their own children instead of being redistributed among the other beneficiaries. Almost always the right choice.
- Contingent beneficiaries: the backup plan if all primary beneficiaries have died. Always name them, or a court decides.

SPECIAL PROVISIONS THAT COME UP:
- Spendthrift clause: see above, standard.
- No-contest clause: discourages challenges to the trust.
- HEMS standard: limits a trustee to distributions for Health, Education, Maintenance and Support. More conservative, more protective.
- Special Needs Trust: if a beneficiary has a disability and receives SSI or Medicaid, a direct inheritance could disqualify them. A third-party special needs trust holds their share separately, supplements rather than replaces benefits, and has no Medicaid payback.
- Pet trust (F.S. § 736.0408): legally enforceable funds for animal care, with a named caretaker.
- S-corporation shares need special trust language to keep S-corp status (IRC § 1361).
- Retirement accounts (IRAs, 401(k)s) and life insurance are NOT retitled into the trust. Beneficiary designations are updated instead. Under the SECURE Act, most non-spouse beneficiaries must withdraw an inherited IRA within 10 years.
- Non-resident aliens: only a $60,000 federal estate tax exemption on US assets. A QDOT trust is needed to give the marital deduction to a non-citizen spouse. FIRPTA withholding applies when a non-US person sells US real estate.

UPDATING DOCUMENTS THEY ALREADY HAVE:
- Trust amendment: one to three targeted changes, attached to the original trust, which stays intact.
- Trust restatement: a complete rewrite into one clean new document, absorbing the original.
- Codicil: a change to a will.
- Review after marriage, divorce, births, deaths, big purchases or a move, or every 3 to 5 years.

AFTER SIGNING: fund the trust, store the originals safely, tell the successor trustee where they are.

LADY BIRD DEEDS (ENHANCED LIFE ESTATE)
═══════════════════════════════════════════════
- A Florida specialty. The owner keeps complete lifetime control (can sell, mortgage, or revoke without the beneficiaries' permission) and the property passes automatically at death with no probate. Homestead protection and the tax exemption are preserved. Medicaid-friendly. Must be recorded.
- Lady Bird deed vs living trust: for one property with a simple pass-at-death goal, the Lady Bird deed is faster and cheaper. A trust adds incapacity planning, staged distributions, protection for minor beneficiaries, and covers all assets. Many clients use both. Which fits is a question for Arthur.

Attorney tiers include preparation and recording by mail with the county clerk. ELDER LAW AND MEDICAID PLANNING
═══════════════════════════════════════════════
- Florida nursing home care can top $10,000 a month. Medicaid has a 60-month look-back on transfers for institutional (nursing home) Medicaid. Florida does not currently impose a look-back on community or home-based Medicaid.
- Two lanes: Elder-Law Essentials (the documents everyone should have for aging: a durable POA with Medicaid powers, healthcare surrogate, living will, HIPAA, pre-need guardian declaration, disposition of remains) and Medicaid Planning, which is always attorney-guided because it is fact-specific and a mistake can disqualify someone or create a penalty.
- CRISIS planning is for someone already in, or about to enter, a nursing home or rehab. ADVANCE planning is 5 or more years ahead of need.
- Key tools, in plain English:
  - Qualified Income Trust ("Miller trust"): required when gross monthly income is over the cap. Holds ONLY income, funded and paid out every month in a strict order, repays Medicaid at death. It does not shelter assets.
  - Medicaid Asset Protection Trust (MAPT): an irrevocable, income-only trust for ADVANCE planning. The grantor keeps the income but not the principal. Subject to the 60-month look-back.
  - Personal Services / Caregiver Agreement: pays a family caregiver at fair-market value so the money is compensation, not a gift. Needs time logs, is taxable income, and must be signed BEFORE the services begin.
  - Medicaid-compliant promissory note: a crisis tool used with a gift to shorten a penalty period.
  - Spousal refusal: the well spouse may decline to make resources available, so eligibility is judged on the applicant alone.
  - Lady Bird deed: passes the home at death outside probate, keeps control and homestead, Medicaid-friendly.
  - The durable POA's Medicaid powers are what let an agent do this planning if the principal loses capacity. Without them, the agent cannot.
- 2026 Florida Medicaid figures (always say "verify the current figures"): income cap $2,982 a month; asset limit $2,000 for a single applicant; community spouse resource allowance up to $162,660; minimum monthly maintenance needs allowance $2,644 to $4,067; personal needs allowance $160 a month.
- Veterans: VA Aid & Attendance is a separate benefit with its own 3-year look-back.
- Capacity is the clock. If a parent has dementia, the documents have to be signed while they still have capacity, which is why elder-law questions should be booked promptly.

PROBATE AND SUMMARY ADMINISTRATION
═══════════════════════════════════════════════
- Summary administration is Florida's fast-track probate (F.S. Chapter 735). It qualifies when non-exempt probate assets are $150,000 or less (the threshold doubled from $75,000 on July 1, 2026), OR when the death was more than 2 years ago at any estate size, because creditor claims are then barred. It is the standard fix for an old un-probated estate or a house still in a deceased person's name.
- No personal representative is appointed; the court orders distribution directly. Typical timeline 3 to 8 weeks uncontested. Protected homestead is generally exempt from creditors and does not count toward the $150,000.
- Court filing fees run about $235 to $405 depending on the county, plus recording and publication, billed at cost.
- Honesty rule: a very small estate with no real estate may qualify for Disposition of Personal Property Without Administration, a clerk filing that needs no attorney. Say so.
- Contested estates, unknown heirs, or estates over the threshold need formal administration, which Arthur quotes after a qualification review.

PERSONAL INJURY (general information only)
═══════════════════════════════════════════════
- Florida's statute of limitations for most negligence claims is now 2 years (F.S. § 95.11, changed by HB 837 in 2023). Acting early matters.
- Florida uses modified comparative negligence (F.S. § 768.81): a person found more than 50% at fault recovers nothing.
- Florida is a no-fault state for auto accidents: PIP coverage (F.S. § 627.736) pays first, up to its limits, regardless of fault.
- Injury cases at Truestead are on contingency: the client pays nothing unless there is a recovery. Arthur reviews every injury inquiry himself.
- Never estimate what a case is worth or how long it will take. Those depend entirely on facts Arthur has to review.

═══════════════════════════════════════════════
- Products: Florida Residential Lease, Florida Commercial NNN Lease, Landlord Notice Pack. Attorney lease tiers include the Notice Pack.
- Florida residential leases need no witnesses or notary. The lease includes the § 83.49 deposit disclosure, the radon disclosure, and the lead-paint disclosure for pre-1978 homes.
- Deposit deadlines: written notice of how the deposit is held within 30 days of receipt; after move-out, return it within 15 days OR send a certified-mail claim notice within 30 days. Miss it and the landlord forfeits the claim.
